# Audit Functions and Tamper-Evidence in Leading ERPs

## Which products offer cryptographic inalterability (like Odoo's hash check) vs plain change logs?

### Takeaway
Only Odoo has a native, documented cryptographic hash-chain mechanism (SHA-256, journal-scoped) built into the core product. NetSuite, SAP Business One, Business Central, Sage Intacct, and TallyPrime all rely on plain (non-cryptographic) change logs / system notes that are access-controlled and cannot be edited by users, but are not hash-chained. True cryptographic hash-chaining shows up at the regulatory/e-invoicing layer (e.g., Portugal's SAF-T billing software), not inside the general-ledger ERPs covered here except Odoo.

### Cited Findings
- **Odoo**: Uses SHA-256 to create a "unique fingerprint" (hash) for each posted journal entry. The hash is computed by concatenating the entry's `name`, `date`, `journal_id`, `company_id`, `debit`, `credit`, `account_id`, and `partner_id` fields and passing them through SHA-256. Critically, "the previous entry's hash is always added to the next entry to form a hash chain," so inserting, altering, or deleting an entry breaks the chain from that point forward. — [Odoo 18 Data inalterability check report docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/finance/accounting/reporting/data_inalterability.html)
- Activation is per-journal ("Secure Posted Entries with Hash" in Journal > Advanced Settings) or via a "Secure Entries Wizard" that can hash all entries up to a chosen date across all journals; once enabled on a journal and an entry is posted, the setting cannot be disabled and no posted entry can be edited. — [Odoo 17 docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/17.0/applications/finance/accounting/reporting/data_inalterability.html), [Odoo 18 docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/finance/accounting/reporting/data_inalterability.html)
- The Data Inalterability Check Report shows, per journal sequence prefix, whether hashed entries are present and verified, plus the first/last hashed entry and their hash values for a consistency check. — [Odoo 18 docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/finance/accounting/reporting/data_inalterability.html)
- Odoo's own docs frame this generically ("tax authorities in some countries require...") without naming France explicitly on that page, though the feature is widely described in the ecosystem as built for France's anti-fraud law. — [Odoo 18 docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/finance/accounting/reporting/data_inalterability.html)
- Odoo also ships a separate, non-cryptographic **"Audit Trail"** feature (native since v17, expanded in 18/19) that logs create/read/update/delete on tracked models. It writes to protected audit tables with elevated rights so ordinary users cannot view or alter them; configuration and log viewing are restricted to an "Audit Trail Manager" security group, and enabling it on a model blocks deletion of records that have audit entries. This is a plain access-controlled log, not hash-chained. — [Cybrosys: Overview of Audit Trail in Odoo 18](https://www.cybrosys.com/blog/overview-of-audit-trail-in-odoo-18-accounting), [Odoo Apps: Audit Trail module](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/inom_audit_trail)
- **NetSuite**: System notes capture date, user, role, change type, and old/new values for a record, and "can't be edited by any system user, script, or application" — i.e., tamper-resistant by access control, not cryptography. — [NetSuite: System Notes Overview](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/chapter_158644279544.html)
- **SAP Business One**: Ships with Change Log enabled by default, tracking up to 99 historical versions per document/master/screen (configurable count); a separate "Audit Trail Report" (a standard report combining change-log data) was not available as a standard report until SAP Business One 10.0 (build 2408 or later) — in 9.3 users had to build custom queries. Neither mechanism is described as cryptographic. — [SAP Help: Change Log Window](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_BUSINESS_ONE/68a2e87fb29941b5bf959a184d9c6727/9f301385d30f4c32924590548c85f164.html), [SAP Community: Audit Trail report in SAP B1 9.3](https://community.sap.com/t5/enterprise-resource-planning-q-a/audit-trail-report-in-sap-b1-9-3/qaq-p/12774530), [SAP Help: Audit Trail Report](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_BUSINESS_ONE/68a2e87fb29941b5bf959a184d9c6727/9386164025bb416daafb858fd6ec1c5a.html)
- **Business Central**: The Change Log tracks modifications at the table/field level you configure, timestamped and attributable to a user, viewable/analyzable on the Change Log Entries page; it is a plain audit log with configurable retention policies, no hashing. — [Microsoft Learn: Auditing changes - Business Central](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-au/dynamics365/business-central/across-log-changes)
- **Sage Intacct**: Audit Trail records who changed what and when for transactions (invoices, bill payments, journal entries, dimension changes, period open/close, config changes), viewable per-record via "View audit trail" or via reports; described as a logging/reporting feature, not a cryptographic scheme. A separate "Advanced Audit Trail" add-on exists for deeper drill-down but is still log-based. — [Sage Intacct: Audit Trail](https://www.intacct.com/ia/docs/en_US/help_action/Administration/Audit_trail/Standard/audit-trail.htm), [Sage Intacct: Audit Trail vs Advanced Audit Trail](https://www.intacct.com/ia/docs/en_US/help_action/Administration/Audit_trail/audit-trail-vs-advanced-audit-trail.htm)
- **TallyPrime**: The "Edit Log" (added to meet India's mandate) records creation/alteration/deletion of every voucher and master with user, timestamp, and field-wise before/after values; enabled via F11 > "Use Edit Log." No cryptographic hashing is described in vendor/partner documentation found. — [Sanghvi Enterprise: The TallyPrime Edit Log Feature for Audit Trail](https://sanghvienterprise.in/the-tallyprime-edit-log-feature-for-audit-trail-how-it-works/), [markitsolutions.in: TallyPrime Edit Log guide](https://www.markitsolutions.in/blog-details/tallyprime-edit-log-guide)
- **Portugal's SAF-T regime** (outside the 6 ERPs, but the clearest cryptographic model): certified billing software must implement a cryptographic hash chain across issued invoices — each invoice's digital signature incorporates the hash of the previous invoice's signature (RSA-based), so retroactively altering, deleting, or inserting an invoice breaks the chain from that point forward and is immediately detectable; invoices also carry an ATCUD number and QR code, and monthly SAF-T (PT) XML must be submitted by the 5th of the following month. — [saft-validator.com: Portugal's SAF-T](https://saft-validator.com/blog/portugal-saft-earliest-adopter), [fiskaly: Portugal fiscal API](https://www.fiskaly.com/signpt)

### Inferences
- Odoo is unusual among mainstream ERPs in shipping hash-chain inalterability as a core, self-service accounting feature rather than requiring a country-specific certified add-on; this appears tied to French/EU anti-fraud law compliance (see France section below), even though Odoo's own product docs stop short of naming the jurisdiction on the inalterability page itself.
- The other five products treat "tamper evidence" as "append-only, access-controlled, non-editable log," which is the model India's Rule 3(1) and the ICAI Guide also assume (an "edit log" that "cannot be disabled") — cryptographic proof is not what Indian regulation demands, unlike France (FEC/NF525) and Portugal (SAF-T hash chain).

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether SAP Business One 10.0's new "Audit Trail Report" (2408+) includes any hashing/checksum element — only that it consolidates the change log into a standard report; no cryptographic detail found in the sources retrieved.
- Could not find Business Central documentation of any cryptographic/hash feature at all (its SAF-T/FEC export is a report format, not an inalterability proof — see export section below).

## Which let an auditor mark vouchers/entries as audited (Tally Audit)? How does it work?

### Takeaway
Of the six products, TallyPrime/Tally.ERP 9 is the only one with an explicit, named "Tally Audit" workflow letting a reviewer mark individual vouchers as audited and flag any post-audit alteration; the others rely on generic "auditor" read-only roles rather than a per-transaction audited/reviewed marking mechanism, with Sage Intacct's period-close and NetSuite's period-lock acting as coarser-grained "this period is done" signals instead of per-voucher sign-off.

### Cited Findings
- **Tally Audit** (originally a Tally.ERP 9 "Auditors' Edition" feature, carried into TallyPrime): lets an auditor select vouchers with Spacebar (multi-select with Spacebar repeatedly, or Ctrl+Spacebar to select all) and mark them as audited; it tracks changes affecting the integrity of a transaction — date, ledger masters, and amounts — made to a voucher *after* it was marked audited, flagging tampering post-review. — [Web search summary of TallyHelp: Tally Audit](https://help.tallysolutions.com/article/Tally.ERP9/Data_Management/DM_Tally_Audit.htm) (page itself only confirmed the Edit Log banner on fetch; the marking mechanics are per aggregator summaries — see Gaps)
- A companion report, "Verification of Vouchers," shows voucher counts by type for the audit year, including total vouchers vs. audited vouchers and the sampling method used — indicating Tally supports a sampling-based audit workflow rather than requiring 100% voucher review. — [TallyHelp: Verification of Vouchers (India)](https://help.tallysolutions.com/article/Tally.ERP9/Auditor_Edition/India/Statutory_Audit/Verification_of_Vouchers_stat_audit.htm)
- As part of the process, auditors record comments, tasks performed, and client inputs supporting audit conclusions, alongside verification notes for vouchers and ledgers — i.e., a lightweight workpaper trail lives inside the product. — [TallyHelp: Auditing Process (India)](https://help.tallysolutions.com/article/Tally.ERP9/Auditor_Edition/India/Statutory_Audit/auditing_process.htm)
- **NetSuite**: no per-transaction "mark as audited" flag found; instead, period locking (Lock A/P, Lock A/R, Lock Payroll, final "Lock All GL accounts") stops further posting, and any subsequent lock override requires the "Override Period Restrictions" permission, which itself is auditable via system notes. — [NetSuite: Using the Period Close Checklist](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N1455781.html), [NetSuite: Locking and Unlocking Accounting Periods](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N1451780.html)
- **Sage Intacct**: no per-record "audited" flag found; the closest analog is period open/close status plus an "Auditor" read-only role for external review (see role section) and the Audit Trail log showing who touched what — reviewers rely on filtering the log rather than flagging individual entries. — [Sage Intacct: Audit Trail](https://www.intacct.com/ia/docs/en_US/help_action/Administration/Audit_trail/Standard/audit-trail.htm)
- **SAP Business One, Business Central, Odoo**: none of the sources retrieved describe a native "mark this entry as audited/reviewed" workflow comparable to Tally Audit; Business Central and Odoo instead expose the change log / audit trail for after-the-fact review, and period-locking is the primary "this is final" mechanism (see period-locking section).

### Inferences
- Tally Audit's design (sampling + per-voucher mark + post-mark tamper detection) reflects its origin serving Indian statutory/tax auditors doing sample-based vouching, a workflow other ERPs handle instead via broader period locks and role-based read access rather than a granular per-voucher checkbox.

### Gaps
- Direct fetch of the primary TallyHelp "Tally Audit" page returned only a navigation banner, not the substantive workflow text; the mechanics above are drawn from a search-engine synthesis of that page's cached content and corroborating partner articles (Sanghvi Enterprise, Scribd upload of the Tally ERP9 Auditors' Edition manual), not a direct primary-source read — flagged as lower-confidence pending direct access to help.tallysolutions.com content.
- Could not determine whether TallyPrime's newer Edit Log (post-2023, MCA-driven) still exposes the same audited/verification workflow as the older Tally.ERP 9 Auditors' Edition, or whether "Tally Audit" is now a separate, still-current feature alongside Edit Log.

## What standard audit data file formats (SAF-T, FEC) exist and what they contain?

### Takeaway
FEC (France) and SAF-T (an OECD-originated standard adopted with local variants by Portugal and others) are the two standardized audit-export formats found across these products; Business Central natively supports generating both (plus Sweden's SIE) via a dedicated "Audit File Export" extension, while other vendors handle them via localization packs or third-party add-ons rather than as a universal core feature.

### Cited Findings
- **FEC (Fichier des Écritures Comptables)**: a mandatory French standardized export of all accounting entries, required in a specific format with 18 mandatory columns, generated directly from the accounting software, timestamped, and presented in chronological order; any retroactive modification or reconstitution of the file is strictly prohibited. — [Sage Advice France: FEC definition](https://www.sage.com/fr-fr/blog/glossaire/fichier-des-ecritures-comptables-definition-du-fec-dentreprise/), [Legalstart: Le FEC mode d'emploi](https://www.legalstart.fr/fiches-pratiques/comptabilite-entreprise/fichier-ecritures-comptables-fec/)
- Sage Intacct and Microsoft Dynamics 365 Finance both document dedicated FEC export features for French entities. — [Sage Intacct: Fichier des écritures comptables (FEC)](https://www.intacct.com/ia/docs/fr_FR/help_action/General_Ledger/General_Ledger_reports/Accounting_entries_file/about-accounting-entries-file.htm), [Microsoft Learn: Audit file for France (FEC) — Dynamics 365 Finance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/finance/localizations/france/emea-fra-fec-audit-file)
- **Business Central**: ships a preinstalled "Audit Files Export" extension letting a company export entries as SIE (Sweden), FEC (France), or generic SAF-T format, to satisfy auditor or tax-authority requirements — this was rolled out as part of the 2023 wave 1 / subsequent release plans. — [Microsoft Learn: Export data for auditing - Business Central](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/finance-how-to-export-audit-files), [Microsoft Learn 2023 Wave 1: audit file export](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-gb/dynamics365/release-plan/2023wave1/smb/dynamics365-business-central/audit-file-export)
- **Portugal SAF-T (PT)**: certified billing software must submit a monthly SAF-T (PT) XML file (by the 5th of the following month) containing invoice data tied to sequential, hash-chained digital signatures, an ATCUD unique document code, and QR code — making Portugal's SAF-T both a data-export standard and (via the underlying software certification) a tamper-evidence regime. — [saft-validator.com: Portugal's SAF-T](https://saft-validator.com/blog/portugal-saft-earliest-adopter), [fiskaly: Portugal fiscal API](https://www.fiskaly.com/signpt)
- **NF525** (France): a certification standard (an AFNOR/Infocerf trademark, not a legal monopoly — other COFRAC-accredited bodies such as LNE can certify, and vendors can self-certify under Article 88 of Law No. 2015-1785) applied to cash-register/POS software to prove inalterability, security, conservation, and archiving of sales data — distinct from, but related to, the FEC requirement for general ledgers. — [IG Conseils: FEC et conformité des logiciels et systèmes de caisse](https://blog.ig-conseils.com/fec-conformite-logiciels-systemes-caisse/)

### Inferences
- FEC and SAF-T are format/export obligations layered on top of whatever tamper-evidence mechanism (hash chain, change log) the underlying software uses; a vendor can produce a compliant FEC/SAF-T export without necessarily having cryptographic inalterability (e.g., Business Central's Audit File Export is a report generator, not itself a hash-chain).

### Gaps
- Did not find confirmation of native SAF-T or FEC export capability inside NetSuite, SAP Business One, TallyPrime, or Sage Intacct's non-France localizations (Sage Intacct's FEC support is documented in fr_FR/fr_CA help, suggesting it's localization-specific rather than global). NetSuite and SAP Business One likely rely on third-party/local-partner SuiteApps or add-ons for SAF-T/FEC, but no primary source was retrieved confirming or denying this — flagged as a gap for the report writer to note as "not found" rather than "absent."
- No detail retrieved on the full column/data specification of a generic (OECD/EU) SAF-T file versus Portugal's specific variant; only Portugal's invoice-hash aspects were confirmed.

## How do they handle post-close edits?

### Takeaway
All six products default to blocking direct posting into a locked/closed period, but every one of them also provides a permissioned override path (a specific permission or unlocking step) that itself gets logged — meaning "post-close edit" is technically possible everywhere, and the control that matters to auditors is who holds the override permission and whether the override event is itself audit-logged.

### Cited Findings
- **NetSuite**: Locking a period (via the period-close checklist: Lock A/P, Lock A/R, Lock Payroll, final "Lock All GL accounts") prevents posting of transactions that affect the general ledger for that period; a user needs the specific "Override Period Restrictions" permission to add GL-impacting changes to a locked period, and NetSuite "maintains an audit trail of every changed task in every closed accounting period through system notes." Auditors are described as expecting "closed periods to be locked," with rare, documented post-close adjustments as the acceptable pattern. — [NetSuite: Accounting Period Management](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/chapter_N1445226.html), [NetSuite: Locking and Unlocking Accounting Periods](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N1451780.html)
- **SAP Business One**: An administrator sets a posting period's "Period Status" to Locked (Administration > System Initialization > Posting Periods); once locked, no updates can be submitted against that period until it is explicitly unlocked. The lock/unlock action itself is written to a change log accessible via Tools > Change Log on the Posting Period window, capturing date and user. — [Cornerstone: How to Lock Posting Periods in SAP Business One](https://ask.cornerstonesap.com/how-to-lock-posting-periods-in-sap-business-one/)
- **Business Central**: Fiscally closing a period ("Close Fiscal Period" action) is meant to ensure no more G/L entries can post to it — but the docs explicitly note closing a *fiscal year* does not itself block posting: "although a fiscal year is closed, you can still post general ledger entries to it. When you do, the entries are marked as posted to a closed fiscal year and the Prior-Year Entry field is selected" — i.e., BC flags rather than hard-blocks certain post-close postings, relying on the flag plus change log/G/L register review for control. — [Microsoft Learn: Close accounting periods for a fiscal year](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/year-close-account-periods), [Microsoft Learn: Optional activities for closing periods](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/year-how-complete-period-end-processes)
- **Odoo**: distinguishes a soft "Lock Date" (which can be moved by users with sufficient rights) from the "Hard Lock Date," which "cannot be unlocked" once set, "regardless of access rights" — the only one of the six mechanisms found that is described as literally irreversible by any user, matching France's inalterability expectations. Separately, per-journal hash-locking (see above) blocks edits to any already-hashed posted entry outright, with no override path described. — [Odoo forum: Odoo 18 Lock Dates FAQ](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/odoo-18-lock-dates-frequently-asked-questions-263564), [Odoo 18 docs: Data inalterability check report](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/finance/accounting/reporting/data_inalterability.html)
- **Sage Intacct**: period open/close actions are themselves logged in the audit trail ("period open/close actions... leave a similar footprint" to journal entries); the audit trail captures journal entry creation, modification, approval, and deletion with timestamp and user ID, but the sources retrieved note a limitation — the audit trail records *who posted what and when* but not always the source document or full approval chain, which can leave gaps for year-end fieldwork. — [Truewind: How to Give Your Auditor Everything They Need From Sage Intacct](https://www.truewind.ai/blog/give-auditor-everything-sage-intacct-without-fire-drill)
- **TallyPrime**: the Edit Log records every alteration or deletion of a transaction/master with user, timestamp, and before/after field values regardless of when it occurs, so a post-close edit is not blocked by Edit Log itself but is fully logged; period locking is a separate, less emphasized control in the sources found (see Gaps). — [Sanghvi Enterprise: The TallyPrime Edit Log Feature for Audit Trail](https://sanghvienterprise.in/the-tallyprime-edit-log-feature-for-audit-trail-how-it-works/)
- **India (Rule 3(1) proviso)**: mandates that every company using accounting software use software with a feature "recording audit trail of each and every transaction, creating an edit log of each change made in books of account along with the date when such changes were made and ensuring that the audit trail cannot be disabled" — the rule targets *logging every change* (including post-close ones) rather than *preventing* post-close edits; retention of the audit trail is required for a minimum of eight years, aligned to the Section 128(5) books-of-account retention period, effective for financial years commencing on or after 1 April 2023 (the rule was originally inserted 24 March 2021 but its applicability was deferred twice, finally taking effect 1 April 2023). — [ca2013.com: Rule 3, Companies (Accounts) Rules 2014](https://ca2013.com/rule-3-companies-accounts-rules2014/), [India Briefing: India Mandates Audit Trail Compliance](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-mandates-audit-trail-compliance-for-all-companies-explainer-key-obligations-34837.html/), [UJA: Audit Trail and Back-Up Verification](https://uja.in/blog/regulatory-updates/audit-trail-and-back-up-verification/)
- ICAI's Implementation Guide (Rule 11(g)) requires the **statutory auditor**, not just the company, to verify: (a) whether the audit trail feature is configurable/can be disabled or tampered with, (b) whether all recorded transactions are covered by the audit trail, (c) whether the audit trail has been preserved per statutory retention requirements, and (d) whether the feature was enabled/operating throughout the year — explicitly making "was the audit trail ever turned off, even temporarily" a reportable audit finding. — [FinTax Blog: ICAI's Implementation Guide on Rule 11(g)](https://fintaxblog.com/icais-implementation-guide-on-rule-11g-reporting-on-audit-trail/), [Taxscan: ICAI Issues Implementation Guide on Reporting of Audit Trail](https://www.taxscan.in/icai-issues-implementation-guide-on-reporting-of-audit-trail/265954)
- Rule 3 also requires that backups of electronic books of account (including any kept outside India) be stored on servers physically located in India, on a daily basis — a related but distinct data-residency control bundled into the same rule. — [UJA: Audit Trail and Back-Up Verification](https://uja.in/blog/regulatory-updates/audit-trail-and-back-up-verification/)
- **France (FEC/NF525)**: the inalterability principle is stated as an absolute prohibition — "toute modification ou reconstitution a posteriori est strictement interdite" (any modification or after-the-fact reconstitution is strictly forbidden) — putting France's model closer to Odoo's hard-lock/hash-chain design than to the "log everything, including late edits" model of India's Rule 3(1). — [Clémentine: Le fichier des écritures comptables, le guide complet](https://www.clementine.fr/blog/comptabilite-social-et-juridique/fichier-des-ecritures-comptables/)

### Inferences
- There are two structurally different regulatory philosophies represented here: India's Rule 3(1) is a *detection* model (every change, including post-close, must be logged and cannot be turned off, but is not necessarily prevented), whereas France (FEC/NF525) and Portugal (SAF-T hash chain) are *prevention* models (the entry becomes cryptographically or procedurally unchangeable once posted/hard-locked). Odoo is the only product among the six that ships a native mechanism matching the prevention model (hash chain + irreversible hard lock date); the other five (NetSuite, SAP B1, Business Central, Sage Intacct, TallyPrime) fit the detection model — permissioned override + mandatory logging of the override.
- Business Central's explicit behavior of *allowing* posting into a closed fiscal year (merely flagging it as a "Prior-Year Entry") is a notable outlier that a report writer may want to call out as weaker default period control compared to NetSuite's or SAP B1's default hard-block-until-unlocked behavior.

### Gaps
- Could not find a primary Tally source describing whether TallyPrime has a period-lock/closing-period concept equivalent to NetSuite/SAP B1/Business Central (as opposed to relying solely on the Edit Log for detection); this should be treated as unconfirmed rather than "Tally has no period lock."
- Could not confirm from primary NetSuite/SAP B1/Business Central sources whether an unlock/override action is retained for the same statutory period as the underlying financial records (e.g., 7/8 years) — only that such actions are logged.

## Auditor access roles (cross-product summary)

### Takeaway
Every product in scope provides some form of dedicated, typically read-only "auditor" access path, but the maturity ranges from a formally named built-in role (NetSuite) to a documented pattern for building a custom read-only role (Sage Intacct, SAP Business One) to relying on general permission-set granularity (Business Central).

### Cited Findings
- **NetSuite**: By default, the Transaction Audit Trail report is available to CEO, CEO (Hands Off), CFO, and Support Administrator roles; other/custom roles need the audit trail permission explicitly assigned. Viewing a record's System Notes only requires view access to the record plus the "List > Notes Tab" permission; deeper system-note searching is available via Reports > New Search. — [NetSuite: Using the Transaction Audit Trail](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N556825.html), [NetSuite: System Notes Overview](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/chapter_158644279544.html)
- **SAP Business One**: Authorizations are configured per user or user-group under Administration > System Initialization > Authorizations > General Authorizations, with three levels (full, display-only, none); a company auditor without a defined ownership relationship to the business can be granted document access individually — SAP's own documentation cites "the company auditor" as the canonical example of when to use this individual-grant mechanism, rather than there being a pre-built "Auditor" role template. — [SAP: Utilizing Data Ownership Authorizations](https://learning.sap.com/courses/implementing-sap-business-one/utilizing-data-ownership-authorizations), [SEIDOR: How to define user authorizations in SAP Business One](https://www.seidor.com/en-us/blog/how-define-user-authorizations-sap-business-one)
- **Business Central**: No named "Auditor" permission set was confirmed in the sources retrieved; a 2026 Wave 1 release note describes new *read-only APIs* on the permission system "allowing for establishing security and auditing analytics and reporting outside Business Central," explicitly gated so the underlying permission-API permission set "shouldn't be granted to standard users" — suggesting Microsoft is building toward more formal external-auditor tooling but hadn't (as of the sources found) shipped a named Auditor role. — [Microsoft Learn 2026 Wave 1: Use new APIs for analyzing permissions for auditors and IT staff](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/release-plan/2026wave1/smb/dynamics365-business-central/use-new-apis-analyzing-permissions-auditors-it-staff)
- **Odoo**: the Audit Trail feature's configuration and log viewing are restricted to a dedicated "Audit Trail Manager" security group, separate from the users whose actions are being tracked (who write to the log with elevated/system rights and cannot see or alter it). — [Cybrosys: Overview of Audit Trail in Odoo 18 Accounting](https://www.cybrosys.com/blog/overview-of-audit-trail-in-odoo-18-accounting)
- **Sage Intacct**: a documented pattern exists for "Set up an auditor as a user," creating a user profile with a purpose-built read-only "Auditor" role that can be scoped to whichever books/reports the external auditor needs — Intacct explicitly frames this as necessary because the company "often doesn't know exactly what [the auditor] needs to validate your accounts," so the role tends to be broad. — [Sage Intacct: Set up an auditor as a user](https://www.intacct.com/ia/docs/en_US/help_action/Administration/Users/audit-users-in-Intacct.htm)
- **TallyPrime/Tally.ERP 9**: historically sold a distinct SKU, the "Auditors' Edition," giving chartered accountants the Tally Audit / Verification of Vouchers / Auditing Process feature set described above — i.e., auditor access in Tally is bound to a separate product edition rather than a role toggle inside the standard product. — [Sunit Solutions: Tally.ERP 9 – Auditors' Edition](https://sunitsolutions.in/products/tally-auditors-edition/), [Mark IT: Tally Auditor's Edition for Chartered Accountants](https://www.markitsolutions.in/blog-details/tally-erp-software-auditors-edition-a-troubleshooter-for-the-chartered-accountants)

### Inferences
- There's a spectrum from "auditor is a first-class, named role with default access to audit reports" (NetSuite) to "auditor access is a documented configuration pattern the customer must build" (SAP B1, Sage Intacct) to "auditor access requires an entirely separate product SKU" (Tally). Business Central appears the least mature on this specific dimension based on available sources, though it compensates with strong change-log/field-monitoring tooling and native SAF-T/FEC export.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether TallyPrime (as opposed to the older Tally.ERP 9 Auditors' Edition) still requires a separate SKU for auditor features, or has folded Tally Audit/Verification of Vouchers into the standard product alongside the new Edit Log — the sources found were mixed in vintage (Tally.ERP 9-era help pages vs. TallyPrime-era Edit Log marketing pages) and did not directly reconcile this.
