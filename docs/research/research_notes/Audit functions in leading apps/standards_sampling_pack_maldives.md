# Audit Sampling (ISA 530/AICPA), JE Testing (ISA 240), PBC Packs, and Maldives Audit Context

## How should a kept sample be documented to satisfy ISA 530 and be reproducible?

### Takeaway
ISA 530 requires the auditor to document the population, the sampling unit, the objective of the test, the method used to determine sample size, the selection method, and the items actually selected, so that the sample design and results can be traced back and re-evaluated; the AICPA Audit Sampling guide adds that PPS/MUS documentation must additionally capture the test objective, the definition of an exception/misstatement, the confidence level, and the results of testing. Neither statistical nor non-statistical sampling is inherently superior, but statistical sampling (true random selection with a mathematically determined size) is more objectively defensible, while non-statistical/haphazard selection depends entirely on the quality of the auditor's documented judgment to be defensible.

### Cited Findings
- ISA 530 applies only when every item in the population has a known, non-zero chance of selection, so results can be projected to the population; it does not apply to 100% testing or to picking specific (non-representative) items — those are separate procedures. — [ciferi ISA 530 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-530-audit-sampling-guide)
- Statistical sampling uses random, mathematically-determined selection and size, and quantifies sampling risk and projects misstatement via confidence intervals; non-statistical sampling uses judgment-based selection (haphazard or systematic) and judgment-based sizing/projection, but "should consider the same factors" as statistical sampling and is only as defensible as its documentation of judgment. — [ciferi ISA 530 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-530-audit-sampling-guide)
- Selection methods identified in ISA 530.A13: (1) **Random selection**, applying via random number generators/tables, required for statistical sampling; (2) **Systematic (interval) selection**, computed as population size ÷ sample size with a random starting point, then selecting every nth item — flagged as risky if the population has periodicity that coincides with the interval (e.g., every 10th invoice being a month-end adjustment when the interval is 10); (3) **Monetary Unit Sampling (MUS/PPS)**, which gives each individual currency unit an equal chance of selection so higher-value items are proportionally more likely to be picked, is described as "the default on most substantive testing... across mid-tier firms," and works well with small sample sizes because it automatically concentrates on large balances; (4) **Haphazard selection**, permitted only for non-statistical sampling, requires the auditor to avoid bias (not just picking easily accessible items, items at the top/bottom of a page, or items from a single time period); (5) **Block selection** (a contiguous block, e.g. all transactions from one week) is generally not an appropriate sampling method on its own. — [ciferi ISA 530 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-530-audit-sampling-guide)
- A widely used MUS/PPS sample-size formula is n = RF × BV ÷ TM, where RF is a reliability factor from statistical tables (e.g., ~3.0 at 95% confidence with 0 expected errors), BV is the book value of the population, and TM is tolerable misstatement. — [WebSearch summary of ISA 530 sources](https://ciferi.com/blog/monetary-unit-sampling-in-practice-isa-530-worked-example-with-the-calculator)
- Key terms the standard defines and that documentation should reflect: **sampling risk** (the risk the sample-based conclusion differs from testing the whole population); **tolerable rate of deviation**; **anomalous misstatement** (demonstrably non-representative — excluded from projection, but its effect is still separately considered and documented); and **stratification** (dividing a population into sub-populations with similar characteristics to reduce variability and focus testing on higher-risk strata). — [ciferi ISA 530 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-530-audit-sampling-guide)
- Selecting specific (key) items by value — the highest-value items, items above a set threshold, or items with a specific risk characteristic (unusual, suspicious, particularly sensitive) — is a distinct approach from sampling under ISA 530 and is commonly combined with a separate random/systematic/MUS sample of the remaining (non-key) population so that 100% coverage of value plus a representative check of the "tail" is achieved. — [WebSearch summary](https://leaccountant.com/isa-530-audit-sampling-summary/)
- Regulatory inspection findings across jurisdictions that ISA 530 documentation commonly fails on: insufficient sample sizes for the assessed risk (Netherlands/AFM), failure to project misstatements to the full population (Netherlands and UK/FRC), inadequate documentation of the *rationale* for sample design, and use of default sample sizes without adjusting for the auditor's specific risk assessment (UK/FRC). France's NEP 530 explicitly requires the auditor to document methodology, sample design, selection, and evaluation in the working-paper file (dossier de travail). — [ciferi ISA 530 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-530-audit-sampling-guide)
- The AICPA's Audit Sampling Guide (updated through recent editions, most recently referenced as of Dec 2019) covers nonstatistical and statistical sampling for both tests of controls and substantive tests of details, with dedicated chapters/case studies on monetary unit sampling and classical variables sampling, plus appendices with attribute- and MUS-sampling tables. — [AICPA & CIMA Audit Sampling Guide](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/cpe-learning/publication/audit-sampling-audit-guide-OPL); [DART/Deloitte summary](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/auditing/aicpa/audit-accounting-guides-audit-risk-alerts/audit-sampling)
- For PPS/MUS documentation specifically, the AICPA guide's stated documentation requirements are: the test objective, the definition of an exception, a description of the population tested and the sampling unit, the confidence level, the significance of the attribute (deviation/misstatement condition), the sample size, and the results of testing. — [WebSearch summary of AICPA Audit Sampling Guide](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/cpe-learning/publication/audit-sampling-audit-guide-OPL)

### Inferences
- For an application to make a kept sample "reproducible" in the ISA 530/AICPA sense, it should persist, per sample: (a) the population definition (source table/report and as-of date/filter), (b) the sampling unit definition, (c) the test objective (control test vs. substantive test, and the specific assertion), (d) the method (statistical/non-statistical, and which selection technique — random/systematic/MUS/haphazard/key-item), (e) the parameters used to compute size (confidence level or reliability factor, tolerable misstatement/deviation rate, expected misstatement/deviation, and for MUS the book value used), (f) a deterministic **seed** (or the RNG parameters) so a random or systematic selection can be regenerated exactly, (g) the resulting size, (h) the list of items actually selected (with their sampling-interval position for MUS, so overlap with subsequent random selections within an interval can be explained), and (i) results/exceptions found and whether any were treated as anomalous (with rationale). Key-item selections should be tagged separately from the "random sample of the remainder" so the two components of coverage are auditable independently.
- Because a "default sample size" and "same criteria/entries as last year" are both explicitly called out as inspection failure patterns, the app should force the preparer to record a risk-based rationale (not just a number) each period, and should discourage silently re-using a prior period's parameters without an explicit review step.

## What fields must a GL export have for JE testing (ISA 240 management override)?

### Takeaway
ISA 240.32–34 requires the auditor to test the appropriateness of journal entries and other adjustments, with mandatory focus on entries made at the end of the reporting period and on entries posted directly to the financial statements (e.g., consolidation/top-side adjustments); practitioner guidance converges on needing GL exports with posting date/time, posting user, effective/transaction date, source/module, account, description, and debit/credit amounts so that timing-, user-, and pattern-based red flags (late postings, unusual users, round amounts, weekend/post-close postings, unusual account combinations) can be screened.

### Cited Findings
- ISA 240 requires the auditor to "make inquiries about inappropriate or unusual activity relating to processing of journal entries," to "select journal entries made at the end of the reporting period," and to "consider the need to test journal entries throughout the period" (not just at year-end) — because unpredictable management override is treated as a fraud risk present in all entities and, by default, a significant risk. — [WebSearch summary of ICAEW/IAASB](https://www.icaew.com/technical/audit-and-assurance/faculty-resources/implementing--isas-international/management-override); [IFAC ISA 240 Handbook](https://www.ifac.org/_flysystem/azure-private/publications/files/A012%202013%20IAASB%20Handbook%20ISA%20240.pdf)
- ISA 240.32–34: selecting journal entries and other adjustments made at the end of the reporting period and testing their appropriateness is mandatory; testing must include entries posted directly to the financial statements (e.g., consolidation adjustments), not only entries that flow through the subledgers. — [ciferi ISA 240 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-240-fraud-guide)
- Red-flag characteristics practitioners select on: entries posted outside normal business hours, entries made by senior management (users who do not normally post journals), entries to unusual or seldom-used account combinations, round-dollar/round-amount entries (especially near or above performance materiality), missing or generic descriptions, top-side adjustments that bypass the subledgers, and entries missing a second-person/maker-checker approval. Selection criteria should focus on these fraud characteristics, not merely on transaction size. — [ciferi ISA 240 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-240-fraud-guide); [WebSearch summary](https://www.macroslm.com/blog/what-is-journal-entry-testing)
- A caution repeated in the ciferi worked example: repeating the same population, the same selection criteria, and the same number of entries tested year after year — and reaching "the same conclusion" — is flagged as a sign of insufficient testing; narrowly focusing on manual entries risks missing high-risk *automated* postings that also warrant testing. — [ciferi ISA 240 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-240-fraud-guide)
- A generic GL/journal-entry data API (the AICPA-affiliated "Audit Data Standards" reference implementation) models journal entries with: an effective date range (start/end, referring to the entry's *effective* date), an entity identifier (for multi-entity systems), and four separate amount fields per line — Functional Amount, Reporting/Presentation Amount, Local Amount, and Transaction Amount (to support multi-currency reporting) — alongside a linked chart-of-accounts endpoint (account identifiers, independent of balances) and a separate trial-balance endpoint keyed by period-end date. — [AICPA-ADS AuditData-API GL Primer (GitHub)](https://github.com/AICPA-ADS/AuditData-API/blob/master/GL-Primer.md)

### Inferences
- Combining the ISA 240 selection criteria with the AICPA Audit Data Standards journal-entry model, a GL export fit for JE testing needs, at minimum, one row per journal *line* carrying: entry/batch number, **posting date** (when recorded in the system) *and* **effective/transaction date** (the period it affects) as two distinct fields (to detect late postings and post-close entries, which by definition have posting date after period-end but effective date within it), the **posting user/preparer** (and, where relevant, an approver/second-signer field, to detect missing segregation of duties), **source/module** (manual journal vs. subledger-generated vs. system-automated, to distinguish top-side entries), **description/memo** (to flag missing or generic text), **account(s) debited and credited**, and **debit/credit amount** (to flag round numbers and materiality thresholds). A timestamp (not just a date) is needed to detect "outside normal business hours"/weekend postings. This is a synthesis of ISA 240's stated selection criteria and the AICPA GL data model, not a single directly-cited field list from ISA 240 itself.
- Because ISA 240 explicitly warns against a static, repeated selection approach, an app's JE-testing feature should let the auditor vary/parameterize selection criteria each period (not hardcode "same top N round entries") and should be able to include entries generated by automated interfaces, not just manually keyed entries.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the literal ISA 240 paragraph text (32–34) from the primary IAASB PDF within the tool budget; the paragraph range and content above are as reported by the secondary source (ciferi.com), not independently verified against IAASB wording. A report writer needing the verbatim standard should pull it from the IFAC handbook link above.

## What is in a standard year-end PBC list?

### Takeaway
A standard year-end PBC (prepared-by-client) request list for a small/medium company spans: the trial balance and GL detail plus draft financial statements; AR and AP aging schedules with related reserve/accrual analyses; a fixed asset register showing additions, disposals, and depreciation; inventory listings/valuations and physical count results; bank reconciliations, statements, and confirmations; debt/loan schedules and confirmations; payroll and tax filings; and governance items (minutes, attorney letters, related-party and subsequent-event disclosures) — nearly all delivered as Excel/Google Sheets workbooks for schedules plus PDF for signed/scanned documents (contracts, confirmations, minutes).

### Cited Findings
- A PBC request list is described as "the itemized set of financial statements, reconciliations, contracts, confirmations, and supporting schedules the audit team needs before and during fieldwork." — [Zendoc PBC workflow article](https://zendoc.ai/articles/audit-pbc-request-list-workflow/)
- Typical financial-record items: year-end adjusted trial balance; general ledger detail for all accounts; journal entry listing (including adjusting and closing entries); and draft financial statements (balance sheet, income statement, cash flow, equity). — [FileRequestPro PBC List guide](https://filerequestpro.com/articles/pbc-list)
- Receivables/payables items: accounts receivable aging schedule (year-end) with allowance-for-doubtful-accounts analysis; accounts payable aging schedule (year-end); accrued expense schedules with supporting calculations. — [FileRequestPro PBC List guide](https://filerequestpro.com/articles/pbc-list)
- Fixed assets/inventory items: fixed asset register with additions, disposals, and depreciation for the period; inventory listing with quantities, unit costs, and valuations; physical inventory count results; obsolescence reserve analysis. — [FileRequestPro PBC List guide](https://filerequestpro.com/articles/pbc-list)
- Cash/banking items: bank reconciliations for all accounts (year-end); bank statements (last month of the period and first month following); bank confirmations. — [FileRequestPro PBC List guide](https://filerequestpro.com/articles/pbc-list)
- Debt/equity items: loan agreements and amendments; debt amortization schedules and year-end balance confirmations; equity roll-forward documentation. — [FileRequestPro PBC List guide](https://filerequestpro.com/articles/pbc-list)
- Tax/payroll items: federal/state (i.e., relevant jurisdiction's) tax returns; payroll tax filings (quarterly and annual); estimated tax payment records. — [FileRequestPro PBC List guide](https://filerequestpro.com/articles/pbc-list)
- Governance/other items: board and shareholder meeting minutes; an attorney letter confirming pending or threatened litigation; insurance policies; the management representation letter (signed at the close of fieldwork) and, in some firm templates, an attorney representation letter. — [FileRequestPro PBC List guide](https://filerequestpro.com/articles/pbc-list); [Chazin & Company PBC checklist article](https://www.chazinandcompany.com/2021/05/the-pbc-audit-checklist-and-why-you-need-one/)
- General PBC-list framing from other secondary sources: a "frozen" TB, GL detail by account, and mapping to financial-statement lines are commonly requested; fixed-asset rollforwards, depreciation, policies, and lease registers/memos are standard; signed contracts and leases entered into during the period are also typically requested alongside board minutes and representation letters. — [WebSearch summary of PBC-list sources](https://madrasaccountancy.com/blog-posts/audit-ready-in-30-days-pbc-request-list-template-for-first-time-audits); [WebSearch summary](https://www.glitter.io/blog/process-documentation/audit-preparation-checklist)
- File format: the FileRequestPro guide names Excel and Google Sheets as the standard formats for schedules; it does not specify a preferred submission format for signed/scanned documents (contracts, confirmations, minutes), which in practice are PDF. — [FileRequestPro PBC List guide](https://filerequestpro.com/articles/pbc-list)

### Inferences
- The PBC categories map closely to the assignment's target list (trial balance, GL detail, AR/AP aging, fixed asset register and depreciation, inventory valuation, bank reconciliations, tax returns, payroll, loans) with two items — **related parties** and **subsequent events** — not independently confirmed as distinct PBC-list line items in the sources retrieved (they appear implicitly, e.g. via board minutes and attorney letters, which are the usual audit evidence for both), so these two should be treated as standard-practice inclusions inferred from adjacent evidence rather than directly cited PBC-list entries.
- Because schedules (TB, aging, FA register, depreciation, inventory listings, bank recs, debt schedules) are inherently tabular/numeric, Excel/CSV is the natural and cited preferred format for structured PBC items, while signed or third-party documents (confirmations, contracts, minutes, tax filings, attorney letters) are typically exchanged as PDF/scanned copies — this format split is an inference from the nature of each item type plus the one explicit format citation (Excel/Google Sheets for schedules) rather than a single source stating both formats side by side.

### Gaps
- No single source in this research batch explicitly listed "related party transactions" or "subsequent events" as their own named PBC checklist line items (distinct from board minutes/attorney letters); a report writer wanting a directly-cited line item for these should pull a big-four or mid-tier firm's own published PBC template (not retrieved in this session — the specific "Appendix III" year-end PBC list PDF found via search failed to yield readable text through the fetch/index tooling used).
- Could not independently verify a named source's explicit statement on PDF vs. Excel/CSV as the *industry-standard* preferred submission format split; this is treated as an inference above, not a cited fact.

## Which Maldivian companies must be audited, under which standards, and what does MIRA need?

### Takeaway
Under the Maldives Companies Act 2023 (Law 07/2023, in force from 1 January 2024, replacing Act 10/96), the blanket audit requirement for all private limited companies was removed; audited financial statements are now required only for companies with annual turnover of MVR 10,000,000 (10 million) or more (per section 102(b) of the Income Tax Regulations, MIRA's operative rule), with smaller companies only required to keep accounting records. CA Maldives has endorsed ISA (2021, with 2025 rolling-adoption of amendments) and IFRS/IAS/IFRS for SMEs (2021) as the applicable auditing and accounting standards, and a CA Maldives (or Auditor General, for statutory/public-sector work) practicing certificate is required to provide statutory audit services; audited companies above the threshold must submit the audit report to MIRA together with the profit/income tax return.

### Cited Findings
- The Maldives Companies Act 2023 came into force on 1 January 2024, replacing the 1996 Companies Act (Act 10/96); existing companies were given until 31 December 2024 to comply with the new provisions. — [WebSearch summary of SHC Lawyers/CTL Strategies](https://shclawyers.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/SHC-Regulatory-Bulletin-Companies-Act-2023-16.1.2024.pdf); [CTL Strategies](https://www.ctlstrategies.com/latest/the-president-ratifies-the-new-companies-act/)
- Under the repealed 1996 Act, **all** private limited companies, regardless of size or turnover, were required to appoint an auditor and present audited financial statements at a shareholders' general meeting; a 2018 MIRA Tax Ruling had already administratively limited the *submission-to-MIRA* requirement to companies with turnover ≥ MVR 10,000,000. — [WebSearch summary](https://www.nasheeds.co/blog/companies-a-new-era-for-audited-financial-statements); [WebSearch summary](https://www.lawrbit.com/article/company-law-of-maldives-a-recent-overview/)
- The new Companies Act 2023 formally relaxed the audit requirement: companies below the MVR 10,000,000 turnover threshold are no longer required to appoint an auditor or hold an audit at all — only to maintain accounting records; companies at or above the threshold must appoint an auditor and submit audited financial statements, per section 102(b) of the Income Tax Regulations. — [WebSearch summary](https://www.lawrbit.com/article/company-law-of-maldives-a-recent-overview/); [Nasheed & Co blog](https://www.nasheeds.co/blog/companies-act-a-new-era-for-audited-financial-statements)
- The first submission deadline for financial statements under the new regime was 30 June 2024. — [Nasheed & Co blog](https://www.nasheeds.co/blog/companies-act-a-new-era-for-audited-financial-statements)
- CA Maldives (Institute of Chartered Accountants of the Maldives) endorsed IFRS, IAS, and IFRS for SMEs for financial reporting in the Maldives on 23 November 2021. — [WebSearch summary of camaldives.org](https://camaldives.org/accounting/)
- CA Maldives endorsed ISA and other IAASB pronouncements for use in the private sector via Council Resolution RES/2021/028, and in 2025 adopted subsequent ISA amendments, establishing an ongoing process to adopt future ISA revisions unless the Council formally defers/declines. — [WebSearch summary](https://camaldives.org/auditing/); confirmed via direct fetch: [CA Maldives Auditing page](https://camaldives.org/auditing/)
- CA Maldives enforces audit licensing through the "Regulation for Statutory Audit and Assurance Services in the Maldives," a Quality Assurance Review (QAR) framework, and derives its legislative authority from the Chartered Accountants Act 13/2020 (also referenced elsewhere as the "Maldives Chartered Accountants Act 2020," which assigns CA Maldives responsibility for setting auditing standards); it maintains public registries of licensed audit firms, licensed sole practitioners, and registered Public Interest Entity auditors. — [CA Maldives Auditing page](https://camaldives.org/auditing/)
- Individuals/firms must hold a valid practicing certificate issued by CA Maldives (per the Regulation for Statutory Audit and Assurance Services) to provide statutory audit and assurance services; separately, the Regulation on Statutory Audit issued by the Auditor General's Office requires financial audits of state-owned enterprises to be conducted per ISA, and the Maldives Banking Act 2010 requires bank external auditors to audit on a consolidated basis per recognized auditing standards (interpreted as ISA). — [WebSearch summary](https://www.ctlstrategies.com/latest/the-president-ratifies-the-new-companies-act/); [WebSearch summary of Regulation for Statutory Audit sources]
- MIRA requires financial statements to be submitted together with a taxpayer's tax return unless MIRA has exempted that person from the requirement; the required statements are the Statement of Profit or Loss and Other Comprehensive Income, the Statement of Financial Position, and the Statement of Cash Flows. — [MIRA legislation summary](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/submission-of-fs-and-appointment-b49)
- Accounts required under the relevant MIRA regulation must be prepared under IFRS or another MIRA-acceptable international accounting standard on the accrual basis; however, a person whose annual turnover does not exceed MVR 10,000,000 may elect the cash basis instead. All large businesses must appoint a "Category A" auditor registered with MIRA, and all audits for the purposes of the Act must follow ISA and, where relevant, ISAE. — [MIRA legislation summary](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/submission-of-fs-and-appointment-b49)
- Under the Tax Administration Act/Regulation, all documents required to be maintained must be kept at the taxpayer's principal place of business in the Maldives (or another Maldives location accessible to MIRA on demand) and must be maintained legibly; failure to maintain records carries a civil penalty of up to MVR 5,000 and a criminal penalty of 1–6 months' house arrest. A specific numeric retention period (in years) for these records was not stated in the sources retrieved. — [WebSearch summary of MIRA Tax Administration Regulation](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Tax-Administration-Regulation-consolidated)

### Inferences
- Taken together, an accounting/audit app targeting Maldivian SMEs should treat MVR 10,000,000 annual turnover as the operative audit trigger (driven by MIRA's Income Tax Regulations reading of the Companies Act 2023), should default financial statements to accrual-basis IFRS/IFRS-for-SMEs above that threshold (with an explicit cash-basis option below it, per the MIRA regulation), and should support attaching/flagging a "Category A auditor" registration field and an audit report artifact for submission alongside the profit/income tar return.
- Because CA Maldives runs a rolling-adoption process for ISA amendments (2021 baseline, formal 2025 update round), any statement of "current" ISA/ISA-for-SME status in a Maldives-facing product should be periodically re-verified against CA Maldives' own auditing page rather than treated as permanently fixed.

## India's Rule 3(1) audit-trail/edit-log requirement (comparative reference)

### Takeaway
India's Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014, Rule 3(1) proviso (effective for FYs commencing on/after 1 April 2023) requires every company using accounting software to use software that records an audit trail of every transaction, creates an edit log (with the date) of every change made to the books of account, and cannot have that audit trail disabled — a "three limbs" test — with the statutory minimum only requiring the *date* of change (not necessarily the user), and companies must retain books (including the trail) for at least eight years under Section 128(5) of the Companies Act, 2013.

### Cited Findings
- The Rule 3(1) proviso text: "every company which uses accounting software for maintaining its books of account, shall use only such accounting software which has a feature of recording audit trail of each and every transaction, creating an edit log of each change made in books of account along with the date when such changes were made and ensuring that the audit trail cannot be disabled," effective for financial years commencing on or after 1 April 2023. — [Taxguru: Audit Trail Compliance under Rule 3(1) and Rule 11(g)](https://taxguru.in/company-law/audit-trail-compliance-rule-3-1-rule-11-practitioners-guide.html)
- Three limbs must all be satisfied: audit trail of every transaction; an edit log of every change with its date; and no ability to disable the trail. The rule's statutory minimum only requires the change *date*, not the identity of the user who made it, though "every reasonable product" records the user in practice and every auditor asks for it. — [Taxguru guide](https://taxguru.in/company-law/audit-trail-compliance-rule-3-1-rule-11-practitioners-guide.html)
- Rule 11(g) of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014 turns this into an auditor reporting obligation: the statutory auditor's report (from FY 2023-24) must state whether the audit-trail software feature exists, was operated throughout the year for all recorded transactions, was not tampered with, and has been preserved per the statutory retention requirement. — [Taxguru guide](https://taxguru.in/company-law/audit-trail-compliance-rule-3-1-rule-11-practitioners-guide.html)
- Applicability: every company registered under the Companies Act, 2013 (including small companies, One Person Companies, Section 8 companies, and listed companies) — no carve-out by size — but LLPs, partnerships, HUFs, and proprietorships are outside this specific rule. — [Taxguru guide](https://taxguru.in/company-law/audit-trail-compliance-rule-3-1-rule-11-practitioners-guide.html)
- Because the audit trail is deemed part of the "books of account," Section 128(5) of the Companies Act, 2013 requires it to be preserved for a minimum of eight financial years — an obligation that survives a change of accounting software or cancellation of a subscription. Separately, Rule 3(5) requires companies keeping books electronically to take a daily backup on servers physically located in India, and Rule 3(6) requires annual intimation to the Registrar of the electronic-records service provider's details when books are hosted on a third-party/cloud server. — [Taxguru guide](https://taxguru.in/company-law/audit-trail-compliance-rule-3-1-rule-11-practitioners-guide.html)
- For cloud/SaaS accounting products, auditors are expected to conclude "cannot be disabled" and "not tampered with" via: the absence of any disabling setting in the product itself (an "off switch" test), the vendor's SOC/service-organization report under SA 402 (India's equivalent of ISA/SSAE service-org reporting), and the auditor's own substantive testing of the log against known transactions. — [Taxguru guide](https://taxguru.in/company-law/audit-trail-compliance-rule-3-1-rule-11-practitioners-guide.html)
- Related but distinct Indian retention/edit-log rules: GST's CGST Rules Rule 56(15) has required an edit/delete log for electronic records since 1 July 2017 (applicable to every registered person, any legal form, including LLPs/proprietorships that Rule 3(1) does not reach); CGST Act Section 36 separately requires books/records to be retained for a minimum of 72 months (6 years) from the due date of filing the relevant annual return, which in practice extends retention beyond 7 years from financial year-end. — [Taxguru guide](https://taxguru.in/company-law/audit-trail-compliance-rule-3-1-rule-11-practitioners-guide.html); [WebSearch summary re: CGST Section 36](https://ardhorajiya.com/period-of-retention-of-accounts-section-36-of-gst/)

### Inferences
- As a comparison point for an audit/accounting app operating in a jurisdiction without an explicit statutory audit-trail mandate (such as the Maldives, where no equivalent rule was found in this research), India's Rule 3(1)/11(g) pairing is a useful design target: an immutable, always-on edit log capturing at least the change date (and, as best practice, the user) satisfies the *strictest* comparable regime found, and would likely satisfy weaker ISA-240-driven expectations (which only require that the auditor be *able* to identify unusual entries/users/timing, not that the software itself be legally required to log them).

### Gaps
- No equivalent statutory audit-trail/edit-log mandate for accounting software was found for the Maldives in the sources reviewed; this section is included purely as the comparative reference the assignment requested, not as a Maldives finding.
