# Audit-Firm / Client-Auditor Collaboration Tools: PBC Workflow, Data Ingestion, JE Testing & Sampling

## What does a standard PBC list contain for a small company year-end audit?

### Takeaway
A small-company year-end PBC list is organized around the trial balance/GL, supporting schedules for every balance-sheet line, bank/cash support, and a small set of governance/legal items — the auditor issues it weeks before fieldwork and tracks it item-by-item (assignee, due date, status) rather than as a flat document checklist.

### Cited Findings
- A PBC checklist is "a list of all documentation, spreadsheets, and schedules that your auditing firm expects your organization to provide as part of your annual audit," typically issued a few months before the audit starts — [Chazin & Company](https://www.chazinandcompany.com/2021/05/the-pbc-audit-checklist-and-why-you-need-one/)
- Core financial-record items on a typical list: the final, frozen trial balance (ideally in searchable Excel), detailed general ledger activity for all accounts (used for substantive testing), a complete draft of the financial statements (footnotes/schedules/disclosures), and a list with support for every non-standard or significant year-end journal entry — [Suralink/aggregated PBC guides](https://www.suralink.com/library/understanding-the-fundamentals-of-pbc-provided-by-client-request-lists)
- Cash/banking items: month-by-month bank reconciliations for all accounts for the full year, copies of final bank statements, and a list of authorized bank signers — [PBC list guides synthesis](https://contentsnare.com/pbc-list/)
- Other standard items: a memo describing changes to internal controls, systems, or accounting policy since the prior audit; and significant contracts, litigation documents, legal correspondence, and attorney representation letters where applicable — [PBC list guides synthesis](https://contentsnare.com/pbc-list/)
- "A PBC list is not a standard checklist for all auditors" — contents vary by company size, industry, audit objective, and prior-year experience — [Chazin & Company](https://www.chazinandcompany.com/2024/02/streamlining-audits-with-pbc-checklists-what-you-need-to-know/)
- Audit PBC requests are defined as items auditors ask the client's finance/accounting/tax/treasury/operations teams to provide: "schedules, reconciliations, reports, calculations, documents, approvals, or explanations that support the financial statements" — [AuditDashboard](https://www.auditdashboard.com/post/what-is-a-pbc-request-list-or-pbc-list)

### Inferences
- Because the trial balance and detailed GL are the two items every source calls out first, an accounting app aiming to "feed" audit tools should be able to export a frozen, period-locked TB and full-year GL detail as its highest-priority deliverable, not just summary reports.
- The "list of significant/non-standard journal entries" item implies the accounting app should be able to flag/tag journal entries as standard vs. manual/adjusting at the point of entry, since auditors ask for this subset specifically (this maps directly to JE testing tools' need for entry metadata — see Sampling section).

### Gaps
- No single authoritative "master PBC list" template was found (sources agree the content is firm- and engagement-specific); a definitive small-company-specific published checklist (vs. general guidance) was not located.

## How do these tools ingest data from accounting apps (APIs, file formats, CSV templates), and what fields do they require?

### Takeaway
Two ingestion patterns dominate: (1) direct API/connector integration (Validis, and Inflo's "Data Ingestion Wizard") that pulls GL/TB/subledger data straight from the accounting system in near-real-time, standardizing it into a common schema; and (2) structured file upload (CSV/XLSX/XLS export from the accounting system, following a vendor-specified template) when no direct connector exists. The AICPA's Audit Data Standards (ADS) define the canonical field sets (GL detail, TB, JE) that these ingestion pipelines target.

### Cited Findings
- Validis "connects to accounting systems including QuickBooks, Sage (50, 100, 200, Intacct), NetSuite, Xero, Microsoft Dynamics (365, GP, NAV, BC), SAP Business One, Workday and Oracle — both cloud and on-premise," and "with a single API integration, Validis automatically extracts, standardizes and structures financial data from every major accounting package" — [Validis](https://www.validis.com/product/audit/)
- Validis defines "audit data extraction" as "the process of pulling general ledger, trial balance, journal entry and subledger data directly from a client's accounting system, ready for audit testing," connecting to "100+ accounting systems" and delivering "standardized, audit-ready data in minutes" — [Validis Audit & Assurance FAQ](https://www.validis.com/product/audit/)
- Where a client runs a system without a direct connector, Validis falls back to an "upload-and-share workflow," and once connected, data can be "refresh[ed] on demand or on a schedule — no manual export and re-import each period" — [Validis Audit & Assurance FAQ](https://www.validis.com/product/audit/)
- Validis is "headless": "AI agents can query standardized client financial data through Validis's API or MCP server, in real time, with no human in the loop to retrieve it," with access "permissioned and read-only at the point of extraction" — [Validis](https://www.validis.com/product/audit/)
- For Xero specifically, Validis requires GL and TB reports exported "in (.CSV), (.XLSX), or (.XLS) file format," with "file size must not exceed 100 MB" and reports must "already contain the required fields without manual manipulation" — [Validis Support: Downloading GL & TB from Xero](https://support.validis.com/hc/en-gb/articles/37948517395860-Downloading-GL-TB-from-Xero)
- Validis publishes per-source-system guides (Xero, QuickBooks Online, Accounts IQ, MRI Financials, Carta, Yardi Voyager, Access Dimensions, Exchequer, Microsoft Dynamics 365 F&O, Dynamics AX, etc.), each with its own extraction instructions — [Validis Support Center](https://support.validis.com/hc/en-gb/articles/37948517395860-Downloading-GL-TB-from-Xero)
- Inflo's data ingestion "extracts transactional data from the client general ledger accounting system, transforming this data into a standard data model format, checking it for completeness and accuracy and loading the data into tools for advanced automation and analysis," working "in real-time" and ingesting "from 100% of client accounting systems" via an "intuitive Data Ingestion Wizard" that "guides you through multiple extraction techniques for each accounting system" — [Inflo Software: Data Ingestion](https://inflosoftware.com/features/data-ingestion/)
- The AICPA's Audit Data Standards (ADS) General Ledger Standard "adds the chart of accounts, source listings, trial balance, and GL detail" to the standardized field set — [AAA/AICPA teaching notes](https://publications.aaahq.org/jetatn/article-supplement/9512/pdf/tnjt-18-01-07_s01/)
- The AICPA-ADS Audit Data API (GL Primer) specifies that a **journal entry endpoint must return, at minimum**: `Journal_ID`, `Effective_Date`, `GL_Account_Number`, `Amount`, `Amount_Currency`, `Amount_Credit_Debit_Indicator` — additional required fields are marked in the Swagger schema — [AICPA-ADS AuditData-API GL Primer](https://github.com/AICPA-ADS/AuditData-API/blob/master/GL-Primer.md)
- That same API deliberately returns flat (non-nested) data for every endpoint except the journal-entry endpoint, where line items are nested as children of the journal entry, "to allow maximum flexibility to support transfer from as many systems as possible" — [AICPA-ADS AuditData-API GL Primer](https://github.com/AICPA-ADS/AuditData-API/blob/master/GL-Primer.md)
- The API also exposes a "Trial Balance in XBRL" endpoint returning an XBRL-JSON instance of the trial balance, filterable by end date, entity identifier, and specific GL account number — [AICPA-ADS AuditData-API GL Primer](https://github.com/AICPA-ADS/AuditData-API/blob/master/GL-Primer.md)
- General ledger detail fields referenced in AICPA/ADS-derived material include an Entry ID (identifier per GL entry), User/User ID (person who created the entry), and Posted Date (system-generated timestamp when the entry was actually posted/validated to the ledger) — [AAA/AICPA teaching notes; ADS materials](https://publications.aaahq.org/jetatn/article-supplement/9512/pdf/tnjt-18-01-07_s01/)

### Inferences
- An accounting app that wants to be "fed to" these tools rather than compete with them should prioritize: (a) a direct read-only API/connector Validis/Inflo can pull from, or failing that (b) a one-click CSV/XLSX export of GL detail and TB that matches the field set these vendors already expect (entry ID, effective/posted date, account number, amount, currency, debit/credit indicator, user/preparer ID) — mirroring the AICPA ADS journal-entry schema is the safest common denominator since multiple ingestion vendors already build around it.
- The recurring "no manual manipulation of the export" requirement (Validis/Xero) suggests audit-ingestion tools are sensitive to exports that have been reformatted or filtered — a native, unmodified system export capability matters as much as field completeness.

### Gaps
- Exact full field lists for Inflo's "Data Ingestion Wizard" and per-system CSV templates were not retrievable in full detail (the fetched page returned mostly binary/image content); only its general description was available. A gap: the precise minimum required fields Inflo enforces per source system were not found.
- Detailed field-level requirements for CaseWare Cloud's own GL import (as opposed to CaseWare IDEA) were not directly examined in this pass.
- No confirmation was found on whether these tools support real-time/webhook-based sync from QBO/Xero/NetSuite APIs versus only scheduled batch pulls — Validis states "refresh on demand or on a schedule," implying batch/pull rather than event-driven webhooks, but this was not explicitly confirmed either way.

## What client-facing features (Q&A, uploads, status) are considered best practice?

### Takeaway
Best-practice client collaboration tools (Suralink, AuditDashboard/AuditBoard, CaseWare PBC Requests, Inflo Collaborate, Thomson Reuters Engagement Manager, CCH ProSystem fx Engagement Organizer, Canopy) converge on the same core pattern: a per-item request with an assignee, a due date, drag-and-drop upload, a real-time status dashboard (outstanding → submitted → approved), threaded Q&A/comments on each request, automatic reminder notifications, and a full audit trail of who did what and when.

### Cited Findings
- Suralink lets firms "build a dynamic PBC request list from existing spreadsheet templates, assign individual firm and client users to specific requests with controlled permissions, track outstanding and completed requests in real time via a central dashboard, and review and approve each submission through a built-in workflow with a full audit trail" — [Suralink](https://www.suralink.com/technology/request-list-management)
- Every action in Suralink is logged with an audit trail, "giving firms full visibility into request statuses, document uploads, and client interactions" — [Suralink](https://www.suralink.com/library/understanding-the-fundamentals-of-pbc-provided-by-client-request-lists)
- Suralink emphasizes enterprise-grade encryption, role-based access controls, and SOC 2/GDPR/CCPA compliance for the collaboration platform — [Suralink](https://www.suralink.com/library/understanding-the-fundamentals-of-pbc-provided-by-client-request-lists)
- AuditDashboard/AuditBoard gives "visibility into what has been requested, what has been uploaded, who each request is assigned to, and for what date," with requests automatically flipping "from outstanding to submitted when a client responds ... by uploading documents or answering a query" — [AuditDashboard](https://www.auditdashboard.com/pbc-requests)
- AuditDashboard is positioned as ideal for "projects that are done remotely, involve multiple people, or have long lists of requests," tracking both team and client PBC requests together — [AuditDashboard](https://www.auditdashboard.com/pbc-requests)
- Caseware PBC Requests are built on "Query" documents: a request list that "can be instantly sent to client contacts" and tracks progress as clients respond; the workflow includes reviewing the query, sending it, clients responding, reviewing client progress/responses, collaborating with clients, and a dedicated "query dashboard" for status, followed by a "complete the query" wrap-up step — [Caseware PBC Requests Docs](https://www.caseware.com/docs/en/cloud/pbc-requests/pbc-requests/pbc-requests-index)
- Caseware PBC lets teams "work directly in an engagement file while building and sending information requests," retrieve/accept documents that flow automatically into the engagement file, and get "real-time status updates" via "built-in automation for enhanced status-tracking" — [Caseware blog](https://www.caseware.com/resources/blog/casewares-pbc-requests-client-collaboration-redefined)
- Inflo Collaborate is a "file sharing functionality that automates prepared by client workflow," storing files requested from end clients, shared between accounting-firm users and end-client users assigned by an admin with the User Administration role — [ICAEW Inflo evaluation](https://www.icaew.com/-/media/corporate/files/members/offers-and-discounts/accreditation-reports/inflo-evaluation-report.ashx)
- Thomson Reuters Engagement Manager's Client Communications feature lets firms "send and track all client communications — including PBC requests — right from Engagement Manager," with auditors and clients seeing "each other's comments in real time," drag-and-drop upload, and the ability to "upload sample documents to show how a request should be completed" — [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/en/insights/articles/engagement-manager-client-communications-on-cloud-audit-suite)
- Thomson Reuters notes that managing PBC lists via email creates "version-control challenges," which centralized cloud collection solves with real-time visibility into received vs. outstanding items — [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/en/insights/articles/engagement-manager-client-communications-on-cloud-audit-suite)
- CCH ProSystem fx Engagement's "Engagement Organizer" lets clients "upload documents into a secure portal by the date required," and firms can "upload templates or documents their clients need to complete," with automatic notifications "when an organizer is ready for the client, when changes are made ... and when requests are outstanding and the due date is approaching" — [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/news/wolters-kluwer-enhances-its-cch-prosystem-fx-engagement-solution-with-new-engagement-organizer)
- Canopy's document-request feature is "well-designed for the back-and-forth of tax and advisory engagements — tracking what's been requested, what's been received, and sending automated reminders without requiring staff to manually track" — [Financial Cents comparison](https://financial-cents.com/resources/articles/karbon-vs-canopy/)
- DataSnipper's Test of Detail workflow lets auditors "automatically extract and cross-reference supporting documentation to your audit sample" and "share a 'plug-and-play' Excel file throughout your team, that is easy to open and review," with a "Document Matching" capability that finds "the correct text, date and/or number in a source document" and auto-creates a reference to sample data — [DataSnipper](https://www.datasnipper.com/external-audit/test-of-detail)

### Inferences
- The consistent feature set across every vendor (assignee + due date + upload + status + reminder + audit trail + Q&A thread) is effectively the minimum viable spec for any "client portal" surface an accounting app might build or integrate with — this is not vendor-differentiated, it is table stakes.
- Because every tool treats the request list as the same object that later becomes the audit trail/evidence log, an accounting app that only exposes a generic "file upload" without per-request metadata (assignee, due date, linked GL account/schedule) would not satisfy what auditors expect these platforms to carry.

### Gaps
- Detailed UI/UX specifics of AuditBoard's own (non-AuditDashboard) PBC module were not directly retrieved — findings here rely on AuditDashboard, which functions as AuditBoard's client-facing PBC product.
- Karbon's own PBC-specific features were not clearly distinguished from its general email/workflow tooling in the sources found; comparisons found were mostly Karbon-vs-Canopy summaries rather than Karbon PBC feature documentation itself.

## What sampling methods do audit tools implement (MUS, random, stratified, key items) and how are samples documented?

### Takeaway
Audit sampling/analytics tools (CaseWare IDEA, TeamMate Analytics/Diligent ACL Analytics, MindBridge, DataSnipper) implement the standard statistical toolkit — Monetary Unit Sampling (MUS/PPS), simple random, systematic, stratified, and classical variables sampling — often layered with 100%-population AI/ML risk-scoring (MindBridge, DataSnipper) that either replaces or prioritizes traditional sampling; documentation centers on a defined sample-design rationale, the selection method, and (for MUS) a projected-misstatement/conclusion report, per ISA 530.

### Cited Findings
- CaseWare IDEA "supports a wide range of statistical sampling methods, including Monetary Unit Sampling (MUS), Probability Proportional to Size (PPS), classical variables, and stratified sampling, enabling precise risk-based testing on massive datasets" — [CaseWare Africa](https://www.casewareafrica.com/caseware-idea_internal-audit/)
- In MUS, "each monetary equivalent is considered a sampling unit, so that residuals in an array with a higher value have a proportionately higher probability of selection," directing audit effort to higher-value amounts; IDEA can "calculate the coefficients and form MUS in two clicks" and then "generate a conclusion report to further examine the nature of the data" — [CaseWare Ukraine: Monetary Unit Sampling](http://idea.caseware.com.ua/en/2020/04/30/monetary-unit-sampling/)
- Stratified random sampling is described as useful for "dual-purpose tests," randomly selecting items from each stratum per the auditor's judgment on how many items to draw from each group, and can be "focused on areas that are more material" — [CaseWare Ukraine](http://idea.caseware.com.ua/en/2020/04/30/monetary-unit-sampling/)
- Diligent ACL Analytics "supports a wide range of sampling methods including random, systematic, stratified, and Monetary Unit Sampling (MUS)"; Wolters Kluwer TeamMate Analytics likewise offers "statistical methods like random, stratified, systematic, and monetary unit sampling (MUS)" — [aggregated audit-sampling-software summary](https://gitnux.org/best/audit-sampling-software/)
- MUS is described elsewhere as "a specialized form of stratified sampling where the 'strata' are effectively defined by dollar magnitude, with larger balances proportionally more likely to be selected"; for populations dominated by a few large items, stratified sampling "often includes full testing of items above a scoping threshold and a random sample from the remainder" (i.e., key-item/scoping-threshold testing combined with random sampling of the residual population) — [audit sampling methods synthesis](https://zipdo.co/best/audit-sampling-software/)
- Under ISA 530, "the auditor decides to use sampling for tests of controls or substantive tests of details," and the standard "requires the auditor to determine a [sample design, size, and selection method]"; for tests of controls the sampling unit is typically a transaction/document and results are expressed as a deviation rate, while for tests of details the sampling unit is typically a monetary unit or transaction/balance and results are expressed as a projected misstatement amount — [ciferi: ISA 530 Audit Sampling Guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-530-audit-sampling-guide)
- Regulatory inspection findings cited under ISA 530/equivalents across jurisdictions repeatedly flag: insufficient sample sizes for the assessed risk level, failure to project misstatements to the full population, inadequate documentation of the rationale for sample design, weak evaluation of results, and use of "default" sample sizes without adjusting for specific circumstances (Netherlands/AFM, Germany/WPK, UK/FRC, France/H3C) — [ciferi: ISA 530 Audit Sampling Guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-530-audit-sampling-guide)
- MindBridge's General Ledger Analytics runs "a suite of machine learning, statistical, and rule-based control points or tests" — "32 tests simultaneously on up to 500 million records," equivalent to "analyzing 16 billion general ledger data points" — enabling analysis of "100% of journal entries" rather than a drawn sample — [MindBridge](https://www.mindbridge.ai/blog/better-approach-journal-entry-testing/); [MindBridge General Ledger Analytics](https://www.mindbridge.ai/general-ledger-analytics/)
- The "MindBridge score" for a GL analysis is "a weighted average of the control points configured in the library or engagement," and transactions/entries with higher risk scores are "more likely to be of interest during audit scenarios," letting auditors focus manual JE testing on entries that failed multiple control-point tests (e.g., as indicators of error or management override of controls) — [MindBridge Support](https://support.mindbridge.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360055739874-MindBridge-score)
- DataSnipper's approach to journal-entry testing includes "risk-based screening" that "screens the full journal entry population against risk criteria and surfaces high-risk items for review, with a complete audit trail," plus AI-driven "full-population analysis" that flags "patterns and anomalies across entire datasets, enabling full-population testing — eliminating limitations of manual sampling" — [DataSnipper resources](https://www.datasnipper.com/resources/how-external-audit-testing-evolve-as-data-volumes-and-risk-increase)
- DataSnipper's Document Matching feature automates evidence-linking for a drawn sample: it "automatically match[es] Excel data with supporting documents, such as invoices, bank statements, and contracts," finding "the correct text, date and/or number in a source document" and creating "a reference to your sample data" — used for both Test of Detail and Test of Control procedures — [DataSnipper Test of Detail](https://www.datasnipper.com/external-audit/test-of-detail)

### Inferences
- The industry is bifurcating: classical statistical sampling (MUS/random/stratified, as in CaseWare IDEA, ACL/TeamMate Analytics) remains the ISA-530-compliant baseline, while AI-analytics vendors (MindBridge, DataSnipper) increasingly pitch full-population risk-scoring as a substitute or complement, meaning any GL export an accounting app produces should support both a drawable "population" (for classical sampling) and a "flag/score-ready" full dataset (entry-level metadata) for anomaly-detection tools.
- Because ISA 530 inspection failures repeatedly cite "documentation of the rationale for sample design" and "projection of misstatements to the population," any sample a downstream tool draws needs source data with enough context (population size, stratification boundaries, total population value) — reinforcing that a feeding accounting app should expose population totals/counts alongside GL detail, not just the transaction rows.

### Gaps
- Specific software step-by-step documentation format used by CaseWare IDEA or TeamMate Analytics for a completed sample's audit trail/workpaper (i.e., exact fields recorded per sample item) was not retrieved in full; only qualitative descriptions of the sampling methods and general "conclusion report" mention were found.
- Key-item/exception testing as a distinct, named sampling method within a specific vendor's product documentation (vs. general audit-methodology descriptions) was not directly confirmed — the finding here is inferred from stratified-sampling/scoping-threshold descriptions rather than a vendor page explicitly labeling a "key items" feature.
