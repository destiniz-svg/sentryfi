# External Confirmations, Inventory Count Observation, and Proposed Adjustments (ISA 505/AS 2310, ISA 501, ISA 450)

## ISA 505 control requirement: can a confirmation be sent through the client's own portal link? What safeguards make it acceptable (auditor controls sending, responses go directly to auditor)?

### Takeaway
Under both ISA 505 and the new PCAOB AS 2310 (effective for fiscal years ending on or after June 15, 2025), the auditor — not the client — must select what is confirmed, send the request, and receive the response; a client-controlled portal is acceptable only as a pass-through consent/authorization mechanism (satisfying the bank's own customer-consent requirement) that never lets the client see, hold, or alter the substantive request or response. Confirmation.com and Circit are both built around this split: the client authorizes disclosure, but the confirming institution's response is delivered straight to the auditor's account, bypassing the client entirely.

### Cited Findings
- ISA 505 requires the auditor to "maintain control over external confirmation requests, including determining the information to be confirmed and selecting who will be asked to provide the confirmation," and to design the requests (mailing address, auditor's own return information, a named knowledgeable individual at the confirming party) to maximize reliability and response likelihood — [WebSearch summary of ISA 505 text via iasplus/ICJCE/IBR-IRE sources](https://www.ibr-ire.be/docs/default-source/nl/Documents/regelgeving-en-publicaties/rechtsleer/normen-en-aanbevelingen/ISA-s/clarified-ISA-s/ISA-update-2015/English/A024-ISA-505-for-Handbook_formatted.pdf)
- An oral response does not meet the ISA 505 definition of an external confirmation because it is not a direct written response — [same source](https://www.ibr-ire.be/docs/default-source/nl/Documents/regelgeving-en-publicaties/rechtsleer/normen-en-aanbevelingen/ISA-s/clarified-ISA-s/ISA-update-2015/English/A024-ISA-505-for-Handbook_formatted.pdf)
- A positive confirmation request asks the confirming party to respond whether they agree or disagree, or to supply the information; a negative confirmation request asks the confirming party to respond only if they disagree, so silence is treated as implicit agreement. Negative confirmations give less persuasive evidence and under ISA 505 may only be used when: assessed risk of material misstatement is low, the auditor has evidence that relevant controls operate effectively, the population is a large number of small, homogeneous balances, and the auditor has no reason to believe recipients will disregard the request — [ciferi, Positive vs Negative Confirmation](https://ciferi.com/glossary/positive-vs-negative-confirmation)
- For a positive confirmation, non-response is not evidence; ISA 505.12 requires the auditor to perform alternative procedures for every non-response — [ciferi, Positive vs Negative Confirmation](https://ciferi.com/glossary/positive-vs-negative-confirmation)
- PCAOB AS 2310.14–.16: "The auditor should maintain control over the confirmation process to minimize the likelihood that information exchanged between the auditor and the confirming party is intercepted or altered. The auditor should (i) select the items to be confirmed, (ii) send confirmation requests, and (iii) receive confirmation responses. The auditor should send the confirmation request directly to the confirming party and obtain the confirmation response directly from the confirming party." — [PCAOB AS 2310](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2310)
- AS 2310.17 and Appendix B permit using an intermediary to facilitate *direct electronic transmission* between auditor and confirming party, but only if the auditor evaluates the intermediary's controls against interception/alteration risk: "Obtain an understanding of the intermediary's controls that address the risk of interception and alteration... Determine whether the controls used by the intermediary... are designed and operating effectively" — [PCAOB AS 2310, Appendix B](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2310)
- AS 2310.21–.23: if the auditor gets no response to a positive request, the auditor follows up with the confirming party; if a response is returned to anyone other than the auditor, the auditor must contact the confirming party and request it be re-sent directly to the auditor — if that doesn't happen, the auditor treats it as a nonresponse and performs alternative procedures — [PCAOB AS 2310](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2310)
- AS 2310 modernizes a standard drafted over 30 years ago (originally issued by AICPA in 1991, adopted by PCAOB in 2003), applies to all PCAOB audits for FYs ending on/after June 15, 2025, and explicitly "emphasizes the auditor's responsibility to maintain control over the confirmation process," with the auditor "responsible for selecting the items to be confirmed, sending confirmation requests and receiving confirmation responses" — [Baker Tilly / Schneider Downs summary via WebSearch](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/pcaob-introduces-enhanced-confirmation-process-standards); [PCAOB AS 2310 page](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2310)
- Confirmation.com's workflow: the auditor registers, selects the confirmation type (e.g., "Financial" for bank confirmations) and searches for the responding institution, pays the fee, and the *audit client* is required to provide online authorization for the confirming party to disclose information to the auditor — the client does not need to register or log in to Confirmation.com to give that authority. Once authorized, the confirming institution logs into its own queue, completes the confirmation using its internal data, and sends it to the auditor; the auditor gets an email notification and downloads the completed confirmation for the workpapers — [Confirmation.com Auditor User Guide via WebSearch](https://www.confirmation.com/media/236013/confirmationcom-auditor-user-guide_global_nov_2017.pdf); [Confirmation.com support article](https://confirmation.my.site.com/support/s/article/How-does-Confirmation-work); [Electronic Audit Confirmation Workflow](https://us.confirmation.com/blog/electronic-audit-confirmation-workflow)
- Circit is a regulated Account Information Service Provider (AISP) under PSD2 that connects auditors, banks, law firms, and fund administrators on one platform, hosted on Microsoft Azure — [Circit / Morningstar via WebSearch](https://www.circit.io/); [Morningstar funding announcement](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260210ln84981/circit-secures-22-million-growth-equity-funding-to-build-the-independent-verification-layer-powering-the-future-of-audit)
- "Once authorization is granted by the account holder, the verified transactions are delivered directly to the auditor from the back-end system of the bank, with no interference from the client... the client cannot access completed confirmations" — [ICAEW webcast on Circit's Verified Transactions Process, via WebSearch](https://www.icaew.com/-/media/corporate/files/technical/audit-and-assurance/audit-and-assurance-faculty/webcasts/circit-verified-transactions-process.ashx)
- Circit's Provider Network reports teams saving roughly 4 hours per client and bank confirmation turnaround down about 80% versus manual/paper confirmation processes — [Circit Provider Network, via WebSearch](https://www.circit.io/platform/provider-network)

### Inferences
- The client's role in a compliant digital confirmation workflow is narrow and specific: granting *consent/authorization* for the third party (bank, customer, supplier, law firm) to disclose data to the auditor. That consent step exists because the confirming party (especially a bank) legally needs customer authorization to release account data — it is a data-privacy gate, not an audit-control handoff. It must not be conflated with control over the confirmation *content* or *response*.
- A design is acceptable only if: (1) the auditor, not the client, chooses which balances/parties to confirm and what confirmation type to send; (2) the request is transmitted to the confirming party through a channel the client cannot edit or intercept; (3) the response is delivered to an address/account the auditor controls, with no client visibility or ability to modify it in transit; and (4) if any intermediary (a portal, a fintech, an open-banking rail) sits in the middle, the auditor has evaluated that intermediary's controls against interception and alteration (per AS 2310 Appendix B) — the client itself can never be that intermediary.
- Conversely, a "confirmation sent through the client's own portal" is **not** acceptable if the client's portal lets the client see the draft request before it goes out, edit or select what's confirmed, receive the response first, or forward/relay the response to the auditor at its own discretion — any of these would violate the "maintain control" / "receive directly from the confirming party" requirements in ISA 505 and AS 2310.14–.16. A well-designed accounting/audit app must architecturally prevent the client user role from having read or write access to confirmation requests or responses; only "grant consent" actions should be exposed to the client.
- For an accounting app that wants to support confirmations (e.g., surfacing a "request bank confirmation" feature to accountants using the client's own books), the safe pattern mirrors Confirmation.com/Circit: the auditor's tooling initiates and receives, the client's UI only ever shows a consent/authorization prompt, and the client cannot open, download, or resend the confirmation response.

### Gaps
- No primary-source text was found spelling out an exhaustive, itemized "a confirmation request must contain X, Y, Z" checklist beyond mailing address, auditor's return information, and a named knowledgeable contact; the full ISA 505 application material (A-paragraphs) likely has more granularity but the fetched PDF of the IBR-IRE ISA 505 handbook text did not extract as readable text in this session (binary/PDF parsing issue), so this notes file relies on secondary summaries rather than the primary clause numbers for that specific point.
- Did not find a documented case of a vendor being cited by a regulator for offering a non-compliant "client-controlled" confirmation portal; the "must NOT do" conclusions above are drawn from the standards' plain requirements rather than from an enforcement example.

## What does good software for count observation look like (auditor test counts recorded separately, cut-off documentation)?

### Takeaway
ISA 501 requires the auditor, when inventory is material, to attend the physical count (unless impracticable), evaluate management's counting instructions, observe management's count procedures, perform independent test counts in both directions (floor-to-sheet and sheet-to-floor), and capture cut-off (last goods-received/despatched document numbers) at the time of the count rather than reconstructing it later from the client; well-designed software should let the auditor log their own test counts as a distinct, timestamped, auditor-owned record separate from the client's count sheets, and capture cut-off document numbers contemporaneously in the field.

### Cited Findings
- ISA 501.4: "When inventory is material to the financial statements, the auditor shall obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the existence and condition of inventory by... attendance at physical inventory counting, unless impracticable" — [WebSearch summary of ISA 501 text](https://pasai.squarespace.com/s/isa-501.pdf); "the matter of general inconvenience to the auditor is not sufficient to support a decision that attendance is impracticable" — same source
- "To perform a stock count observation under ISA 501.4, evaluate the client's count instructions before attending, observe whether counters follow those instructions, perform test counts [in both directions], investigat[e] discrepancies between count records and the inventory listing, and check[] cut-off by recording the last goods received and despatched documents" — [ciferi, How to Perform Stock Count Observations Under ISA 501](https://ciferi.com/blog/how-to-perform-stock-count-observations-isa-501)
- Common practical failure flagged by file inspection (per ciferi's audit-methodology commentary): teams walk the warehouse, observe with no documented test counts, and request cut-off numbers "from the client a week later at completion" via email, typing them into the working paper after the fact rather than capturing them live at the count — [ciferi, How to Perform Stock Count Observations Under ISA 501](https://ciferi.com/blog/how-to-perform-stock-count-observations-isa-501)
- Floor-to-sheet: the auditor selects items physically present in the warehouse and traces them to the count sheet, testing that the count sheet is *complete* (nothing on the floor is omitted from the records). Sheet-to-floor: the auditor selects items already listed on the count sheet and traces them to the physical warehouse, testing *existence* (nothing on the records is fictitious) — [WebSearch summary, audit inventory test-count sources](https://www.chegg.com/homework-help/questions-and-answers/1-auditing-sheet-floor-procedures-test-existence-inventory-discuss-meant-sheet-floor-count-q35279355)
- A blind count is one where the counters perform a hands-on physical count without being shown the book/system quantity beforehand, which prevents counters from subconsciously matching an expected number and produces more reliable variance detection than an "open" count where the expected quantity is visible — [WebSearch summary, blind count sources incl. Acctivate and Megaventory](https://blog.megaventory.com/blind-count-definition-pros-cons-examples/)
- Where inventory is held at multiple locations, the auditor must decide which locations to attend based on materiality and risk assessment, and if attendance at a given location is impracticable, must perform alternative procedures to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence on existence and condition — [WebSearch summary of ISA 501](https://pasai.squarespace.com/s/isa-501.pdf)

### Inferences
- Good software support for the count-observation procedure would give the auditor their own recording surface, separate from the client's count sheets: a place to log each test count (item, location, quantity counted by the auditor, direction — floor-to-sheet or sheet-to-floor — and any discrepancy versus the client's count sheet), time-stamped and ideally photo-evidenced, that is never merged into or overwritten by the client's own inventory records.
- Cut-off should be captured as a first-class, contemporaneous data point at the count (e.g., "last goods-received note #____ dated ____" and "last despatch note #____ dated ____," recorded by the auditor on the day), not retrieved later from the client by email — this is exactly the deficiency the ciferi commentary calls out as the most common inspection finding on inventory files.
- Blind-count support (hiding the book/system quantity from the person keying in the physical count, revealing it only after entry) is a feature a count-observation app should offer as a configurable mode, since it materially strengthens the reliability of the client's own count — and separately, the auditor's own test counts should always be entered blind relative to the count sheet's quantity, to avoid the auditor's number being anchored by what the sheet already says.
- A multi-location inventory system should let the audit team flag which locations were attended/observed (vs. not attended) and why, tying that decision to a documented materiality/risk rationale, since ISA 501 requires that judgment to be made and (implicitly) evidenced.

### Gaps
- No vendor-specific "inventory count observation" audit-app feature set (e.g., a named module in CaseWare, Wolters Kluwer, or a standalone inventory-count app built specifically for auditors) was found in this research window; the search budget was spent confirming the ISA 501 procedural requirements and the general blind-count concept rather than surveying named commercial count-observation tools. This is a genuine gap — the report writer should flag that concrete "software offers X" claims for the count-observation procedure specifically (as opposed to confirmations and AJEs, where named vendors were found) are thin, and any product claims here should be treated as inferred design recommendations rather than documented features of a named tool.
- Did not find primary ISA 501 text (clause numbers beyond .4) due to the same PDF-extraction issue noted in the confirmations section; the procedural bullet list above is sourced from a secondary summary (ciferi) rather than directly quoted IAASB clause text.

## How are proposed adjustments recorded, statused (proposed/accepted/passed/rejected), and summarised for uncorrected misstatements?

### Takeaway
ISA 450 requires the auditor to accumulate all identified misstatements (factual, judgmental, or projected) other than those that are "clearly trivial" — a much smaller threshold than materiality — communicate them to management for correction, and if management declines to correct some or all, summarize the uncorrected ones and evaluate materiality before forming the opinion; dedicated audit/working-paper software (CaseWare) models this as a distinct proposed-adjustment ledger with a "Summary of Misstatements" that only pushes a subset of entries into the client's real books, whereas mainstream accounting apps (QuickBooks Online Accountant, Xero, Sage Intacct) only support directly posting a (optionally "adjusting"-tagged) journal entry to the live ledger — they have no built-in staged proposed/accepted/passed/rejected disposition workflow or automatic uncorrected-misstatement summary.

### Cited Findings
- ISA 450 governs "the auditor's responsibility to evaluate the effect of identified misstatements on the audit and of uncorrected misstatements, if any, on the financial statements," and under ISA 450.12 the auditor must determine whether uncorrected misstatements are material, individually or in aggregate, after reassessing materiality (ISA 320) in light of the entity's actual results — [WebSearch summary of ISA 450](https://pasai.squarespace.com/s/isa-450.pdf); [ACCA, Evaluation of misstatements](https://www.accaglobal.com/africa/en/student/exam-support-resources/professional-exams-study-resources/p7/technical-articles/misstatements.html)
- ISA 450.A2's "clearly trivial threshold" (CTT) is "not another expression for 'not material'" — it is "a wholly different (smaller) order of magnitude than materiality... clearly inconsequential whether taken individually or in aggregate." If there is any uncertainty whether an item is clearly trivial, it is not clearly trivial and must be accumulated. In practice, per ciferi's methodology commentary, CTT is often set at 3–5% of overall materiality (sometimes up to 10%, varying by firm) — [ciferi, ISA 450: Evaluating Misstatements and the Trivial Threshold](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-450-evaluation-misstatements-guide)
- ISA 450.A3–A5 distinguishes three misstatement types: factual (no doubt about amount/direction), judgmental (differences in management's estimates or accounting-policy judgment that the auditor considers unreasonable), and projected (the auditor's best estimate extrapolated from a sample) — [ciferi, ISA 450 Misstatement Accumulation](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-450-misstatement-accumulation-tracking-factual-judgmental-and-projected-misstatements)
- The auditor must communicate all accumulated (non-trivial) misstatements to management on a timely basis and request correction; if management refuses to correct some or all, the auditor must obtain an understanding of management's reasons and factor that into evaluating whether the financial statements as a whole are free of material misstatement (including considering possible management bias) — [ciferi, ISA 450 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-450-evaluation-misstatements-guide)
- Uncorrected misstatements and their effect on the opinion must be communicated to those charged with governance under ISA 260, and the auditor must document: the clearly-trivial threshold used, all misstatements accumulated during the audit and whether corrected, and the auditor's conclusion on materiality of the uncorrected balance — [WebSearch summary of ISA 450](https://pasai.squarespace.com/s/isa-450.pdf)
- The management representation letter must state management's belief that uncorrected misstatements — summarized in an attached schedule — are immaterial, individually and in the aggregate; a commonly used sentence is: "We believe that the effects of the uncorrected financial statement misstatements summarized in the accompanying schedule are immaterial, both individually and in the aggregate, to the financial statements taken as a whole." For certain federal/government audits, the Summary of Uncorrected Misstatements (SUM) is attached to the rep letter *without* the auditor's own calculations, evaluation, or conclusion, so management is representing on its own understanding of the schedule — [WebSearch summary citing OMB M-08-24 and AICPA rep-letter guidance](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/whitehouse.gov/files/omb/memoranda/2008/m08-24.pdf); [PCAOB AS 2805, Management Representations](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2805)
- CaseWare Working Papers' Adjusting Journal Entries module distinguishes entry types: only entries marked "Financial" post to the client's normal accounting/actual records; other types (e.g., "Unrecorded – Factual," used for factual misstatements not recorded due to immateriality, or general "proposed" entries) can adjust the working trial balance and be reported on separately without altering the client's real books — the working trial balance itself is not updated by non-Financial entries, though its calculations reflect them — [CaseWare documentation, Adjusting Journal Entries Interface](https://documentation.caseware.com/2018/WorkingPapers/en/Content/Accounting_and_Assurance/Adjusting_Journal_Entries/r_Adjusting_Journal_Entries_Interface.htm)
- CaseWare has a dedicated "Summary of Misstatements" / "Summary of Identified Misstatements Worksheet" document that charts all adjusting and proposed journal entries in the engagement file — functionally the working-paper equivalent of the ISA 450 uncorrected-misstatements summary — [CaseWare, Summary of misstatements](https://docs.caseware.com/2020/webapps/31/en/Engagements/Statements-and-Conclusions/Summary-of-misstatements.htm?region=ca); [CaseWare, Summary of Identified Misstatements Worksheet](https://documentation.caseware.com/2016/Audit/18_00/en_us/Content/User_Completing_and_Reporting/Completing_and_Reporting/c_Summary_of_Identified_Misstatements.htm)
- QuickBooks Online Accountant: the "Is Adjusting Journal Entry?" checkbox is available only in QuickBooks Online **Accountant** (it shows as unavailable/False on any journal entry created in ordinary QBO Simple Start, Essentials, or Plus). The accountant creates a normal journal entry, ticks that box, and can then run an "Adjusted Trial Balance Report" showing account balances before and after the adjusting entries and the total of adjustments — [QuickBooks/Intuit support, Make adjusting journal entries in QuickBooks Online Accountant](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/accountant-reports/make-adjusting-journal-entries-quickbooks-online/L1IvjNses_US_en_US)
- Xero: accountants post corrections as manual journals; the "find and recode" feature lets an accountant bulk-update transactions and leaves "a detailed audit trail of adjustments made"; a Journal Report shows all manual journals posted, useful for audit/client review — [WebSearch summary of Xero Central/community sources](https://learning.central.xero.com/student/path/4750-make-adjustments-and-find-and-correct-errors); [Xero Central, Audit adjustments](https://central.xero.com/s/question/0D53m00009T97xBCAR/audit-adjustments)
- Sage Intacct's audit trail logs every journal-entry creation, modification, approval, and deletion with a timestamp and user ID, and shows the before/after value of a changed field; best practice guidance recommends dating/versioning adjusting entries, keeping a memo explaining any reversal/repost, and attaching source documents (POs, approval emails, contracts) directly to the journal entry at the time of posting — [Truewind, Give your auditor everything from Sage Intacct](https://www.truewind.ai/blog/give-auditor-everything-sage-intacct-without-fire-drill); [Sage Intacct, Audit Trail documentation](https://www.intacct.com/ia/docs/en_US/help_action/Administration/Audit_trail/Standard/audit-trail.htm)

### Inferences
- There is a structural gap between purpose-built audit/working-paper software and general-ledger accounting apps: CaseWare (an audit tool) maintains proposed adjustments as their own object type, distinct from the client's real ledger, and only certain types ("Financial") ever flow into the actual books — everything else stays as an audit-file-only adjustment that feeds a Summary of Misstatements. QuickBooks Online Accountant, Xero, and Sage Intacct, by contrast, only offer a single mechanism — post a real journal entry to the live ledger, optionally tagged "adjusting" (QBOA) or explained in a memo/audit trail (Xero, Sage Intacct) — with no separate, reversible "proposed but not yet accepted" staging state, and no disposition workflow (accepted/rejected/passed) or automatic aggregation into an uncorrected-misstatements summary.
- A well-designed accounting app that wants to support the ISA 450 workflow natively (rather than leaving it entirely to a separate audit tool) should therefore offer: (1) a "proposed adjustment" object that is visibly distinct from a posted journal entry and does not affect the live trial balance or any financial reports until explicitly accepted; (2) a status field per proposed adjustment — at minimum proposed → accepted (booked) or passed (left unrecorded, e.g. because immaterial) or rejected (client disagrees, becomes an uncorrected misstatement the client is not accepting for a stated reason) — mirroring the "Financial" vs. non-"Financial"/"Unrecorded – Factual" distinction CaseWare already uses; (3) classification of each proposed item as factual/judgmental/projected per ISA 450.A3–A5, since that classification drives how it's aggregated and whether it can be treated as anomalous; (4) an automatically generated summary of all "passed"/uncorrected items, with each item's amount, nature, and effect, suitable for attaching to the representation letter as the SUM; and (5) a record of the reason management declined to book each rejected/passed item, since ISA 450 requires the auditor to document management's rationale.
- What such an app must **not** do: silently merge a "proposed" adjustment into the posted ledger/trial balance before it is accepted (this would misstate interim reporting and remove the audit trail distinguishing proposed from booked); let the client edit or delete a proposed adjustment's audit-file record (only the disposition — accept/pass/reject — should be a client-side action, with the underlying proposed entry and its rationale preserved); or drop "passed" (immaterial, unbooked) items from the historical record — ISA 450 requires them to remain part of the accumulated misstatements schedule even though they never hit the books.

### Gaps
- No named CaseWare source located in this research explicitly uses the three-way label set "proposed / passed / booked" as literal on-screen status values (the documentation found uses "Financial" vs. non-Financial entry types, plus a general "proposed" concept, but not a confirmed "passed" status label) — this should be treated as an inference about how such systems function rather than a verified UI/status-field name; if the report needs the exact CaseWare (or CaseWare Cloud/SmartSync) status-field wording, that requires a follow-up look at current CaseWare product documentation or a live walkthrough, which this session's search budget did not reach.
- Did not find confirmation of whether QuickBooks Online Accountant, Xero, or Sage Intacct have added (or plan to add) a genuine proposed/staged-adjustment feature since AS 2310 and modern audit-software integrations have pushed toward tighter auditor-accountant workflows; the findings above reflect current (2026) documented functionality, which appears to lack that staging concept, but this is based on product-support pages rather than an exhaustive current feature audit of each app.
