# Stock/Inventory in Mid-Market ERPs: Odoo, NetSuite, Business Central, SAP Business One

Scope: Odoo (Inventory/Purchase/MRP, v13–19), Oracle NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central (BC), SAP Business One (B1). Current as of Sept 2026; version differences noted where found.

## Tracking: warehouses, locations, bins, lots/serials, expiry, UoM, variants, goods in transit

### Takeaway
All four systems now support a three-tier physical hierarchy (warehouse → zone/aisle → bin/location), lot/serial/expiry tracking, multi-UoM conversion, and an explicit in-transit state for inter-location transfers — but they model it differently: Odoo treats *everything* (including P&L-like events) as a "stock move" between locations, including virtual ones, which is a distinctive design worth studying; NetSuite, BC, and SAP B1 keep transfers/valuation as separate transaction types layered on top of physical location records.

### Cited Findings
- **Odoo** — Locations form a hierarchy where "every location within a warehouse can serve as a parent location," including virtual location types: Virtual (organizes warehouse hierarchy, e.g., WH grouping Stock/receiving docks/quality checkpoints), Internal (counted in valuation), Customer/Vendor "Partner" locations (external, no longer in stock), Inventory Loss (counterpart for adjustments/scrap), and Transit (inter-company/inter-warehouse transfers) — [Odoo Inventory management, 18.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/warehouses_storage/inventory_management.html); [Warehouses & locations discussion](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/virtual-locations-inventory-adjustment-current-stock-273071).
- **Odoo** — A gain from an inventory count (positive difference between counted and on-hand quantity) is represented as a stock move *from* Virtual Locations/Inventory Adjustment *to* WH/Stock, i.e., adjustments are literal double-entry moves, not just a quantity edit — [Odoo forum: virtual locations/inventory adjustment](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/how-to-move-products-from-virtual-locations-inventory-adjustment-to-actual-locations-278670).
- **Odoo** — While in transit between warehouses, items sit in an "Inter-warehouse transit" location (a Transit Location type); this "does not affect the inventory valuation of the products" — [Odoo forum on transit/partner locations](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/virtual-locations-inventory-adjustment-current-stock-273071).
- **Odoo** — Storage Locations (shelf/room/aisle/rack/bin) must be enabled via Inventory → Configuration → Settings before bin-level tracking is available — [Storage Locations, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/warehouses_storage/inventory_management/use_locations.html).
- **Odoo** — Lots/serials and expiration dates are enabled per-product (Traceability section) and support three date types: Product Use Time (Best-before), Product Life Time (Expiration date), Product Removal Time (Removal date); dates auto-populate on the lot/serial at receipt based on days configured on the product — [Expiration dates, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/product_management/product_tracking/expiration_dates.html).
- **Odoo** — UoM: a product has a default (stock) UoM and a separate Purchase UoM; Odoo auto-converts between them across purchase/sales/delivery/receipt documents — [Units of measure, Odoo 18.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/product_management/configure/uom.html).
- **NetSuite** — Bin records define bin locations within a warehouse; bin types include None (default), Inbound Staging, Storage, WIP, Vendor Returns, Picking, and Outbound Staging — [Creating Bin Records](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2274082.html); [Bin Management by Location](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4713998969.html).
- **NetSuite** — With basic Bin Management + Multi-Location Inventory, bins are used in *all* locations or none; with Advanced Bin/Numbered Inventory Management, specific locations can be chosen to use bins — [Bin Management by Location](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4713998969.html).
- **NetSuite** — Transfer Orders track items in transit between two locations; after fulfillment the order is "Pending Receipt," the items are still counted in the *source* location's inventory (shown via a "Location in Transit" search column that reduces the on-hand count), and value moves from Inventory Asset (source) to an Inventory in Transit account until receipt — [Transfer Order](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N3692380.html); [Inventory Transfer Orders](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2308766.html).
- **NetSuite** — Matrix items (parent + subitems) track SKU-level combinations (e.g., size/color) separately; Multiple Units of Measure lets an item be purchased in one unit (pallets), stocked in another (spools), and sold in a third (feet) via a defined "Units Type" — [Matrix Items](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2227654.html); [Setting Up Units of Measure](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2212143.html).
- **Business Central** — Warehouse structure is three levels: Location (top, a physical warehouse/showroom/service car/plant) → Zone (enabled only when "Directed Put-away and Pick" is turned on for the location) → Bin (most granular; default bins define starting/ending placements) — [Locations, Zones and Bins — usedynamics.com](https://usedynamics.com/business-central/inventory/locations-zones-and-bins-structure/); [Set up a location card and define transfer routes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-setup-locations).
- **Business Central** — Transfer Orders can post with or without an In-Transit Location; the In-Transit Location "holds the cost of the item while it is being moved" and belongs to neither the shipping nor receiving location until receipt is posted; it must be flagged "Use as In-Transit Location" on its Location card and added to Inventory Posting Setup or the transfer fails — [Transfer Orders, Transfer Routes and In-Transit — Probitas](https://probitas.co.uk/2022/03/11/business-central-transfer-orders-transfer-routes-and-in-transit/); [Transfer items between warehouse locations](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-transfer-between-locations).
- **Business Central** — Item Tracking Codes (assigned per item) define how serial numbers, lot numbers, expiration dates, and warranty dates are handled; "Strict Expiration Posting" prevents consumption/sale of expired inventory outright — [Track items with serial, lot, and package numbers](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-work-item-tracking); [Set up item tracking](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-setup-item-tracking).
- **SAP Business One** — Bin locations use up to four configurable sublevel dimensions (e.g., `1-ZONE2-AREA3-ROW1-BIN3`); Sublevel 1 is mandatory once bin locations are enabled; bins can be flagged "Receiving" (temporary/inspection) vs. ordinary storage — [Bin Locations, SAP B1 10.0](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_BUSINESS_ONE/68a2e87fb29941b5bf959a184d9c6727/ad4f233a7b864c7cbe2b57ad09246adb.html); [Bin Location Setup — Vision33](https://blog.vision33.com/how-to-create-bin-locations-in-sap-business-one-for-a-more-efficient-warehouse).
- **SAP Business One** — Serial/batch management is set per item via a "Management Method" field: "On Every Transaction" (must assign at receipt and again at every issue) vs. "On Release Only" (no assignment required at receipt, mandatory only when the item leaves the warehouse); the setting cannot be changed while the item still has on-hand inventory — [Defining Items Managed by Serial Numbers or Batches](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_BUSINESS_ONE/21a663bcb9b2414e8ef39e9c39c22694/af38d3455b1a43ed8e99a8dcc942a9b1.html); [How to Set Up and Use Serial/Batch Valuation, SAP B1 10.0 PDF](https://help.sap.com/doc/how-to-set-up-and-use-serialbatch-valuation-method-in-sap-business-one-100/10.0/en-US/How%20to%20Set%20Up%20and%20Use%20Serial_Batch%20Valuation%20Method%20in%20Release%2010.0.pdf).
- **SAP Business One** — Goods-in-transit between company warehouses is handled via a two-document model: a non-posting "Inventory Transfer Request" (commits the move, no financial/inventory effect) followed by the actual "Inventory Transfer" document that posts the debit/credit between warehouse stock accounts — [Stock transfer document](https://sap-b1-blog.com/en/glossary/stock-transfer-document/); [Warehouse Transfer Posting](https://sap-b1-blog.com/en/glossary/stock-transfer-sap-business-one-2/).

### Inferences
- Odoo's choice to make *every* stock event — including accounting-adjustment-like events (counts, scrap) — a move between two locations (one of which may be virtual) is architecturally distinctive versus the other three, which keep "movement" (locations) and "adjustment/valuation" (journal-style) as separate concepts. This uniformity is powerful for traceability/audit (one ledger of moves explains all quantity changes) but adds conceptual overhead for end users who must learn virtual location semantics.
- All four vendors converge on the same three-level physical hierarchy (warehouse/zone/bin) and the same "in-transit is a distinct place, not a state flag" pattern for inter-location transfers — this is a strong signal that a small-business product should model transfers with an explicit in-transit bucket rather than a boolean "in transit" flag on a single row.

### Gaps
- Did not find a primary-source, quantified comparison of how many hierarchy levels each system allows (e.g., max sublevels) beyond SAP B1's stated "4 sublevel dimensions"; Odoo/NetSuite/BC hierarchy depth limits were not explicitly stated in sources found.

## Costing: methods, granularity, valuation timing, landed costs, revaluation, back-dated adjustment, negative inventory

### Takeaway
Standard / moving-average / FIFO are the common three (NetSuite also documents "Standard" with fixed default cost, "Average," and "FIFO"; SAP B1 supports Moving Average, FIFO, and Standard; Odoo the same three), each system lets valuation method be set at a grouping level above the individual item (Odoo: product category; NetSuite/BC/SAP B1: per item, with NetSuite locking the method after save), and all four separate *when* a transaction's true cost is known from *when* it is recorded, requiring a dedicated back-dated cost-correction batch process (BC's "Adjust Cost – Item Entries," NetSuite's cost recalculation on backdated revaluation, Odoo's valuation-layer recycling for negative stock).

### Cited Findings
- **Odoo** — Three costing methods: Standard Price (manually fixed, ignores actual purchase price), AVCO (weighted average recalculated on each receipt — e.g., after 8 units@$10 + 4@$16, unit cost becomes $12), and FIFO (oldest layers consumed first; a delivery of 10 after receiving 8@$10 and 4@$16 leaves 2 units valued at $16) — [Valuation cheat sheet, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/inventory_valuation/cheat_sheet.html).
- **Odoo** — Costing method and valuation type (Manual vs. Automated) are configured on the **Product Category**, not the individual product or the company as a whole; company-level "Automatic Accounting" must also be enabled in Accounting settings — [Automatic inventory valuation, Odoo 18.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/product_management/inventory_valuation/inventory_valuation_config.html).
- **Odoo** — Manual valuation = periodic (accountants post journal entries from physical counts; vendor bills post as expense, reconciled via a closing entry / stock variation account); Automated valuation = perpetual (real-time journal entries on every stock move; vendor bills post as an asset, expensed to COGS on delivery). Since Odoo 19, perpetual accounting "impacts the stock valuation account at invoice level rather than at each movement" — [Valuation cheat sheet, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/inventory_valuation/cheat_sheet.html); regional default note: "European companies typically use periodic methods, while Anglo-Saxon accounting (US, India) favors perpetual" (same source).
- **Odoo** — Landed Costs can only be applied to products using FIFO or AVCO with Automated valuation — Standard Price is excluded because its unit cost is fixed manually — [Landed costs, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/inventory_valuation/landed_costs.html).
- **Odoo** — Five landed-cost split methods: **Equal** (per product line regardless of quantity), **By Quantity** (per unit — e.g., inspection fees), **By Current Cost** (proportional to each product's value — e.g., insurance), **By Weight** (freight), **By Volume** (container-space allocation); adjustment amounts can be manually edited before validating — [Landed Cost Splitting method forum](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/landed-cost-splitting-method-199354); [Landed costs, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/inventory_valuation/landed_costs.html).
- **Odoo** — Negative stock is *allowed by default*; rationale given is operational (e.g., cross-docking where outbound must be processed before an inbound receipt is validated). Under automated FIFO/AVCO valuation, when quantity goes negative Odoo "cycles through all valuation layers... adjusting pending adjustment layers with future candidate layers until the adjustment quantity is completed" — meaning cost gets retroactively corrected once real receipts land, but in the interim valuation/journal entries can be inconsistent — [Odoo forum: negative stock](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/negative-balances-in-inventory-why-are-they-allowed-how-can-i-prevent-them-293539); [ERPgap: New Stock Valuation in Odoo 19](https://www.erpgap.com/blog/odoo-19-stock-valuation-use-cases/).
- **NetSuite** — Average costing (a "moving average" recalculated continuously) is the *default* method; FIFO maintains cost layers by receipt date/lot; Standard Cost uses only the item's Default Cost field and explicitly ignores purchase price, last purchase price, average cost, and landed cost — [Costing Methods, NetSuite docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2191818.html); [NetSuite Inventory Costing — Houseblend](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/netsuite-inventory-costing-methods).
- **NetSuite** — Costing method is set on the item record and **cannot be changed after the record is saved** — [Costing Methods, NetSuite docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2191818.html).
- **NetSuite** — Landed Cost Categories are each tied to a holding expense account; allocation method (Weight, Quantity, or Value) is chosen per category — e.g., freight typically allocated by Weight, insurance by Value; Landed Cost Templates bundle multiple categories with their own allocation method/cost factor for reuse on transactions — [Landed Cost Categories](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2417902.html); [Creating Landed Cost Templates](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4576805263.html).
- **NetSuite** — Inventory Cost Revaluation (Transactions > Accounting > Revalue Inventory Cost) sets a new standard cost effective a chosen date; on-hand value = new standard cost × current qty on hand, variance posts to a chosen Adjustment Account; an option exists to cascade revaluation to assemblies based on component costs. Backdating a revaluation "triggers a cost recalculation that runs for an extended period," and NetSuite's own guidance is to avoid backdating when possible — [Revalue Standard Cost Inventory](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2205401.html); [Revalue Standard Cost Inventory and Backdate](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/bridgehead_4337969765.html).
- **NetSuite** — Negative inventory is not a deliberate cost-method choice but a validation setting: "Prevent Negative Inventory" (Setup > Accounting > Accounting Preferences, Order Management tab, Enhanced Validations section) blocks transactions that would take on-hand quantity below zero across locations; documentation frames actual negative inventory as usually arising from workflow issues (e.g., posting shipments before receipts) rather than something to allow by design — [Enabling Negative Inventory Validation](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_1515570529.html); [Reviewing Negative Inventory](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2268458.html).
- **Business Central** — FIFO: unit cost = actual value of a specific receipt, assumption that first items in are first sold; Average: unit cost recalculated as the average at each point in time after a purchase, assumption that all inventory is sold simultaneously (i.e., BC's "Average" is closer to a periodic weighted-average than a running moving-average) — [Inventory Costing Methods — d365training](https://d365training.com/blog/inventory-costing-methods-in-business-central-which-one-to-choose/).
- **Business Central** — "Adjust Cost – Item Entries" is a batch job that reconciles cost once real numbers (e.g., a vendor invoice landing after the item already shipped) arrive; original entries are left untouched, new adjustment value entries are created with the correction amount. This is necessary because BC "posts inventory transactions in real time using the best cost information available," and average costing means "every new purchase changes the running average," so items that shipped before the average updated still carry the old cost until the batch runs — [Manually adjust the costs of items](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-adjust-item-costs); [Adjust Cost – Item Entries: What it is and Why You Need It](https://www.speakingbusinesscentral.com/post/adjust-cost-item-entries-what-it-is-and-why-you-need-it).
- **Business Central** — Standard cost changes go through a Standard Cost Worksheet: enter the new standard cost per item, then "Implement Standard Cost Changes," which updates the item card's Standard Cost field *and* automatically creates Revaluation Journal lines for on-hand inventory as of the effective date — [Rolling Your Standard Costs in BC — ERP Software Blog](https://erpsoftwareblog.com/2025/06/rolling-your-standard-costs-in-dynamics-365-business-central/); [Create new value entries for items in the inventory](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-revalue-inventory).
- **Business Central** — Revaluation posting-date rules: should reflect the date of the underlying adjustment; cannot post into a prior period if Inventory Periods are closed for that period — [How Revaluation Works in BC Simplified](https://cynthiapriebe.com/how-revaluation-works-in-business-central-simplified/).
- **SAP Business One** — Three valuation methods: Moving Average (recalculated on every receipt: total value ÷ quantity in stock), FIFO (each receipt is a separate cost "layer," oldest consumed first — useful for expiring goods), Standard Cost (fixed price, adjusted only via revaluation) — [Valuation Methods, SAP B1 training](https://sap-ds.com/training/sap-business-one/logistics/items-and-inventory-management/valuation-methods); [Exploring the Inventory Valuation Methods, SAP Learning](https://learning.sap.com/courses/managing-logistics-in-sap-business-one/exploring-the-inventory-valuation-methods-in-sap-business-one).
- **SAP Business One** — Valuation method applies "if you are using perpetual inventory" and is chosen "for any given item," implying B1 also supports a non-perpetual/periodic posting mode as an alternative — [Valuation methods PDF, help.sap.com](https://help.sap.com/doc/saphelpiis_hc_b1_image_repository_consultant_training_basic_b1_90_tb1000_04_04_pdf/9.0/en-US/B1_90_TB1000_04_04.pdf).
- **SAP Business One** — Negative inventory control is a document setting with three scopes: Block Negative Inventory **By Company** (blocks only when the item's *total* quantity across all warehouses would go below 0), **By Warehouse** (blocks if any single warehouse would go negative), or **By Item Setting** (deferred to the item's own "Manage Inventory by Warehouse" flag) — configured at Administration → System Initialization → Document Settings — [How to Prevent Negative Inventory in SAP Business One](https://www.sap-business-one-tips.com/en/negative-inventory-in-sap-business-one/).

### Inferences
- The three costing methods (standard/average/FIFO) are now table stakes; the differentiator for a small-business product is less "which methods" and more (a) at what level the method is set (Odoo's category-level default is more scalable for catalogs with hundreds of SKUs than NetSuite/BC/SAP B1's per-item setting, which requires bulk tooling to change en masse) and (b) whether the system ships a first-class "cost catch-up" job. BC's Adjust Cost batch and NetSuite's revaluation-with-backdate warning both signal that *any* perpetual/average-cost system needs an explicit, schedulable reconciliation job — this is not optional plumbing, it is a core feature to design for from day one if average costing is offered.
- Landed cost allocation methods are nearly identical across Odoo and NetSuite (quantity/weight/volume/value, i.e., "pick the physical dimension that matches the cost's real driver") — this is a small, well-bounded feature a lean product could copy directly (4–5 allocation strategies) without overkill.
- Negative-inventory policy design converges on a three-tier scope choice (company-wide / per-warehouse / per-item) in SAP B1 and NetSuite's binary but similarly-scoped ("across locations") validation — worth borrowing the scoped toggle rather than a single global switch, since small businesses often want strictness on high-value SKUs only.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from primary SAP B1 docs whether landed costs have a formal multi-method allocation feature (by weight/quantity/value) analogous to Odoo/NetSuite, or whether B1 landed costs are always allocated by value/quantity only — sources found covered valuation methods and negative stock but not a dedicated SAP B1 landed-cost allocation page.
- Could not find a primary BC citation for landed cost allocation basis (BC's "Item Charges" mechanism was not directly researched); this is a gap for a full cross-system comparison of landed cost handling.

## Movements: receipts, deliveries, transfers, adjustments/cycle counts, scrap/returns, manufacturing consumption, project/job consumption

### Takeaway
Odoo's multi-step routes (1/2/3-step receipts and deliveries with dedicated Input/Quality/Output/Packing locations) are the most explicit, configurable model of physical movement found; NetSuite, BC, and SAP B1 achieve similar rigor through bin types (NetSuite), zones/bins plus warehouse activities (BC), or receiving bin locations (SAP B1) rather than a "step count" configuration. All four support ABC/cycle-count scheduling, scrap-to-loss-location, and manufacturing consumption (BOM-driven), and NetSuite/BC both explicitly support inventory consumption tied to projects/jobs.

### Cited Findings
- **Odoo** — Three-step receipt: enabling it creates WH/Input and WH/Quality Control locations; flow is Vendor → WH/Input (validate) → WH/Quality Control (validate) → WH/Stock (validate) — configured per warehouse under "Receive goods in input, then quality and then stock (3 steps)" — [Three-step receipt, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/shipping_receiving/daily_operations/receipts_three_steps.html).
- **Odoo** — Three-step delivery (Pick + Pack + Ship) creates Packing Zone and Output locations; items are picked per removal strategy, packed into shipments in the packing zone, then output/shipped; the three transfers are labeled PICK, PACK, and OUT — [Three-step delivery, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/shipping_receiving/daily_operations/delivery_three_steps.html).
- **Odoo** — Cycle counts are performed by location (requires Storage Locations enabled) via Inventory → Operations → Physical Inventory; ABC analysis classifies products by inventory-value contribution so "high-value items should be counted more frequently" — [Cycle counts, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/warehouses_storage/inventory_management/cycle_counts.html).
- **Odoo** — Scrap moves goods to a virtual "Inventory Loss" location, keeping stock counts accurate by removing unsellable goods from physical inventory — [Scrap inventory, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/warehouses_storage/inventory_management/scrap_inventory.html).
- **Odoo** — Manufacturing: a Bill of Materials defines components/quantities and can include operations (work orders) with production instructions; "Flexible Consumption" lets actual component usage deviate from the BOM quantity; work orders and components auto-populate on a Manufacturing Order from the selected BOM — [Bill of materials, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/manufacturing/basic_setup/bill_configuration.html).
- **Odoo** — Drop-shipping: enabled in Purchase app settings; product needs both "Can be Sold"/"Can be Purchased" plus the Dropship route enabled in Inventory tab; confirming a sales order with the Dropshipping route auto-generates an RFQ to the vendor, which becomes a dropship receipt linked directly to the sales order once confirmed — [Dropshipping, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/shipping_receiving/daily_operations/dropshipping.html).
- **NetSuite** — Assembly work orders read the BOM and list every component (scaled to build quantity); statuses: Planned → Released → In Process → Built → Closed; "Backflushing" automatically deducts components from inventory when the build completes, based on the BOM, without manual issue transactions — [Two Types of Assembly Work Orders](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_161538863125.html); [NetSuite Work Orders & Assemblies Guide](https://www.anchorgroup.tech/blog/netsuite-work-orders-assemblies-guide).
- **NetSuite** — Physical Inventory Worksheet lists all on-hand inventory (item, description, preferred vendor, qty on hand) for manual/electronic (scanner/RFID/barcode) counting; Inventory Count feature takes an on-hand-quantity "snapshot" at count start, and an approved count generates variances between snapshot and final entered count — [Physical Inventory Worksheet](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2354418.html); [NetSuite Applications Suite - Inventory Count](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2296970.html).
- **NetSuite** — Drop shipments never touch NetSuite inventory (no inventory impact, ship vendor→customer directly); Special Orders *are* physically received into the warehouse and reserved exclusively for the linked sales order, impacting Asset and COGS accounts on receipt. Both are enabled via Setup > Company > Enable Features > Items & Inventory, and confirming a sales order with either line type auto-generates a purchase order — [Drop Shipment and Special Order Purchases](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2409296.html); [Drop Shipping vs Special Orders — Gurus Solutions](https://gurussolutions.com/blog/drop-shipping-vs-special-orders-netsuite).
- **NetSuite** — Job Costing (requires Project Management + time tracking) ties material costs to a project via "purchase orders, receipts, and inventory issues to job"; a Budget tab on project/task records tracks cost and billing budgets at both project and task level — [Job Costing, NetSuite docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_3748436379.html); [What Is Job Costing Software?](https://www.netsuite.com/portal/resource/articles/accounting/job-costing-software.shtml).
- **Business Central** — Production BOM holds components/subassemblies; consumption is posted via a Consumption or Production Journal; "Forward flushing" posts expected material quantity automatically when the order is released (or the operation starts), "Backward flushing" posts actual consumption only once the order status reaches Finished (or the operation completes); a manual "Scrap %" field on the BOM/routing adds an expected scrap quantity to the Consumption Quantity — [Production BOM/consumption journal — multiple sources incl. Microsoft Learn "About production orders"](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/production-about-production-orders); [How to register consumption and output](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/production-how-to-register-consumption-and-output).
- **Business Central** — Drop Shipment: on a sales order line, ticking "Drop Shipment" and choosing "Create Purchase Orders" generates a linked PO; the vendor defaults from the Item Card or SKU Card; after shipping/receiving, the *sales order must be invoiced before the purchase order* can be invoiced — [Make drop shipments](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-how-drop-shipment).
- **Business Central** — Physical inventory counting has two mechanisms: Physical Inventory Journals (basic, day-one feature) and Physical Inventory Orders (used with Counting Periods for scheduled cycle counting); Counting Periods are set on the item card or SKU card per location with a per-year count frequency (e.g., slow-mover once/year, fast-mover six times/year); "Calculate per counting period" lists only items not yet counted in the current period — [Setting up Physical Inventory Counting Periods](https://usedynamics.com/business-central/inventory/physical-counting-periods/); [Count, adjust, and reclassify inventory](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-count-adjust-reclassify).
- **Business Central** — Projects/Jobs: item consumption always creates an Item Ledger Entry first (to keep physical qty accurate), which the Projects module then receives as a Project Ledger Entry of type "Usage"; warehouse receipts/put-aways can directly register item usage for a linked project; planning lines (Budget/Billable/Both) track expected vs. actual consumption — [Record Consumption or Usage of Project Resources and Items](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/projects-how-record-job-usage); [Manage project supplies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/projects-how-manage-project-supplies).
- **SAP Business One** — Cycle counting is set per item+warehouse via "Cycle" codes under Administration → Setup → Inventory → Inventory Cycles, defining a recurrence/time-frame; a "Cycle Count Recommendations" report surfaces due items, which are then pushed into Inventory Counting documents (manually entered or imported from Excel) — [How to Conduct Inventory Counting with SAP Business One (PDF)](https://help.sap.com/doc/011000358700000063402014e/9.0/en-US/HowtoConductInventory.pdf); [Inventory Cycle Counting in SAP Business One](https://www.sap-business-one-tips.com/en/inventory-cycle-on-sap-business-one/).
- **SAP Business One** — MRP calculates gross requirements top-down through BOM levels (finished good demand cascades to components), taking into account existing inventory, open POs/production orders, and planning rules per item: Order Multiple, Order Interval, Minimum Order Quantity, Lead Time, Tolerance Days; the wizard's recommendations can auto-generate production orders, inventory transfer requests, or purchase orders via the "Order Recommendation" window — [How to Configure and Use MRP in SAP Business One 9.0 (PDF)](https://help.sap.com/doc/012002523100011886292014e/9.0/en-US/B1_HTG_MRP_90.pdf).
- **SAP Business One** — A Production BOM (parent + child items) is copied onto a Production Order to start manufacturing — [Bill of Materials in SAP Business One — Medium/SkySurge](https://medium.com/@SkySurge/bill-of-materials-in-sap-business-one-4bed95ad270f).
- **SAP Business One** — Goods Receipt PO is the document that both records vendor delivery and updates inventory quantities, typically issued by warehouse staff on receiving the shipment; Blanket Agreements can be linked to Purchase Quotations, POs, Goods Receipt POs, A/P Invoices, A/P Reserve Invoices, and A/P Down Payments, with SAP B1 auto-tracking fulfillment level against the agreement on every linked document post — [Blanket Agreements, SAP B1](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_BUSINESS_ONE/68a2e87fb29941b5bf959a184d9c6727/99f34ef644104dbcb75527fbe2780ede.html); [SAP B1 In-Depth Review: Purchasing and AP Documents](https://firebearstudio.com/blog/sap-business-one-in-depth-review-purchasing-and-accounts-payable-documents.html).

### Inferences
- Odoo's configurable 1/2/3-step routes are the clearest, most teachable model of "how many physical checkpoints does a receipt/delivery pass through" found across all four systems — the other three achieve similar rigor implicitly (via bin types like NetSuite's Inbound Staging/Outbound Staging, or BC's zones) but don't expose "step count" as a single warehouse-level configuration choice. For a small-business product, Odoo's explicit N-step toggle is a strong, easily explainable pattern to borrow; SAP B1's bin-type/blanket-agreement machinery and NetSuite's Special-Order vs. Drop-Ship distinction are comparatively more enterprise-flavored (worth noting as "overkill for small business" candidates unless the target user genuinely reserves stock against custom orders).
- Forward/backward flushing (BC) and backflushing (NetSuite) are the same idea by different names — automatic component consumption tied to a manufacturing status change is a good "avoid manual consumption journals for simple assemble-to-order" pattern for a lean product, with manual consumption reserved for scrap/substitution edge cases.
- Counting-frequency-by-item-value (BC's per-item/SKU counting periods, Odoo's/NetSuite's ABC-driven cycle counts) is a repeated, low-complexity idea: tie count frequency to a simple value/velocity classification rather than building a generic scheduling engine.

### Gaps
- Did not find primary-source detail on Odoo's "returns" workflow specifically (as distinct from scrap) — the assignment asked about returns explicitly; searches surfaced scrap and cycle-count docs but not a dedicated customer/vendor returns documentation page in this pass.
- Could not verify whether SAP Business One has a native project/job costing consumption model equivalent to NetSuite Job Costing or BC Jobs (searches focused on MRP/production and did not surface a SAP B1 "Projects" module inventory-consumption page); flagged as a gap rather than assumed absent.

## Ordering: replenishment, requisitions/RFQ, PO/receipt/bill matching, backorders, drop ship, sales fulfilment

### Takeaway
Reorder-point/min-max replenishment plus a make-to-order override is standard across all four; the meaningful design differences are in *approval routing* (NetSuite's threshold-based requisition→RFQ→PO chain is the most formal; SAP B1's blanket agreements add a commitment-tracking layer; BC's newest wave-1 2026 release adds many-to-many invoice/receipt/PO line matching) and in *bill-control policy* (Odoo's binary Ordered-vs-Received-quantities policy, overridable per product, is the simplest and most transparent model found).

### Cited Findings
- **Odoo** — Two replenishment mechanisms: Reordering Rules (minimum stock level trigger; "Automatic" creates POs/MOs directly when forecast falls below minimum, "Manual" only generates suggestions in a Replenishment report for review) and Make to Order (MTO) routes (create a draft PO/MO immediately on sales order confirmation, "regardless of the current stock level") — [Replenishment, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/warehouses_storage/replenishment.html); [Reordering rules, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/warehouses_storage/replenishment/reordering_rules.html).
- **Odoo** — 3-way matching: activated in Purchase → Configuration → Settings (Invoicing section); compares PO, Vendor Bill, and Receipt; **only works when Bill Control policy is set to "Received quantities"** (the alternative policy, "Ordered quantities," creates the draft bill straight from the PO regardless of what's been received); the control policy can be overridden per-product on the Purchase tab — [Bill control policies, Odoo 18.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/purchase/manage_deals/control_bills.html).
- **NetSuite** — Full six-stage procurement cycle: Purchase Requisition → Requisition Approval → Purchase Order creation → PO Approval → Item Receipt → 3-way match with Vendor Bill; a Purchase Request differs from a Purchase Requisition in that the requester already knows vendor/price (Request) vs. needing sourcing/routing to a buyer (Requisition); an RFQ is a separate sourcing transaction for collecting vendor pricing/terms, with vendors able to respond directly via the NetSuite Vendor Center — [NetSuite Purchase Order Process: Requisition to Receipt](https://www.anchorgroup.tech/blog/netsuite-purchase-order-process); [NetSuite Purchase Requisitions for Manufacturing](https://www.netsuite.alphabold.com/netsuite-purchase-requisitions-manufacturing/).
- **NetSuite** — Approval routing is threshold-based (purchase approval limits + workflow rules route POs/requisitions to supervisors); two distinct matching workflows exist — the "Vendor Bill Approval Workflow" (2-way: PO+bill) and the "3-Way Match Vendor Bill Approval Workflow" (PO+receipt+bill), which validates quantity/price/line-item agreement per configured exception criteria — [The Complete Guide to 3-Way Matching in NetSuite](https://nanonets.com/blog/3-way-matching-netsuite/); [How Does 3-Way Matching in NetSuite Work?](https://ramp.com/blog/netsuite-3-way-match).
- **NetSuite** — Landed-cost-relevant procurement nuance: Drop Shipments have zero inventory impact; Special Orders are received into the warehouse and reserved for one sales order, hitting Asset/COGS at receipt — see Movements section above for full citation (same source covers ordering trigger: PO auto-generated on sales-order approval) — [Drop Shipment and Special Order Purchases](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2409296.html).
- **Business Central** — Reordering policies include Fixed Order Quantity (same fixed qty every trigger — steady demand), Maximum Order Quantity (caps total inventory at a max — space-constrained warehouses), and Lot-for-Lot (aggregates demand over a period into optimized orders — fluctuating demand); order modifiers (Maximum Order Quantity, Minimum Order Quantity, Order Multiple) further adjust the calculated proposal quantity — [Reordering Policies in Business Central](https://www.madaboutbc.com/2023/01/22/reordering-policies-in-business-central/); [Design details - Handling reordering policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/design-details-handling-reordering-policies).
- **Business Central** — Three-way matching validates PO, posted Purchase Receipt, and Purchase Invoice; BC will not let you invoice more than has been received ("You can invoice only the quantity already received"). 2026 Release Wave 1 (BC28) introduces a "Get Order Lines" enhancement allowing a *single invoice line* to match multiple PO lines/receipts, including partial receipts across postings and orders that haven't been fully received — a meaningfully more flexible many-to-many matching model than the simple 1:1:1 match — [Match purchase invoices to multiple order and receipt lines, Microsoft Learn 2026 Wave 1](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/release-plan/2026wave1/smb/dynamics365-business-central/match-purchase-invoices-multiple-order-receipt-lines); [Three-Way Invoice Matching in Business Central](https://d365training.com/blog/purchase-order-three-way-matching-in-business-central/).
- **Business Central** — Drop shipment process detailed above (Movements section); note the sequencing constraint that the sales order must be invoiced before its linked purchase order can be invoiced — [Make drop shipments](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-how-drop-shipment).
- **SAP Business One** — Blanket Agreements are a standing commitment (quantity or monetary value, over a time frame) to a vendor; every subsequent linked purchasing document (quotation, PO, Goods Receipt PO, A/P invoice/reserve invoice/down payment) is automatically tracked against the agreement's fulfillment level — [Blanket Agreements, SAP B1](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_BUSINESS_ONE/68a2e87fb29941b5bf959a184d9c6727/99f34ef644104dbcb75527fbe2780ede.html).
- **SAP Business One** — MRP planning parameters per item: Order Multiple, Order Interval, Minimum Order Quantity, Lead Time, Tolerance Days; MRP output recommendations can auto-generate purchase orders, production orders, or inventory transfer requests via the Order Recommendation window; MRP can also recommend inter-warehouse Stock Transfer Requests instead of new purchase orders when the total company-wide quantity is sufficient but misallocated across warehouses — [How to Configure and Use MRP in SAP Business One 9.0 (PDF)](https://help.sap.com/doc/012002523100011886292014e/9.0/en-US/B1_HTG_MRP_90.pdf); [Stock Transfer Request use case](https://wiki.produmex.name/doku.php?id=implementation:scan:transfer).
- **SAP Business One** — Employees without stock-transfer authorization can still create a (non-posting) Stock Transfer Request, which becomes a real transfer only once copied into an actual Stock Transfer document — a lightweight, permission-gated escalation path — [Stock transfer document, SAP B1 blog](https://sap-b1-blog.com/en/glossary/stock-transfer-document/).

### Inferences
- Bill-control policy design is the clearest borrowable idea in this section: Odoo's simple binary (Ordered vs. Received quantities, overridable per product) gives most of the practical benefit of 3-way matching with far less configuration surface than NetSuite's two-workflow (2-way vs. 3-way) system or BC's newly-flexible many-to-many line matching. For a small-business product, a per-product or per-vendor toggle between "bill on order" and "bill on receipt," gated by a simple "require match before payment" checkbox, likely covers 90% of the real-world need without NetSuite-level approval-threshold workflows.
- NetSuite's formal Requisition→Approval→RFQ→PO chain and SAP B1's Blanket Agreements are recognizably "mid-market-and-up" features (multi-approver routing, standing vendor commitments) — good to flag as overkill for a lean small-business tool unless the target segment specifically needs procurement governance (e.g., multi-location retail chains, not single-location shops).
- Reordering-policy vocabulary (Fixed Order Quantity / Maximum Order Quantity / Lot-for-Lot, plus order modifiers like Order Multiple and Min/Max Order Quantity) is nearly identical between BC and SAP B1's MRP parameters — this is a stable, well-tested small vocabulary (4–6 parameters) that a lean replenishment feature could adopt wholesale rather than reinventing.

### Gaps
- Did not find Odoo-specific documentation on formal purchase requisition/approval routing (Odoo's Purchase app has approval settings, but a dedicated requisition-workflow doc page comparable to NetSuite's was not retrieved in this pass) — flagged rather than assumed equivalent.
- Backorder handling (explicitly asked about) was not directly sourced for any of the four systems in this pass beyond incidental mentions (Odoo Kanban dashboard shows "number of backorders" per operation type); a dedicated backorder-policy citation is missing for all four and should be treated as a gap.

## Module design: menus, dashboards, operation types, reports, mobile/barcode, roles/permissions

### Takeaway
Odoo's Inventory Overview is a Kanban board of "Operation Types" (Receipts, Internal Transfers, Pick/Pack/Delivery, Manufacturing, etc.) each showing waiting/late/backorder counts — a genuinely reusable dashboard pattern; all four ship (or bolt on) a barcode/mobile app for warehouse-floor operations; and all four gate warehouse actions behind role/permission constructs, from Odoo's per-user access rights to BC's per-location "Warehouse Employee" default-location filter to SAP B1's group-based General Authorizations to NetSuite's dedicated WMS role set.

### Cited Findings
- **Odoo** — The Inventory Overview (Kanban view) shows one card per Operation Type (Receipt, Internal Transfers, Pick, Pack, Delivery Orders, PoS Orders, Resupply Subcontractor, Repairs, Manufacturing), each displaying counts for waiting/late/backorders; new operations of that type can be created directly from the card — [Odoo 17 Inventory Overview — Cybrosys](https://medium.com/cybrosys/an-overview-of-odoo-17-inventory-inventory-bde952599e4f); [Understanding Inventory Operation Types in Odoo 18](https://veuzconcepts.com/veuzconcepts.com/public/index.php/blog-single/inventory-operation-types-in-odoo-18).
- **Odoo** — Moves History report (Inventory → Reporting → Moves History) shows Date, Reference, Product, From (source location), To (destination location), Quantity for every completed move; separate dashboards show "Moves lines count by operation" and "Weekly Stock Moves Lines by operation" — [Moves history report, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/warehouses_storage/reporting/moves_history.html); [Inventory dashboards, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/warehouses_storage/reporting/dashboards.html).
- **Odoo** — The Barcode app processes receipts/deliveries/adjustments/transfers in real time via scanner or the Odoo mobile app; described as "hardware-agnostic as long as standard barcode formats are supported," with documented support for Android scanners, Zebra and Honeywell devices, and Bluetooth handheld scanners — [Process receipts and deliveries with barcodes, Odoo 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/barcode/operations/receipts_deliveries.html).
- **NetSuite** — NetSuite WMS ships distinct roles: Administrator (feature enablement/SuiteApp install/record management), WMS Warehouse Manager (mobile-process setup + inbound/inventory/outbound task access), WMS Mobile Operator (all mobile warehouse tasks) with specialized variants WMS Inbound Operator / WMS Outbound Operator; permissions are assigned per role via Setup > Users/Roles > Manage Roles and control access on both the NetSuite UI and the mobile app — [Warehouse Management Roles and Permissions](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_156520467974.html); [Warehouse Management Roles](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_156520478173.html).
- **NetSuite** — WMS dashboards provide "a real-time view of warehouse performance, displaying key KPIs, charts, and graphs" — [NetSuite WMS: A Specialized User Guide](https://cms.nucleusnetwork.com/urban-beat/netsuite-wms-a-specialized-user-guide-1767646448).
- **Business Central** — Each user performing warehouse activities must be set up as a "Warehouse Employee" assigned a default location (and optionally additional non-default locations); BC filters warehouse activities to that employee's default location, restricting them to acting only at their assigned location(s) — [Set Up Warehouse Employees](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/warehouse-how-to-set-up-warehouse-employees).
- **Business Central** — Permission management pattern: Entra ID security groups (e.g., "BC Warehouse," "BC Approvers") map to BC Permission Sets; adding an employee to the security group grants the linked permissions the moment they have a license, removal revokes access immediately; "indirect permissions" let a user perform an action (e.g., posting a warehouse receipt) without direct access to the underlying source document (e.g., the purchase order) — [Business Central Permission Management: Your Complete Guide](https://erpsoftwareblog.com/2025/07/business-central-permission-management-guide/).
- **SAP Business One** — General Authorizations are managed at Administration > System Initialization > Authorizations > General Authorizations, restricted to a Superuser; three access levels per function: Full Authorization, Read Only, No Authorization; a new system ships four default Authorization User Groups pre-populated for finance, sales, purchasing, and inventory-related roles, and custom groups (e.g., "warehouse") can be created so a new hire just gets assigned to the group rather than having permissions configured individually — [How to Define Authorizations in SAP Business One 10.0 (PDF)](https://help.sap.com/doc/04688cec5620478ea24be266ce0a1eda/10.0/en-US/c30f04de51bb4a6b810537a8e6a278d1.pdf); [User and General Authorizations & User Groups in SAP B1](https://www.emerging-alliance.com/user-authorizations-general-authorizations-and-user-groups-in-sap-b1/).
- **SAP Business One** — User Defaults let an admin pre-set a user's default warehouse and other document/printing preferences, reducing daily data-entry friction for warehouse-floor staff — [Search results summary re: SAP B1 authorizations](https://innormax.com/post/how-to-set-up-user-roles-and-permissions-in-sap-business-one) (general authorizations context).

### Inferences
- Odoo's Kanban "Operation Types" board (one card per movement type, with waiting/late/backorder counters, doubling as a launch point to create new operations) is the single most reusable, screenshot-ready dashboard idea across all four systems for a small-business inventory module — it compresses "what needs my attention today in the warehouse" into one glance without a separate BI/reporting layer.
- Role/permission design converges on the same underlying pattern everywhere: a small number of named role templates (Odoo's per-user access group, NetSuite's WMS role set, BC's permission-set + default-location filter, SAP B1's default Authorization User Groups) rather than a fully generic ACL matrix end users configure from scratch. For a lean product, shipping 3–5 opinionated warehouse-staff role templates (e.g., "Warehouse Picker," "Receiving Clerk," "Inventory Manager") that restrict both *screens* and *default location scope* (BC's model) is more usable than exposing a raw permission matrix.
- All four vendors treat barcode/mobile as a separate, purpose-built surface (Odoo Barcode app, NetSuite WMS Mobile, implied BC/SAP B1 mobile add-ons) rather than a responsive version of the desktop screens — reinforcing that warehouse-floor UX (scan-first, minimal fields, offline-tolerant) is a distinct design problem from the back-office inventory screens.

### Gaps
- Could not find a primary Microsoft Learn or SAP help.sap.com page specifically describing a native Business Central or SAP Business One barcode/mobile scanning app comparable to Odoo Barcode or NetSuite WMS Mobile in the searches run; both ecosystems appear to rely more heavily on third-party ISV mobile/WMS add-ons (e.g., the "l-mobile" SAP B1 warehouse app referenced under Movements), which is itself a notable design difference (build vs. rely-on-partner-ecosystem) worth flagging to the product team, but not confirmed as an absolute absence — treat as unconfirmed rather than "BC/SAP B1 have no barcode app."
- Did not source a dedicated "stock valuation report" or "inventory aging report" page for NetSuite, BC, or SAP B1 to compare against Odoo's documented Stock Valuation dashboard and Aging report; only Odoo's reporting suite was directly confirmed in this pass.
