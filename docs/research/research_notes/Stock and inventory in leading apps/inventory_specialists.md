# Stock/Inventory in Inventory-Specialist SaaS Apps (Cin7, Unleashed, Katana, Sortly, Construction Materials)

Scope note: Cin7 has two distinct products — **Cin7 Core** (formerly DEAR Systems; turnkey, self-serve, SMB-oriented) and **Cin7 Omni** (the original omnichannel/EDI platform; vendor-managed, enterprise, 700+ integrations) — [Cin7 Core or Omni: Which One Is Right for Your Business?](https://www.cin7.com/blog/cin7-core-or-omni-which-one-is-right-for-your-business/); [Compare Cin7 Core vs Cin7 Omni (G2)](https://www.g2.com/compare/cin7-core-vs-cin7-omni). Findings below are labeled Core vs Omni where the source distinguishes them.

## Tracking: locations/bins, batches/serials/expiry, units, in-transit, on-hand vs available vs allocated vs on-order

### Takeaway
All four specialist apps model stock status as a set of distinct quantities (on hand, allocated/committed, available, on order/expected) rather than a single number, and support batch/serial tracking and unit-of-measure conversions — capabilities general ledgers don't attempt. Katana's formula and Cin7's status definitions are the most explicit and directly sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Cin7 Core defines: **Available** = On Hand minus Allocated; **On Order** = quantity on authorized purchase orders not yet received; **Allocated** = quantity committed to authorized (but not yet shipped) sales orders — [Calculation of Available, On Hand and Allocated Stock quantity](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/12429786547983-Calculation-of-Available-On-Hand-and-Allocated-Stock-quantity)
- Cin7 Core's dashboard shows backordered/reordered products, the supplier being reordered from, and total quantity available + on order — [Using the Dashboard – Cin7 Core](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034604706191-Using-the-Dashboard)
- Katana's stock terms: **In stock** = physical quantity in warehouse; **Committed** = quantity allocated to open sales orders, manufacturing orders (MOs), or outsourced POs; **Expected** = quantity anticipated from open POs/MOs/outsourced POs — [Basics of the Stock screen](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/articles/5966127-basics-of-the-stock-screen); [Sales item availability](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/articles/5914289-sales-item-availability)
- Katana's available-stock formula: **In stock − Committed + Expected − safety stock level**; a negative result signals a need to create a new MO or PO; higher-priority sales orders reserve available stock ahead of lower-priority ones, which then show as "Expected" or "Not available" — [Sales item availability](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/articles/5914289-sales-item-availability)
- Cin7 Core/Omni WMS supports batch and serial-number entry at pick/pack: users scan or manually enter the batch/SN, and the number is validated against actual stock on hand — [Serial numbers and batch numbers in Cin7 Core WMS](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034555914383-Serial-numbers-and-batch-numbers-in-Cin7-Core-WMS)
- Unleashed's batch/lot tracking assigns numbers at goods receipt, production, or stock adjustment, capturing expiry dates, suppliers, and batch attributes, and monitors batch movement in real time across warehouses/locations/transactions, with full usage history from purchase through sale — [Batch Inventory Management & Lot Tracking](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/product/inventory-management-software/batch-tracking/); [Disassembly - Unleashed Software](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/product/bill-of-materials-software/disassembly/)
- Units of measure: Cin7 Core supports "Additional Units of Measure" to convert, e.g., a product purchased in kg into lb for sale — [Additional Units of Measure – Cin7 Core](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034463961231-Additional-Units-of-Measure)
- Sortly organizes stock into custom nested folders/subfolders that map to physical locations (warehouses, trucks, job sites) and lets staff scan items in/out to keep them in the correct location — [Barcode Inventory Software | Sortly](https://www.sortly.com/barcode-inventory-system/)
- Sortly's barcode/QR system covers the full label lifecycle: scan-and-link existing manufacturer barcodes, or generate/print new QR/barcode labels in sizes from 1"×1" to 8.5"×5.5", with bulk printing by folder or search — [Sortly Review 2026 sourced feature summary](https://bulbthings.com/blog/sortly-review); [QR Code Inventory Management | Sortly](https://www.sortly.com/features/qr-coding-for-inventory-management/)
- Unleashed's mobile Warehouse Management app scans barcodes/QR codes at SKU level to pick, pack, receive, put away, check stock, record bin transfers, and run assembly picking, with offline mode that syncs once connectivity returns — [Warehouse Management App for Small Business | Unleashed](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/warehouse-management-app/)

### Inferences
- Katana's "Expected" bucket effectively functions as an in-transit/incoming indicator sourced from open POs, MOs, and outsourced POs combined, rather than a separate in-transit ledger state — inferred from the Committed/Expected definitions above.
- None of the four sources explicitly documents a dedicated "in-transit" stock status distinct from "on order" (Cin7) or "expected" (Katana); in Cin7/Omni, stock moving between warehouse locations via a transfer order appears to sit in a transfer-in-progress state rather than a named in-transit ledger bucket (see Movements section on two-step transfers).

### Gaps
- No sourced detail found on Sortly's or Katana's explicit "in-transit" stock state (as opposed to on-order/expected) or on cross-warehouse in-transit valuation for either.
- Expiry-date/FEFO-specific UI mechanics (e.g., pick priority displays) were not independently found for Katana or Sortly within the search budget.

## Costing: FIFO/FEFO/average/specific, landed costs, journal posting, back-dating

### Takeaway
Cin7 Core computes COGS using true FIFO/FEFO (actual lot cost) and posts detailed COGS journals per authorized shipment, explicitly noting that "average cost" is a reporting figure only and never drives P&L — a meaningfully different (and more accurate) model than Xero's own single weighted-average method. Unleashed instead runs on weighted-average (or FIFO, chosen once and unchangeable) and syncs stock value to specific Xero/QuickBooks accounts in real time. Katana automates BOM-driven manufacturing cost roll-up but has a materially weaker Xero sync (manual journal entries) versus its QuickBooks sync (more automated).

### Cited Findings
- Cin7 Core: all COGS calculations use accounting costing methods (FIFO or FEFO) based on the actual purchase cost of the picked item; "Average Cost" (Value on Hand ÷ Quantity on Hand, including landed costs) does **not** affect the P&L and is not used for COGS at all — [Average Cost – Cin7 Core](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034464211343-Average-Cost)
- Cin7 Core posts journals to Xero: on authorization of the sale/shipping tab, a single COGS journal is created from the PO invoice, manual journal tab, and any already-allocated landed costs; if landed costs are added **after** the item shipped, a second COGS journal is created later (i.e., back-dated/retroactive cost corrections are supported via a follow-up journal) — [An Introduction to Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) – Cin7 Core](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9982508174223-An-Introduction-to-Cost-of-Goods-Sold-COGS)
- Xero's own inventory feature supports only the weighted-average cost method; FIFO, LIFO, and standard cost are not natively available in Xero — implying that Cin7 Core's FIFO/FEFO costing must be computed by Cin7 and delivered to Xero as journal totals rather than left to Xero's engine — [Xero vs QuickBooks: 7 Gaps That Hurt Inventory-Heavy Teams](https://vncglobalgroup.com/xero-vs-quickbooks-inventory/)
- Cin7 Core landed-cost allocation methods: proportional to the cost of each purchase line, proportional to quantity/weight/volume of the purchased products (weight allocation requires product weight/dimensions to be configured), or fully manual distribution — [Landed cost expense distribution](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034516716047-Landed-cost-expense-distribution); [Landed costs for purchases](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/10553625549711-Landed-costs-for-purchases)
- Unleashed: users must choose FIFO or weighted-average costing **before** connecting the accounting integration; the method cannot be changed later once transactions are recorded — [Unleashed for Xero | Digit](https://digit.business/apps/unleashed); note this source also flags that some competitors like DEAR (now Cin7 Core) use FIFO while Unleashed's default is average cost.
- Unleashed–Xero setup requires specifying which Xero accounts map to sales, COGS, and stock-on-hand; the integration updates stock movements/transactions in real time; if Xero's own inventory tracking is left on for the same items it will conflict, so Unleashed should be the single source of truth and Xero inventory tracking disabled for those items — [Unleashed Software Xero Integration – Xero App Store](https://apps.xero.com/us/app/unleashed-software)
- Katana: manufacturing costs are calculated automatically from the bill of materials (BOM) and production operations; completing a production order auto-deducts raw materials per the BOM — [Production management - Katana MRP](https://katanamrp.com/features/production-management/); [Manufacturing orders | Katana](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/articles/5914371-manufacturing-orders)
- Katana–QuickBooks: purchase orders can post to QuickBooks as bills or expenses; sales orders post as invoices, linkable to multiple income accounts by item category — more automated sync than the Xero path — [QuickBooks MRP for Tracking Production and Finances — Katana](https://katanamrp.com/integrations/quickbooks/quickbooks-mrp/)
- Katana–Xero: sales orders can be sent as invoices and purchase orders as bills with one click, but Xero sync does **not** automatically sync stock levels; users must record manual journal entries periodically using Katana's real-time stock data to keep Xero's inventory value current — [How to "sync" stock to Xero from Katana | Katana](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/articles/5968138-how-to-sync-stock-to-xero-from-katana); [Xero FAQs | Katana](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/articles/5968135-xero-faqs)
- Unleashed supplier returns: a Supplier Return references the original PO and can either (a) remove the "Return Qty" from stock on hand and process a supplier credit/refund, or (b) if no credit is due, use a **Write-off** to retain the cost as a business expense — accounting handled automatically, flowing through to Xero/QuickBooks as automatic journal entries — [How to process a Supplier Return](https://support.unleashedsoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038424952-How-to-process-a-Supplier-Return); [Can you write off stock through a Stock Adjustment?](https://support.unleashedsoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/900004617463-Can-you-write-off-stock-through-a-Stock-Adjustment)

### Inferences
- The Cin7 Core vs Unleashed contrast (real FIFO/FEFO with retroactive-landed-cost journal correction vs. locked-in-advance average/FIFO with real-time but simpler sync) suggests Cin7 Core targets businesses with more complex, cost-sensitive inventory (perishables, import/landed cost, multi-batch) while Unleashed optimizes for simplicity and real-time bookkeeping accuracy.
- Katana's weaker Xero sync (manual journals) versus QuickBooks (automated bills/invoices) is a concrete example of a specialist app's own limitation depending on which accounting app it's paired with — relevant since the research brief specifically asks how these apps sync to Xero/QuickBooks.

### Gaps
- No sourced detail on Katana's own costing method name (FIFO/average/specific) beyond "BOM-driven automated cost roll-up" — the specific inventory valuation method Katana applies to raw materials was not found within the search budget.
- Sortly is a lightweight, non-accounting-costing tool; no FIFO/FEFO/average costing or landed-cost feature was found for it, consistent with its "simple mobile-first inventory" positioning — treated as a gap rather than assumed.
- Detail on whether Cin7 Core posts COGS journals as one summary line vs. line-by-line detail per SKU was not confirmed beyond "a single COGS journal will be created" per shipment — granularity of that journal (summarized vs. itemized) is unconfirmed.

## Movements: transfers, adjustments/stocktakes, assemblies/disassemblies, production, returns/write-offs

### Takeaway
Two-step (pick-then-receive) stock transfers, batch/serial-aware stocktakes with variance reporting, and BOM-driven assembly/disassembly are standard across Cin7 and Unleashed; Katana's production orders auto-consume materials on completion. Cycle counts (partial, frequent) are treated as distinct from full stocktakes across sources.

### Cited Findings
- Cin7 Core/Omni WMS stock transfers: picking from an existing transfer order generates a **new** transfer order (new document number) reflecting only the items actually picked and their from/to locations; the original order is updated to remove picked items and is voided once fully picked — both old and new orders are cross-linked in the Activity log for traceability (i.e., shortfalls-on-receipt are handled by splitting into a second transfer order rather than force-completing) — [Track stock movements – Cin7 Omni Help Center](https://help.omni.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9128580750607-Track-stock-movements)
- Cin7 Core: costs can also be allocated to stock transfers and adjustments (not just purchases) — [Allocate costs to stock transfers and adjustments](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/10553608889871-Allocate-costs-to-stock-transfers-and-adjustments)
- Unleashed: warehouse transfers move stock between multiple locations; the mobile Warehouse Management app supports "Bin Transfers" directly from a handheld scanner — [Bin Transfers Made Easy in the Unleashed Warehouse Management App](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/academy/bin-transfers-made-easy-in-the-unleashed-warehouse-management-app/)
- Cycle counting vs. full stocktake: cycle counts deal with only a portion of total inventory at a time and occur more frequently (often weekly), letting the business keep operating during the count, unlike a full stocktake — [Guide to Cycle Counting & Stock Taking in Inventory | Unleashed](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/inventory-management-guide/cycle-count-inventory/)
- Stocktake variance handling: printing "variances" shows the gap between recorded stock on hand and the counted quantity; adjustments post to two accounts — the item's default stock nominal account and a designated stock **Variance Account** — and the stock item history logs a Movement Type of "Stocktake" — [Best Practices for Stocktaking That Reduce Errors](https://onebusinesserp.com/best-practices-for-stocktaking/) (Sage-sourced mechanic, cited as representative of the category's approach; flagged as not Unleashed/Cin7-specific — see Gaps)
- Unleashed batch/serial-tracked items have dedicated stock-adjustment and stocktake workflows (per-batch/serial number) — [Batch/Serial: Stock Adjustment & Stock Take - Unleashed Software](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/academy-portal/video/batchserial-stock-adjustment-stock-take)
- Unleashed disassembly: breaks assembled products back into component parts and returns usable inventory to stock, recovering value locked in finished goods; the Disassemblies page tracks bulk-bought products divided into components, showing quantities available and yield per disassembly; serial/batch tracking applies throughout the disassembly process — [Disassembly - Unleashed Software](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/product/bill-of-materials-software/disassembly/); [Add Disassembly and Auto Disassembly](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/academy-portal/video/disassembly-auto-disassembly)
- Katana manufacturing orders (MOs): manage production of products from raw materials/subassemblies; BOMs manage assemblies, schedule operations, and monitor material consumption as work progresses; on MO completion, raw materials are deducted automatically per the BOM — [Manufacturing orders | Katana](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/articles/5914371-manufacturing-orders); [How BOM and MRP Work Together — Katana](https://katanamrp.com/blog/bom-in-material-requirements-planning-mrp/)
- Cin7 Core purchase-order backorders/splits: partially received or partially invoiced advanced purchases are flagged with a yellow status icon (vs. green for completed/authorized); Advanced Purchase allows partial invoicing and receipt of items while remaining part of the same PO, and supports multiple credit notes against a single PO — [Advanced Purchase - Partial Orders/Deliveries](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034446632975-Advanced-Purchase-Partial-Orders-Deliveries); [Purchases Views and Order Status Values](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034557801871-Purchases-Views-and-Order-Status-Values); [Backorders and Split Orders](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034480522511-Backorders-and-Split-Orders)
- Unleashed supplier returns and write-offs (see Costing section above) are the "returns/write-off" movement type for purchased stock — [How to process a Supplier Return](https://support.unleashedsoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038424952-How-to-process-a-Supplier-Return)

### Inferences
- Cin7's "split into a new transfer order" pattern for shortfalls is a deliberate design choice to preserve an audit trail (both orders remain linked) rather than silently editing the original order quantity — this differs from a simpler "just receive less than sent" model.
- The generic stocktake variance-to-nominal-account mechanic (item stock account + variance account) is common enough across inventory/ERP systems that it likely represents the category norm, but it was only directly confirmed for a Sage-family product in this search pass — treat the general mechanic as representative, not as confirmed Unleashed/Cin7 posting behavior.

### Gaps
- Blind-count mechanics (i.e., whether counters can see system quantities during a physical count) were not confirmed for any specific app (Cin7, Unleashed, Katana) within the search budget — general industry sources describe blind counts as a best practice but did not confirm which vendor supports them as a toggle.
- "Freeze" behavior during a stocktake (locking transactions against counted stock) was not explicitly sourced for any of the four apps.
- Katana's own returns/write-off workflow (as distinct from Unleashed's) was not found in this pass.

## Ordering: reorder points, forecasting, PO lifecycle, partial receipts/backorders, drop ship, B2B portals

### Takeaway
Unleashed and Katana both auto-calculate reorder points/safety stock and can auto-generate purchase orders or MO/PO suggestions from demand history and lead times; Cin7 Core's PO lifecycle uses a simple open (yellow)/authorized (green) status model with first-class support for partial receipt, partial invoicing, and backorders. Unleashed's B2B portal exposes stock availability, order status, and order history directly to wholesale customers.

### Cited Findings
- Unleashed's Advanced Inventory Manager (AIM) auto-detects an optimal min–max reorder level per item based on each item's lead time and rate of use, and lets users override with custom levels per location; when stock hits the minimum, Unleashed automatically suggests or creates a purchase order and bundles items from the same supplier into a single PO to save on shipping/admin — [Purchase Order Management Software | Unleashed](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/en-us/product/purchase-order-software/)
- Unleashed demand forecasting reviews past sales history to help predict next month's or seasonal requirements, feeding the replenishment/reorder engine — [Purchase Order Management Software | Unleashed](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/en-us/product/purchase-order-software/)
- Unleashed B2B portal tracks stock availability, order status, and previous order history for wholesale/B2B customers — [Predicting B2B Reorders (context source citing Unleashed's B2B feature set)](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/en-us/product/purchase-order-software/)
- Katana reorder points: users set thresholds; Katana highlights when more materials are needed to maintain ideal inventory levels and links inventory levels to each product's BOM for MRP-based reordering — [Automated Inventory Management Software | Katana MRP](https://katanamrp.com/inventory-management-software/automated/); [Reorder Point Formula to Maximize Sales - Katana MRP](https://katanamrp.com/reorder-point-formula/)
- Katana's rules-based automation triggers reorder alerts when materials fall below set thresholds, auto-generates purchase orders, auto-deducts raw materials during production per BOM, and syncs stock levels across sales channels in real time — [Automated Inventory Management Software | Katana MRP](https://katanamrp.com/inventory-management-software/automated/)
- Katana's negative available-stock calculation (In stock − Committed + Expected − safety stock) is the trigger to create a new MO (for products) or PO (for materials) — [Sales item availability | Katana](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/articles/5914289-sales-item-availability)
- Cin7 Core PO status model: open/not-yet-authorized POs show a yellow icon (editable); authorized/completed POs show green; partially received or partially invoiced Advanced Purchases also show yellow — [Purchases Views and Order Status Values](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034557801871-Purchases-Views-and-Order-Status-Values)
- Cin7 Core Advanced Purchase supports partial invoicing/receipt as part of the same PO and multiple credit notes per PO, i.e., the draft→authorized→receive(partial)→invoice(partial) lifecycle is a native, first-class flow rather than a workaround — [Advanced Purchase - Partial Orders/Deliveries](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034446632975-Advanced-Purchase-Partial-Orders-Deliveries)
- Cin7 Omni has a dedicated "Backorder for purchases" flow separate from Core's Backorders/Split Orders mechanic — [Backorder for purchases – Cin7 Omni Help Center](https://help.omni.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9128545394831-Backorder-for-purchases); [Backorders and Split Orders – Cin7 Core](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034480522511-Backorders-and-Split-Orders)
- Unleashed drop-ship: the platform has a documented workflow for managing dropshipping orders (routing a sales order's fulfillment to a supplier who ships directly to the end customer) — [How to manage dropshipping orders in Unleashed](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/academy/how-to-manage-dropshipping-orders-in-unleashed/)

### Inferences
- Katana's tight BOM-to-MO-to-PO chain (negative availability directly suggesting the next MO or PO) is a materially different design from Cin7/Unleashed's separate reorder-point/AIM engines — Katana's forecasting is production-schedule-driven, while Unleashed's/Cin7's is closer to classic min-max replenishment on sales velocity.
- Sortly was not found to have reorder-point-driven PO generation or a B2B ordering portal in this search pass, consistent with its positioning as simple mobile-first inventory tracking rather than full procurement/ERP.

### Gaps
- Sales order → pick/pack/ship workflow specifics (as distinct from PO lifecycle) were only confirmed in outline for Cin7 (WMS pick/pack) and Unleashed (mobile WMS pick/pack/receive/putaway); no dedicated sourcing was found describing Katana's or Sortly's sales-order fulfillment pipeline in detail.
- No sourced detail found on Katana having its own B2B ordering portal (as Unleashed does).
- Cin7's drop-ship workflow specifics were not directly sourced in this pass (only Unleashed's were).

## Module/UX design: navigation, dashboards/KPIs, mobile scanning apps, roles/approvals, alerts

### Takeaway
Each specialist app ships (or sells as an add-on) a dedicated mobile scanning app distinct from its main web UI — Cin7's WMS, Unleashed's Warehouse Management app, Katana's separate Shop Floor Control and Warehouse apps, and Sortly's mobile-first core app — reflecting a UX philosophy where the phone/scanner is the primary data-entry device on the floor, not an afterthought bolted onto a desktop ERP screen.

### Cited Findings
- Cin7 Core/Omni WMS is mobile-friendly for picking, packing, racking, and dispatching from phone or tablet, and has been tested with Zebra TC57, Honeywell CT40, and ScanSKU hardware scanners as well as device-camera scanning — [Using the Cin7 Core Warehouse Management System (WMS)](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034570508687-Using-the-Cin7-Core-Warehouse-Management-System-WMS); [Cin7 Omni & the ScanSKU Barcode Scanner](https://www.scansku.com/pages/cin7-android-barcode-scanner-scansku)
- Cin7 Core dashboard lets users compare current KPI data to the previous period or previous year as percentage change, and surfaces backordered/reordered products plus available+on-order quantities — [Using the Dashboard – Cin7 Core](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034604706191-Using-the-Dashboard)
- Unleashed's separate mobile Warehouse Management app (iOS/Android, phone-first but tablet-capable) does barcode/QR scanning across Code 128, EAN-13, UPC-A, and QR formats, with offline mode and auto-sync on reconnect — [Warehouse Management App for Small Business | Unleashed](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/warehouse-management-app/)
- Katana runs **two** distinct companion apps: the **Shop Floor Control** app (operators scan ingredient/material barcodes via device camera to search/add items to a manufacturing order) and the **Warehouse** app (supports external scanners, dedicated smart-scanner devices, and — per a recent update — phone-camera scanning) — [Shop Floor and Warehouse apps | Katana](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/collections/3335413-shop-floor-and-warehouse-apps); [Scanning from the Warehouse app | Katana](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/articles/9993154-scanning-from-the-warehouse-app)
- Sortly's roles/permissions are customizable, allowing controlled team access, and low-stock alerts notify selected team members by email and in-app notification when quantities drop to a set minimum — [Sortly Review 2026 sourced feature summary](https://bulbthings.com/blog/sortly-review)
- Sortly is designed around nested folders mapped to physical storage locations (warehouses, trucks, job sites), with in-app check-in/check-out scanning — a materially simpler navigation model than Cin7/Unleashed/Katana's module-based (Sales/Purchase/Inventory/Production) navigation — [Barcode Inventory Software | Sortly](https://www.sortly.com/barcode-inventory-system/)

### Inferences
- Katana splitting scanning into two separate apps (Shop Floor Control for production-floor material use, Warehouse for receiving/stocktake/picking) suggests a UX design decision to keep the manufacturing-operator's screen focused only on BOM/production tasks, separate from warehouse logistics tasks — inferred from the distinct app names/scopes, not stated explicitly as a design rationale by Katana.
- Sortly's folder/subfolder navigation (versus module-based nav in the ERP-like tools) is the clearest UX differentiator supporting its "simple mobile-first" positioning — it maps directly to physical space rather than to an accounting-style module structure.

### Gaps
- Specific owner-facing KPI lists (e.g., named dashboard tiles like "inventory turnover," "stockout rate," "days of stock") were not found in detail for Unleashed, Katana, or Sortly — only Cin7 Core's dashboard behavior (period-over-period KPI deltas, backorder/reorder widget) was sourced with any specificity.
- Approval workflows (e.g., who must authorize a PO or stocktake variance) were not sourced with role-level specificity for any of the four apps in this pass.

## Construction/site materials: yard vs. site stock, material issued to job/cost code, transfers

### Takeaway
Construction-specific tools (Procore Materials, Fieldwire, ToolWatch, plus category entrants like Trimble Materials and Ply) organize inventory around **cost codes** and **jobs/projects** rather than SKUs-in-a-warehouse alone: every receipt, transfer, issue, return, and installation is tagged to a project/cost code so budget and field inventory move together in real time. Buildertrend's materials tooling is comparatively lighter — closer to a tracking log (what/where/for which job/who handled it) than a full inventory-costing system.
Note: Buildertrend was substituted for the requested "construction-specific material tracking tools" category alongside Procore since it was explicitly named in the assignment; deeper vendors (ToolWatch, Fieldwire, Trimble Materials) were also captured where sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Procore cost codes categorize labor, materials, equipment, and other project costs; Procore's default cost codes align with the CSI MasterFormat standard, but companies can customize a tiered Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) — [Construction Cost Codes: Best Practices and Industry Insights | Procore](https://www.procore.com/en-ca/library/construction-cost-codes); [What are Procore's default cost codes?](https://support.procore.com/faq/what-are-procores-default-cost-codes)
- Procore Materials Management tracks material status from order to installation, syncing PO lines to materials receipts and automatically flagging delivery-delay risk; field teams use mobile receiving workflows to capture quantities, storage locations, and condition at time of receipt, logging received quantities against the original purchase contract, and documenting defects/damaged or short shipments — [Track your construction materials from purchase to installation | Procore](https://www.procore.com/blog/track-your-construction-materials-from-purchase-to-installation); [Construction Material Management Software | Procore](https://www.procore.com/materials)
- Procore gives teams mobile access to instant inventory counts, storage locations, and material status so "the right materials get to the right crews," and daily-log entries (deliveries, material usage by project, equipment/tool movement) update automatically when tied to RFID/QR/IoT-based tracking, rather than requiring manual entry — [Construction Inventory Management: Track Materials, Tools & Costs | Procore](https://www.procore.com/en-gb/library/construction-inventory-management)
- General category pattern (multiple vendors, e.g., Trimble Materials, Procore Materials, Ply): field teams can receive deliveries against POs, transfer materials between locations, and issue items to jobs/cost codes directly from a mobile device, with each transaction updating job cost immediately ("finance is not discovering month-end surprises"); every receipt, issue, transfer, return, adjustment, and installation should record supplier, PO, cost code, and project — [Essential Features of Construction Inventory Management | Ply](https://www.getply.com/blog/what-s-essential-in-construction-inventory-management-software/)
- Inventory across "trucks, warehouses, trailers, and job sites" is tracked in real time, with visibility into quantities required, ordered, received, issued, installed, or available, viewable by item or by storage location — [Essential Features of Construction Inventory Management | Ply](https://www.getply.com/blog/what-s-essential-in-construction-inventory-management-software/)

## Known limitations / complaints (G2, Capterra, community)

### Takeaway
Across specialist apps, the most consistent complaint themes are: Cin7's billing complexity, support responsiveness, and sync/stability issues; Katana's shallow reporting/customization and steep tiered pricing; Sortly's unreliable barcode scanning and limited support responsiveness; and Buildertrend's clunky estimating workflows and poor data portability on cancellation.

### Cited Findings
- Cin7 holds a 4.3/5 G2 rating across 600+ reviews; most consistent complaints are billing complexity and support response time, with users reporting inventory sync errors, integration issues, system instability, lengthy onboarding, and difficulty getting prompt help; one G2 reviewer states the tech team deploys new features without rigorous QA testing, which has damaged their setup; Cin7 is also described as "functionally dense — powerful when you need it, overwhelming when you don't" — [I compared 9 Cin7 alternatives by total cost (2026)](https://wiserreview.com/blog/cin7-alternatives/) (source aggregates G2-sourced review language)
- Katana holds a 4.5/5 G2 rating with an unlimited-user pricing model praised for predictability; its interface is described as modern, uncluttered, and operator-friendly, and it holds G2 "Most Implementable"/"Fastest Implementation" (Winter 2025) badges — [Compare Cin7 Core vs Katana Cloud Inventory (Capterra)](https://www.capterra.com/compare/133038-172888/Cin7-Core-vs-Katana-MRP)
- Katana's main called-out drawbacks: limited reporting tools and lack of deep customization; recent reviews flag unusually steep price increases, feature "gatekeeping" behind higher-tier plans (a pricing gap between the $899 and $1999 packages), and gaps in deeper functionality such as true serial-number traceability, granular reporting, or bulk export to Google Sheets; a minority of reviewers note slower support response outside European business hours; one reviewer specifically wants SERVICE-type line items (not just MAKE/BUY) to add a service portion to a spare-parts quote — [Katana Cloud Inventory Reviews 2026 | G2](https://www.g2.com/products/katana-cloud-inventory/reviews) (source aggregates G2/Capterra review language)
- Sortly vs. Katana (G2 comparison): Katana Cloud Inventory reviewers highlight ease of reaching support, whereas Sortly's support is noted as less responsive; conversely, Sortly reviewers praise its ease of setup and fast onboarding of new team members — [Compare Katana Cloud Inventory vs Sortly (G2)](https://www.g2.com/compare/katana-cloud-inventory-vs-sortly)
- Sortly-specific complaints: barcode scanning is often unreliable, with issues recognizing products and linking items; users report difficulty printing, grouping, and reprinting barcodes; some features require extra fees; RFID label creation is not supported; reviewers separately want easier QR code management and warehouse mapping — [Sortly Review 2026](https://bulbthings.com/blog/sortly-review)
- Buildertrend complaints (Capterra/G2-sourced): estimating is described as slow and duplicative, with separate proposal and estimate modules causing repeated data entry across allowances and selections; users who tried to cancel found there is no simple or bulk way to download years of files, photos, proposals, and customer information (manual one-item-at-a-time export would take an unreasonable amount of time); broader criticism cites unpredictable updates, pricey subscriptions, clunky estimating and job switching, intrusive notifications, and a mobile app that "doesn't always keep up in the field" — [Honest Buildertrend Review 2026: Pros, Cons, Features, and Pricing](https://connecteam.com/reviews/buildertrend/); [Buildertrend Reviews 2026 | Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/70092/Buildertrend/reviews/)

### Inferences
- The recurring pattern that "the fastest-to-implement, most operator-friendly" tool (Katana) trades off deep reporting/customization, while the most feature-dense tool (Cin7) trades off simplicity/stability, suggests a real specialization spectrum in this category rather than one clearly superior product — worth flagging to the report writer as a genuine trade-off rather than a limitation unique to one vendor.
- Sortly's barcode-reliability complaints are notable given barcode scanning is the app's core differentiator (mobile-first, scan-driven UX) — this is a more damaging class of complaint for Sortly specifically than an equivalent reporting gap would be for an ERP-style tool.
