# Sentryfi's stock design rivals the industry leaders

Sentryfi's stock engine — an append-only ledger of moves, moving weighted average cost tied one-for-one to the Stock account, two-step transfers that hold goods "on the way" until someone confirms arrival, and stock issued to a job at cost — already matches or beats what Zoho, Xero, QuickBooks, Odoo, NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central, SAP Business One, Cin7, Unleashed, Katana, Sortly, and TallyPrime do in their base tiers, and in a few specific places it is ahead of the accounting-first apps (Zoho, Xero, QuickBooks) that small businesses most often compare it to. The core data-model choice — never store on-hand and value, always sum an immutable ledger of moves, and let "available," "committed," and "on order" be queries over that same ledger — is exactly the pattern that inventory-systems design literature holds up as the right answer, and getting it wrong is a real, user-visible bug in at least one market leader (Zoho customers have an active complaint thread about on-hand not netting off committed stock) ([Zoho community thread](https://help.zoho.com/portal/nl/community/topic/inventory-on-hand-when-entering-sales-order-does-not-net-off-committed-stock?page=17)). Sentryfi's real gaps split cleanly into two groups: things already on its own build plan (a second unit of measure, batch and expiry, counting sessions) that the research confirms are non-negotiable for its target segment, and things not yet on the plan — a lightweight three-way match, backorder and committed-stock visibility, and a formal net-realisable-value write-down — that leading systems treat as standard controls. mybooksai.com itself turned out to be a parked domain; the real product, mybooksai.app by Zetran Corporation, has a shallow and largely unverified inventory feature set, so no meaningful head-to-head with Sentryfi is possible there.

## The item and place model gets the shape right, and the depth is already on the roadmap

Every system researched, from the cheapest accounting app to the SAP-grade ERP, converges on the same three ideas Sentryfi is missing today: a second unit of measure, batch/lot tracking with expiry, and a real physical hierarchy under the warehouse. Zoho Inventory lets a business convert between a purchase unit and a sale unit (box vs. piece) once "Unit Conversion" is switched on ([Zoho UoM settings](https://www.zoho.com/us/inventory/help/settings/uom.html)), and NetSuite, Odoo, and Business Central all support a base stocking unit with separate purchase and sales units that auto-convert on every document ([NetSuite Units of Measure](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2212143.html), [Odoo UoM, 18.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/product_management/configure/uom.html)). The design-patterns research is blunt about why this matters for Sentryfi's actual customers: "for hardware shops (bulk purchase, unit sale) and importers (buying by container/pallet, selling individually), UoM conversion is not optional polish but a core requirement" (research notes, standards_and_patterns.md, Design patterns: units of measure conversion). Batch/expiry tracking shows the same universality — Zoho, Odoo, NetSuite, Business Central, SAP Business One, Cin7, Unleashed, and TallyPrime all ship it, several with a first-expiring-first-out picking rule ([Zoho Batch Tracking](https://www.zoho.com/us/inventory/help/advanced-inventory-tracking/batch-tracking.html), [Odoo expiration dates, 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/product_management/product_tracking/expiration_dates.html), [Unleashed batch tracking](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/product/inventory-management-software/batch-tracking/), [TallyHelp batch-wise inventory](https://help.tallysolutions.com/manage-inventory-batch-wise-tally/)). Neither gap is a blind spot for Sentryfi — both are already named in its build plan ("a second unit (box and piece), batch and expiry where switched on") — so the research here mainly confirms the plan is pointed at the right two features, in roughly the right order, for a Maldives importer/hardware-shop/factory customer base.

Bins are the one piece of physical hierarchy Sentryfi deliberately leaves out, and the research suggests that is the correct call for now rather than a gap. SAP Business One, NetSuite, Business Central, and Odoo all support a three-level warehouse → zone → bin hierarchy ([SAP B1 Bin Locations](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_BUSINESS_ONE/68a2e87fb29941b5bf959a184d9c6727/ad4f233a7b864c7cbe2b57ad09246adb.html), [NetSuite Bin Management by Location](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4713998969.html), [BC Locations, Zones and Bins](https://usedynamics.com/business-central/inventory/locations-zones-and-bins-structure/)), but even Zoho — the accounting-tier app with the richest tracking model — gates bins to paid plans with hard caps (2,000 bins per warehouse on Premium, 5,000 on Enterprise) ([Zoho Bin Locations](https://www.zoho.com/us/inventory/bin-locations/)), while Xero and QuickBooks Online have no bin concept at all in their base products. Bins are a feature that shows up once a business runs large, densely-shelved warehouses; Sentryfi's named places with a kind (store, godown, outlet, site, factory, vehicle) and a person in charge already give a Maldives hardware shop or contractor the granularity it actually needs, and — notably — tying a site to a project is a capability none of the general inventory apps offer natively. NetSuite and Business Central only get equivalent job-level tracking through a separate Job Costing or Projects module bolted onto the inventory ledger ([NetSuite Job Costing](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_3748436379.html), [BC Record Consumption or Usage of Project Resources and Items](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/projects-how-record-job-usage)), and the accounting-first apps don't get there at all — the research explicitly flags that "issuing stock to projects/jobs is not a native job-costing feature" in Zoho, and that Xero and QuickBooks push it to manual workarounds too (research notes, accounting_first_apps.md, Movements takeaway). Sentryfi baking the project link into the place record itself, rather than into a separate module, is a genuine design advantage for its contractor customers.

## Moving weighted average, company-wide, is the textbook-correct choice — and the backdating fix matches what leaders had to build

IAS 2 and IFRS for SMEs Section 13 permit exactly two cost formulas for fungible inventory — FIFO or weighted average — and explicitly name the "moving average" variant, recalculated as each shipment is received, as a valid form of weighted average (research notes, standards_and_patterns.md, quoting [IAS 2 official text](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/publications/html-standards/english/2025/issued/ias2.html) paras 25 and 27). Sentryfi's moving weighted average is squarely inside that rule, and setting the method company-wide rather than per place mirrors Zoho's own design: "costing method is set at item creation and applies company-wide across all warehouses that stock it; the Inventory Valuation Report can be filtered by warehouse for reporting only, not for separate per-warehouse costing" ([Zoho Inventory Valuation Report](https://www.zoho.com/en-in/erp/help/analytic-reports/inventory-valuation-reports.html)). That is a meaningful validation, since a naive design would let each warehouse carry its own average and silently diverge from the books.

The harder problem — a bill dated before sales that were already costed — is one every perpetual, average-costing system in this research has had to solve, and Sentryfi's answer (one re-costing correction dated today, closed months never reopened) is close to the industry-converged fix rather than an improvisation. Microsoft's own documentation states plainly that "if you use moving average, there's no way to revalue the individual transactions" retroactively ([Microsoft Learn, Inventory costing FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/cost-management/inventory-costing-faq)), which is exactly why Business Central ships a dedicated "Adjust Cost – Item Entries" batch job that "reconciles cost once real numbers... arrive," leaving original entries untouched and posting a new correcting value entry instead ([BC Manually adjust the costs of items](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-adjust-item-costs)). NetSuite's equivalent, Inventory Cost Revaluation, explicitly warns that backdating "triggers a cost recalculation that runs for an extended period" and recommends avoiding it where possible ([NetSuite Revalue Standard Cost Inventory and Backdate](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/bridgehead_4337969765.html)). Zoho takes the strictest route of all — it blocks locking a period outright if a sales invoice depends on a later-dated bill, and disallows partial unlocking for inventory-tracked organisations ([Zoho Transaction Locking](https://www.zoho.com/us/books/help/accountant/transaction-lock.html)). Sentryfi's "one correction dated today, closed months never reopened" sits precisely between these: it does the forward-dated true-up leaders converged on, without QuickBooks Online's more permissive stance of letting FIFO layers silently ripple and only recommending before/after valuation reports to document the damage ([QBO fix negative inventory issues](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/inventory-management/fix-negative-inventory-issues-quickbooks-online/L95hlEOoc_US_en_US)).

Sentryfi's landed cost — freight allocated by container, CBM, or chargeable weight, plus agent's disbursements and customs, folded into goods' value — matches the sophistication of the mid-market ERPs rather than the accounting-tier apps. Odoo offers five landed-cost split methods (equal, by quantity, by current cost, by weight, by volume) and reserves the feature for products on FIFO or weighted-average valuation ([Odoo landed costs, 19.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/inventory_valuation/landed_costs.html)); NetSuite's Landed Cost Categories tie each cost type to a holding account and an allocation basis — freight typically by weight, insurance by value ([NetSuite Landed Cost Categories](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2417902.html)). By contrast, Xero has no landed-cost allocation at all and it remains an open feature request on Xero's own product-ideas forum ([Xero landed cost allocation request](https://productideas.xero.com/forums/967139-purchase-orders-bills-inventory/suggestions/48529637-inventory-landed-cost-allocation)), and no official QuickBooks source describes a native landed-cost feature in either QBO or Desktop Enterprise (research notes, accounting_first_apps.md, Costing Gaps). Sentryfi shipping CBM/weight/chargeable-weight allocation by default puts it ahead of the two accounting apps most Maldives businesses would otherwise default to, and matches the accounting requirement in IAS 2 para 11 that transport, handling, and non-recoverable duties be folded into the cost of purchase ([IAS 2 official text](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/publications/html-standards/english/2025/issued/ias2.html)).

On negative stock, the three accounting apps split cleanly — Xero blocks it outright, Zoho makes it a configurable toggle (org-wide or per-location), and QuickBooks Online allows it and estimates cost until a real purchase lands (research notes, accounting_first_apps.md, Costing Inferences) — while SAP Business One offers a genuinely three-way scoped choice: block by company (total quantity only), by warehouse, or defer to the item's own setting ([SAP B1 negative inventory](https://www.sap-business-one-tips.com/en/negative-inventory-in-sap-business-one/)). Sentryfi's rule — nothing may go below zero overall, but a place can still show below zero and gets flagged for it in "what looks wrong" — is functionally the same scoped compromise SAP B1 offers under "block by company," and it is a defensible one given that Sentryfi's costing is company-wide rather than per-place: a place going briefly negative because a transfer hasn't posted yet is a location-tracking event, not a valuation error, so flagging rather than blocking is the right severity. One accounting control this research surfaces that Sentryfi's design does not yet mention is the mandatory, capped write-down/reversal to net realisable value that IAS 2 requires whenever a book cost exceeds what stock can actually be sold for — "inventories shall be measured at the lower of cost and net realisable value," with any reversal capped at the original write-down amount ([IAS 2 official text](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/publications/html-standards/english/2025/issued/ias2.html), paras 9, 33–34). That is a real compliance gap worth closing, discussed further below.

## Two-step transfers and job issuance already match what leaders had to converge on

The standards research describes the industry-converged design for inter-location transfers in language that reads almost as a specification for what Sentryfi already built: "the app should model transfers as two independent moves (ship-out move decrementing source, receive-in move incrementing destination) linked by a transfer-order id, with an explicit in-transit quantity bucket in between — and the receive step must allow receiving less than shipped (partial receipt), automatically leaving the shortfall as an open in-transit balance requiring investigation/write-off/re-ship" (research notes, standards_and_patterns.md, Operations: two-step transfers). NetSuite holds transit-order value in "Inventory in Transit" until receipt ([NetSuite Transfer Order](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N3692380.html)), Business Central uses a dedicated in-transit location flagged "Use as In-Transit Location" that "holds the cost of the item while it is being moved" and belongs to neither the shipping nor receiving location ([BC Transfer Orders and In-Transit](https://probitas.co.uk/2022/03/11/business-central-transfer-orders-transfer-routes-and-in-transit/)), and SAP Business One uses a two-document model — a non-posting Inventory Transfer Request followed by the posting Inventory Transfer ([SAP B1 Stock transfer document](https://sap-b1-blog.com/en/glossary/stock-transfer-document/)). Cin7's take on the hard case — a shortfall on receipt — is instructive by contrast: it splits the shortfall into a brand-new, cross-linked transfer order rather than editing the original ([Cin7 Track stock movements](https://help.omni.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9128580750607-Track-stock-movements)), while a live Acumatica community thread shows competitors resorting to an awkward workaround — receive the full amount, then reverse the difference with a one-step transfer back ([Acumatica correcting an in-transit transfer](https://community.acumatica.com/distribution-6/how-to-correct-an-in-transit-2-step-transfer-transfer-order-6856)). Sentryfi's rule — a shortfall needs a reason and is written off at average cost to "Stock counted short or over," while more than sent is simply refused — solves the same problem the Acumatica thread calls "an imperfect workaround" more directly than either Cin7's split-order or the reversal hack, without inventing a new transaction type to do it.

Sentryfi's decision to let stock issued to a job post at average cost to the item's cost account or "Materials used on jobs" closes a gap the research found across every accounting-tier app examined. The finding is stated three separate times in near-identical language: for Zoho, "issuing stock to projects/jobs is not a native job-costing feature... Zoho Inventory was not designed specifically for contractors" ([ZBrains job costing analysis](https://zbrains.net/zoho-erp/zoho-job-costing/)); for QuickBooks, "allocating existing inventory item cost directly to a project is currently unavailable... the workaround is a manual transfer/inventory adjustment" ([QBO job costing for inventory items](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/do-more-with-quickbooks/job-costing-for-inventory-items/00/1495058)); and the cross-vendor conclusion drawn from all three: "none treats 'consume inventory against a job' as a first-class transaction; this is consistently pushed to manual adjustments or third-party job-costing modules" (research notes, accounting_first_apps.md, Movements Inferences). Only the mid-market ERPs close this gap, and only via a separate module — NetSuite's Job Costing needs Project Management plus time tracking enabled, and Business Central's project-usage flow routes an Item Ledger Entry into a Project Ledger Entry of type "Usage" through its own Projects module ([NetSuite Job Costing](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_3748436379.html), [BC Record Consumption or Usage](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/projects-how-record-job-usage)). Construction-specific tools go further still and make the cost code and project the primary axis of every transaction, not an add-on ([Procore cost codes](https://www.procore.com/en-ca/library/construction-cost-codes), [Ply construction inventory features](https://www.getply.com/blog/what-s-essential-in-construction-inventory-management-software/)). Sentryfi sits between these two worlds — issuance to project/department is native to the core ledger, the way construction tools want it, without requiring a separate module the way NetSuite and Business Central do — which for a contractor-heavy Maldives customer base is the more useful place to sit.

Stock counts, credit-note returns, and the shrinkage account all land on solid, standard ground, with one piece of best practice not yet built in. Posting count differences to a dedicated shrinkage account rather than folding them into COGS matches the more careful pattern found in the research (a Sage-family mechanic posts variances to "the item's default stock nominal account and a designated stock Variance Account," research notes, inventory_specialists.md, Movements) and is arguably tighter than Zoho's own default, which posts stock-count adjustments straight to Cost of Goods Sold with the generic reason "stock taking results" ([Zoho Stock Counts](https://www.zoho.com/us/inventory/help/items/stock-counts.html)). Credit notes returning goods at the cost they left at is the standard treatment everywhere it was checked. What's missing is the count discipline the standards research treats as load-bearing rather than cosmetic: blind counting (hiding the system quantity from the counter to prevent confirmation bias) and count frequency tied to an ABC value classification, with A-items counted monthly and C-items annually ([RFgen Cycle Counting Best Practices](https://www.rfgen.com/blog/complete-guide-to-inventory-cycle-counting-best-practices/), [NetSuite ABC Analysis](https://www.netsuite.com/portal/resource/articles/inventory-management/abc-inventory-analysis.shtml)). Sentryfi's planned "counting sessions a place at a time with differences shown before posting" is the right vehicle to add both.

## Ordering is deliberately human-gated — the gaps are the ones the brief already names

Sentryfi's quote-to-invoice chain (quotes, sales orders, purchase orders, deliveries against purchase orders, billing in parts, an accepted quote drafting its own invoice, nothing posting or sending without a person) reflects the same human-approval philosophy the standards research treats as a core internal control rather than friction: segregation-of-duties sources are explicit that "no single person should both initiate, execute, and verify an inventory transaction," and that app-enforced approval gates are the practical substitute for a small business that cannot literally split three people across three roles ([SafetyCulture Segregation of Duties](https://safetyculture.com/topics/internal-control/segregation-of-duties)). The low-stock flag in "Needs You" — at or below reorder level — is the same basic tier Zoho, Tally, and classic Xero all offer without automatic purchase-order generation; Zoho itself only gets automatic reorder-to-PO through a separate marketplace add-on ([Zoho Automated Item Reorders](https://marketplace.zoho.com/app/inventory/automated-item-reorders-for-zoho-inventory)), and classic Xero has no native reorder points at all (research notes, accounting_first_apps.md, Ordering). Given that Sentryfi's stated philosophy is that nothing posts without a person, staying at the flag-not-auto-order tier is a deliberate and defensible choice, not an oversight — the min/max reorder-level model the standards research recommends as "the pragmatic default UI" for stable-demand SMBs, with EOQ-style optimisation layered on only for high-value items, is exactly the shape Sentryfi already has ([Lokad Min/Max Inventory Method](https://www.lokad.com/min-max-inventory-planning-definition/)).

Two gaps the project brief already names as open are real ones by the industry's own standard, and both have a cheap, well-precedented fix rather than requiring NetSuite-level machinery. The absence of a formal three-way match is a genuine control gap: "three-way matching is the control that compares a supplier invoice against the purchase order that approved it and the receiving report that confirms delivery, before the invoice is approved for payment... where they do not [agree within tolerance], the invoice is held as an exception" ([Beancount.io, Three-Way Matching for Small Businesses](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/01/three-way-match-accounts-payable-controls-duplicate-phantom-payments)). But the research is equally clear that the enterprise-grade version of this — NetSuite's six-stage requisition-to-3-way-match chain with threshold-based approval routing ([NetSuite Purchase Order Process](https://www.anchorgroup.tech/blog/netsuite-purchase-order-process)) — is overkill for a small business, and holds up Odoo's binary alternative as the better fit: "3-way matching... only works when Bill Control policy is set to 'Received quantities'... the control policy can be overridden per-product" ([Odoo Bill control policies, 18.0](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/purchase/manage_deals/control_bills.html)), a design the research explicitly calls "the clearest borrowable idea in this section... likely covers 90% of the real-world need without NetSuite-level approval-threshold workflows" (research notes, midmarket_erps.md, Ordering Inferences). The second named gap, backorder handling, is treated as a first-class object everywhere it was checked — Zoho raises a backorder PO directly from a sales order ([Zoho backorder definition](https://www.zoho.com/inventory/academy/order-fulfillment/what-is-backorder.html)), and Odoo's Kanban dashboard shows a live backorder count per operation type — but the research also found this specific control was under-sourced even for the big ERPs, meaning Sentryfi is not behind some precisely-documented industry bar here so much as behind the general expectation that "what's still owed" should be visible somewhere rather than reconstructed by hand. Pick/pack/ship, by contrast, is correctly out of scope: it is a warehouse-logistics feature that even Xero and QuickBooks Online only get through a separate, more expensive product tier (Xero Inventory Plus, QuickBooks Desktop Enterprise), and Sentryfi's target customers — contractors, single-outlet hardware shops, importers, small factories — are not running multi-picker warehouse floors.

## What to build next, in order

The highest-value work is already on Sentryfi's own roadmap, and this research is best read as confirmation, not redirection: batch and expiry tracking and a second unit of measure are universal features at every tier examined and are explicitly named as "core requirement, not optional polish" for hardware shops and importers by the standards research — build these first, exactly as planned. Counting sessions, also already planned, should ship with two specific best-practice details this research surfaces: default the count screen to blind (hide the system quantity until the count is submitted) and let count frequency vary by an automatically-computed ABC value class rather than one fixed schedule, since "high-value A items should be counted monthly, C items annually" is the consistent finding across sources ([RFgen](https://www.rfgen.com/blog/complete-guide-to-inventory-cycle-counting-best-practices/), [RFSmart](https://www.rfsmart.com/resources/inventory-cycle-counting-guide)).

Beyond the current plan, three additions would close real, evidenced gaps at low engineering cost. First, a lightweight three-way match modelled on Odoo's binary bill-control policy — a per-vendor or per-item toggle between "bill on order" and "bill on received quantity," with mismatches held in an exception queue rather than silently posted — would close the "no formal 3-way match rule yet" gap without importing NetSuite's multi-stage approval machinery. Second, a net-realisable-value write-down with the IAS 2-mandated capped reversal is a genuine compliance gap for slow-moving or damaged stock in a hardware-shop/importer business, and should be its own auditable move type, distinct from an ordinary shrinkage or count adjustment, exactly as the standards research recommends. Third, surfacing committed (reserved-to-open-sales-order) and on-order (open-purchase-order) quantities alongside on-hand in the owner's Stock view would close the gap that Cin7 and Katana treat as basic vocabulary and that Zoho customers have an active, unresolved complaint about when it's missing. A fourth, lower-urgency item is backorder visibility — what's still owed to a customer, and from a supplier — which every general inventory app treats as first-class but which this research could not pin to a single canonical design, so a simple "open quantity remaining" indicator on the sales order and purchase order screens would likely be sufficient without a dedicated backorder object. Barcode/QR scanning, item variants, drop-shipping, and consignment-stock flagging are all real, sourced features elsewhere in the category, but each is a lower priority than the four above given Sentryfi's photo-first item design and its contractor/hardware-shop/importer target segment.

## How leaders design the module — and what to copy

The single most reusable dashboard idea in the entire research set is Odoo's Inventory Overview: a Kanban board with one card per operation type (Receipts, Internal Transfers, Deliveries, Manufacturing), each card showing live waiting/late/backorder counts and doubling as a launch point to create a new operation of that type ([Odoo Inventory Overview](https://medium.com/cybrosys/an-overview-of-odoo-17-inventory-inventory-bde952599e4f)). It compresses "what needs my attention today" into one glance without a separate reporting layer — precisely the job Sentryfi's "Needs You" screen already does conceptually, and evolving it toward the same per-category, count-based tile treatment (backorders, short counts, overdue arrivals) would make it more scannable at a glance. Cin7 Core's dashboard adds one further idea worth copying cheaply: showing each KPI's percentage change against the same period last year or last month, turning a static number into a trend at no extra screen cost ([Cin7 Using the Dashboard](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034604706191-Using-the-Dashboard)).

On KPIs specifically, the standards research converges on four as the minimum viable set for an owner dashboard — inventory turnover (COGS ÷ average inventory), days of inventory on hand, a dead/slow-moving stock percentage, and a stock-out rate ([AltexSoft Inventory KPIs](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/inventory-kpis-turnover-ratio-return-rate-shrinkage/), [Omniful Inventory KPI Dashboards](https://www.omniful.ai/blog/inventory-kpi-dashboards-turnover-stockouts-reorder)) — and notes that none of the vendors researched pair this with a valuation-to-GL reconciliation tile, which is exactly the "agrees with the books" figure Sentryfi already leads with. Adding turnover ratio and days-of-inventory alongside the existing "no movement 90 days" and low-stock flags would round out that set cheaply, since the underlying move-ledger data already supports the calculation.

On roles, Business Central's pattern is the closest published analogue to what Sentryfi already does: each warehouse user is set up as a "Warehouse Employee" with a default location, and BC filters what they can act on to that location ([BC Set Up Warehouse Employees](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/warehouse-how-to-set-up-warehouse-employees)) — the same shape as Sentryfi's person-in-charge-per-place plus phone confirmation without visible prices, which additionally satisfies the segregation-of-duties principle that the person who moves the goods should not be the one who sees or sets their value. On mobile and offline, every specialist app in the category treats the phone as a separate, purpose-built surface rather than a shrunk desktop screen — Odoo's Barcode app, Unleashed's Warehouse Management app, and Katana's split Shop Floor Control / Warehouse apps all work offline and sync on reconnect ([Odoo Barcode app](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/barcode/operations/receipts_deliveries.html), [Unleashed Warehouse Management app](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/warehouse-management-app/)) — which is precisely the design choice Sentryfi already made for arrival confirmation. The one piece of that pattern Sentryfi doesn't yet have is barcode/QR scan-to-receive or scan-to-count, which would speed up high-SKU-count hardware shops but is reasonably deferred behind batch/expiry and UoM given the current photo-based, lower-SKU-count target segment.

## mybooksai.com: the domain is parked, and the real product's inventory claims are thin

The assignment's fifth named competitor, mybooksai.com, is not a live product. A direct fetch of the domain on 2026-09-26 returned an HTTP 307 redirect to `forsale.godaddy.com` — the domain is listed for sale, not hosting an app (research notes, tally_and_mybooksai.md, citing the direct fetch). The actual product operates under a different domain, mybooksai.app, built by Zetran Corporation, a US-registered developer based in Marlborough, Massachusetts, and distributed as "myBooksAi Billing & Accounting" on Google Play (package `app.mybooksai.acct`) and the Apple App Store ([Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.mybooksai.acct&hl=en), [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mybooksai-billing-accounting/id6748947230)). It carries a 4.6-star rating from only 19 reviews and over 1,000 downloads, and its feature set — GST-compliant invoicing and GSTR 1/2/3B/9 tax filing — strongly signals an India-first product with no Maldives-specific localisation found anywhere in the research.

What can be confirmed about its inventory handling is limited to marketing copy: "track stock levels, organize products, receive low-stock alerts, and monitor expiry dates" ([Google Play description](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.mybooksai.acct&hl=en)). What could not be verified, despite a dedicated search pass, is whether it supports multiple warehouses or locations at all, what costing method it applies (FIFO, average, or none stated), whether it has a purchase-order or sales-order workflow as distinct from direct invoicing, or how its advertised "AI Accounting Agent" actually touches stock — whether it reads a photographed bill and updates quantities automatically, or merely applies a rule-based low-stock threshold a user has already set. No independent third-party reviews (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot) could be found, and the app's own pricing page did not render actual figures during the research pass, leaving only the App Store's in-app-purchase tiers ($7.99–$129.99/month or year) as a confirmed price point. Given this, no responsible feature-by-feature comparison against Sentryfi is possible: what is verified about mybooksai.app's inventory handling is thinner than what Sentryfi already has live, but the gap could be larger or smaller than it appears, since most of the product's actual mechanics are simply undocumented in any source this research could reach.

## Conclusion

The clearest takeaway from setting Sentryfi against thirteen established products and the accounting standards underneath them is that its foundational choices were the hard part, and they were made correctly: an immutable move ledger, moving weighted average tied to a single Stock account, and a two-step transfer model with an honest in-transit state are exactly the design decisions that the standards and design-patterns research treats as non-negotiable for a perpetual system, and that several well-known products (Zoho's netting complaints, the Acumatica shortfall workaround, QuickBooks' permissive negative-stock stance) visibly got wrong or left unsolved. That means the remaining work is additive rather than structural — batch/expiry and a second unit of measure slot into the existing ledger without redesigning it, and a lightweight three-way match or an NRV write-down are new move types layered onto a data model built to hold exactly that kind of addition. The more interesting long-term implication is that Sentryfi's willingness to put job/project issuance and location-to-project linkage in the core ledger, rather than bolting it on as a separate module the way NetSuite and Business Central do, is a genuine structural advantage for its contractor-heavy customer base — one that no accounting-tier competitor currently offers at all, and one worth protecting rather than complicating as the batch, UoM, and counting-session features get built on top of it.
