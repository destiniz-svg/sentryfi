# Auditors need control, not just access

The one requirement that cuts across every standard and every serious audit tool researched here is that the auditor, not the client, must control the substance of the highest-risk procedures — which balances get confirmed, which entries get sampled, which adjustments get proposed — while the client's own software should only ever be asked to authorize, supply, or accept. Sentryfi's live audit workspace (1.42.0) already gets the hardest part of that principle right: a SHA-256 hash-chain seal check that no mainstream ERP except Odoo offers natively ([Odoo data inalterability docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/finance/accounting/reporting/data_inalterability.html)), and a sampling tool that keeps the drawn sample with its population count so it can be reproduced, a feature not one of the four leading small-business accounting apps (Xero, QuickBooks Online, Zoho Books, Sage) offers at all. But the research also surfaces five specific places where Sentryfi's still-planned features risk repeating mistakes the audit-tooling industry has already paid for: a client-portal confirmation design that must be careful not to let the client see or touch what it is confirming (ISA 505 and the new PCAOB AS 2310 are explicit that this breaks the standard); a sample that is kept but not reproducible unless it also stores its seed and selection method; a GL/journal export that will not satisfy real JE-testing tools (Validis, MindBridge, DataSnipper) unless it separates posting date from effective date and names the preparer; a proposed-adjustment feature with no formal accepted/rejected disposition or roll-up of what stayed uncorrected; and auditor access that, per the TODO, is meant to expire on its own — a feature no vendor surveyed here (Xero, QuickBooks, Zoho, Sage, NetSuite, SAP Business One, Business Central, Sage Intacct, TallyPrime) has been found to ship. None of these are hard to build relative to what Sentryfi has already shipped; they are mostly field additions and status disciplines layered onto features that already exist.

## Mainstream accounting apps hand the accountant a login; nobody hands the auditor a workspace

Xero, QuickBooks Online, Zoho Books, and Sage all solve "let an outside professional into the books" the same way: an invited role with elevated rights, attached permanently to the company file until someone removes it by hand. Xero's Adviser role can view, adjust, and — uniquely — set the lock dates that block everyone else, while a separate, more restrictive Read Only role exists but excludes inventory, multicurrency, and payroll entirely ([Xero Central: User roles and permissions](https://central.xero.com/0/article/User-roles-and-permissions-in-Xero-Business-edition-GL)). QuickBooks Online invites an accounting firm through Settings → Manage users → Accounting Firms, and once accepted, that firm gets "Accountant Tools" — batch reclassify, undo bulk reconciliations, batch write-off — that go well beyond read access ([Intuit: Invite accountant users](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-global/help-article/account-management/managing-accountant-users-quickbooks-online/L2AcdYvHw_ROW_en)). Zoho Books folds the accountant into the company's own sidebar as an "Accountant" module rather than a separate firm portal ([Zoho Books Help: Accountant](https://www.zoho.com/us/books/help/accountant/accountant.html)), and Sage 50 gates the same idea behind a per-user checkbox in Maintain → Users → Set Up Security ([Sage KB: Configure users access to Lock Date](https://gb-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=250331094252220)). What none of the four documents is a role built specifically for an *external, arm's-length auditor* who should read everything but touch nothing — the closest any of them come is Xero's narrower Read Only role, which is too narrow (no inventory or multicurrency) to actually support an audit. And critically, **no vendor across either research pass — the four small-business apps or the six ERPs (NetSuite, SAP Business One, Business Central, Odoo, Sage Intacct, TallyPrime) — was found to offer self-expiring, time-boxed external access**; access is binary, invited-or-removed, with offboarding left entirely to the business admin. This is confirmed independently in both the accounting-apps research and the ERP research, which makes Sentryfi's planned "access given for a period, ends by itself" a genuinely differentiated feature once built, not a me-too checkbox.

Among the ERPs, auditor-role maturity spans a real spectrum. NetSuite ships the most complete answer: system notes capture date, user, change type, and old/new values on every record and cannot be edited by any user, script, or application, and the Transaction Audit Trail report is available by default to CEO, CFO, and Support Administrator roles ([NetSuite: System Notes Overview](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/chapter_158644279544.html)). Sage Intacct instead documents a *pattern* for building an auditor user with a purpose-built read-only role, explicitly because "the company often doesn't know exactly what the auditor needs to validate your accounts" ([Sage Intacct: Set up an auditor as a user](https://www.intacct.com/ia/docs/en_US/help_action/Administration/Users/audit-users-in-Intacct.htm)) — a tacit admission that the role has to be assembled rather than switched on. TallyPrime's lineage is the most telling data point for Sentryfi's own ambitions: it historically sold a separate "Auditors' Edition" SKU carrying **Tally Audit**, a feature that lets a reviewer multi-select vouchers with the spacebar, mark them audited, and get an automatic alert if anything changes on a voucher *after* it was marked — paired with a "Verification of Vouchers" report showing total vouchers versus audited vouchers and the sampling method used ([TallyHelp: Verification of Vouchers](https://help.tallysolutions.com/article/Tally.ERP9/Auditor_Edition/India/Statutory_Audit/Verification_of_Vouchers_stat_audit.htm)). That is functionally the same shape as Sentryfi's live sample-and-tick feature, arrived at independently for the same statutory-audit use case, which is a strong external validation that Sentryfi's design instinct here — sample, open the document, tick as seen with a note — is the right one, not a novel idea needing to prove itself.

Tamper evidence is where the products diverge sharply, and Sentryfi's hash chain already sits at the strict end. NetSuite, SAP Business One, Business Central, Sage Intacct, and TallyPrime all rely on plain, access-controlled, non-editable change logs — real audit trails, but not cryptographically provable ones. Only Odoo ships a true hash chain in the mainstream ERP set: each posted journal entry's SHA-256 fingerprint incorporates the previous entry's hash, so "inserting, altering, or deleting an entry breaks the chain from that point forward," and once a journal is hash-enabled the setting cannot be turned off and no posted entry in it can be edited ([Odoo 18 docs: Data inalterability check report](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/finance/accounting/reporting/data_inalterability.html)). This maps almost exactly onto Sentryfi's own hash-chain journal — the research finds no other general-purpose ERP doing the same thing, which means Sentryfi's seal check is not merely on par with the leaders, it is ahead of every product except Odoo. Where regulation forces the question, two philosophies emerge: France's FEC/NF525 regime treats any retroactive change as "strictement interdite" — strictly forbidden — a prevention model matching Odoo's hard lock ([Clémentine: FEC guide](https://www.clementine.fr/blog/comptabilite-social-et-juridique/fichier-des-ecritures-comptables/)), while India's Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014, Rule 3(1) takes a detection model instead: software must record an audit trail of every transaction with an edit log and date, and that trail must never be capable of being disabled — but nothing in the rule requires blocking the edit itself ([Taxguru: Audit Trail Compliance under Rule 3(1) and Rule 11(g)](https://taxguru.in/company-law/audit-trail-compliance-rule-3-1-rule-11-practitioners-guide.html)). Sentryfi's combination — posted entries immutable outright, plus a hash chain that proves it — already clears the bar of both philosophies at once, which none of the five other ERPs can claim.

## Dedicated audit-collaboration tools solve PBC, sampling, and JE testing in ways worth copying wholesale

The four accounting apps have essentially nothing for the middle of an audit — sampling, structured client requests, or JE testing — which is exactly the gap a second, separate industry of audit-collaboration tools exists to fill, and their converged design is worth treating as a specification. A prepared-by-client (PBC) list is not a flat checklist; best-practice tools (Suralink, AuditDashboard, CaseWare PBC Requests, Thomson Reuters Engagement Manager, CCH ProSystem fx, Canopy) all model it as a per-item request with an assignee, a due date, drag-and-drop upload, and a status that flips from outstanding to submitted automatically once the client responds, backed by a full audit trail of who did what and when ([Suralink](https://www.suralink.com/technology/request-list-management); [AuditDashboard](https://www.auditdashboard.com/pbc-requests)). Thomson Reuters frames the alternative bluntly: managing a PBC list over email creates "version-control challenges" that a centralized dashboard eliminates ([Thomson Reuters: Engagement Manager](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/en/insights/articles/engagement-manager-client-communications-on-cloud-audit-suite)). This is precisely the shape of Sentryfi's own comment threads — a per-entry or per-document conversation already exists in the product — so the planned "questions pinned to entries/documents, answered in-app" item is not a new subsystem, it is a thin, well-precedented layer over infrastructure Sentryfi already has, and every named vendor treats it as table stakes rather than a differentiator.

For data ingestion, the pattern is equally consistent: Validis connects to over 100 accounting systems including Xero, QuickBooks, Sage, and NetSuite, and pulls "general ledger, trial balance, journal entry and subledger data... ready for audit testing" in minutes, falling back to a structured CSV/XLSX upload only where no direct connector exists, with an explicit warning that a system export must contain the required fields "without manual manipulation" ([Validis Audit & Assurance FAQ](https://www.validis.com/product/audit/)). The AICPA's own Audit Data Standards (ADS) define the canonical journal-entry schema these ingestion pipelines target, and its GL Primer states that a journal-entry endpoint must return at minimum `Journal_ID`, `Effective_Date`, `GL_Account_Number`, `Amount`, `Amount_Currency`, and `Amount_Credit_Debit_Indicator` ([AICPA-ADS AuditData-API GL Primer](https://github.com/AICPA-ADS/AuditData-API/blob/master/GL-Primer.md)). Layered on top of that, ISA 240 gives the actual *reason* JE-testing tools want specific fields: paragraphs 32–34 require the auditor to test journal entries made at period-end and any entries posted directly to the financial statements, screening for entries outside normal business hours, made by users who don't normally post journals, to unusual account combinations, in suspiciously round amounts, or missing a description ([ciferi: ISA 240 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-240-fraud-guide)). None of that screening is possible from a GL export that only carries one date field and no user — which is exactly the shape of export most accounting apps produce today, and exactly the gap Sentryfi's own audit-pack export needs to avoid falling into.

Sampling methodology tells a similar story of an industry standard Sentryfi's live feature already approximates, with one missing ingredient. CaseWare IDEA and Diligent's ACL Analytics/TeamMate Analytics implement the full statistical toolkit — Monetary Unit Sampling (MUS/PPS), simple random, systematic, and stratified sampling — while a newer generation of tools (MindBridge, DataSnipper) increasingly screens 100% of the population with risk scoring instead of drawing a sample at all, MindBridge running "32 tests simultaneously on up to 500 million records" ([MindBridge General Ledger Analytics](https://www.mindbridge.ai/general-ledger-analytics/)). ISA 530 requires that a kept sample be traceable: the population definition, the sampling unit, the objective, the method used to size it, the selection method, and the items actually selected all have to be documented so the sample "can be traced back and re-evaluated" ([ciferi ISA 530 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-530-audit-sampling-guide)). Sentryfi's live sample feature already keeps the population count and "what each said when drawn" — the harder, more common regulatory-inspection failure the research surfaces repeatedly (flagged by the Netherlands' AFM, Germany's WPK, the UK's FRC, and France's H3C alike) is *insufficient documentation of the rationale for sample design* and reuse of a prior period's parameters without review ([ciferi ISA 530 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-530-audit-sampling-guide)). The one concrete, mechanical gap between what Sentryfi has and what ISA 530 asks for is reproducibility: nothing in the live feature is described as persisting the random seed or the exact selection method (random `random()` draw vs. threshold), so the same sample cannot be regenerated later and checked — a small, cheap addition given the feature already exists.

## Confirmations, count observation, and adjustments are the three procedures the standards guard most tightly — and where a well-intentioned design can quietly fail

ISA 505 and the PCAOB's modernized AS 2310 (effective for fiscal years ending on or after 15 June 2025) both say the same thing in stronger language than almost any other audit procedure researched: the auditor must "maintain control over external confirmation requests," personally select what gets confirmed, personally send the request, and personally receive the response, and if a response is somehow returned to anyone other than the auditor, it must be treated as a non-response requiring alternative procedures ([PCAOB AS 2310](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2310)). Both Confirmation.com and Circit, the two dominant electronic-confirmation platforms, are engineered around a single narrow role for the client: granting consent. On Confirmation.com, the client authorizes the confirming bank to disclose data — nothing more — after which the bank logs into its own queue and sends the completed confirmation straight to the auditor, who is notified by email ([Confirmation.com Auditor User Guide](https://www.confirmation.com/media/236013/confirmationcom-auditor-user-guide_global_nov_2017.pdf)). Circit, a regulated Account Information Service Provider under PSD2, states it even more plainly: "once authorization is granted by the account holder, the verified transactions are delivered directly to the auditor from the back-end system of the bank, with no interference from the client... the client cannot access completed confirmations" ([ICAEW webcast on Circit](https://www.icaew.com/-/media/corporate/files/technical/audit-and-assurance/audit-and-assurance-faculty/webcasts/circit-verified-transactions-process.ashx)). This is the sharpest possible warning for Sentryfi's planned confirmation feature: routing the request and the response *through the customer/supplier portal link Sentryfi already has* is the right transport, but only if that portal link becomes a one-tap "I authorize" gate for the counterparty — and never lets the client see the draft request, edit what's being confirmed, or view the answer before the auditor does. A design where the client's own portal shows the confirmation content or its answer, even briefly, would fail ISA 505/AS 2310 outright, no matter how convenient it is to build on infrastructure that already exists.

ISA 501's stock-count requirements read almost like a checklist against Sentryfi's own blind-count feature (item 28) plus its planned "watch the year-end count." The standard requires attendance whenever inventory is material — "the matter of general inconvenience to the auditor is not sufficient to support a decision that attendance is impracticable" — evaluation of the client's count instructions beforehand, independent test counts performed in both directions (floor-to-sheet for completeness, sheet-to-floor for existence), and cut-off captured live, at the count, as the last goods-received and despatch document numbers ([ciferi: ISA 501 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/how-to-perform-stock-count-observations-isa-501)). The most common real-world failure the research surfaces is mundane but important: teams "request cut-off numbers from the client a week later at completion" instead of recording them on the day. The one structural point Sentryfi's plan needs to hold onto is that the auditor's own test counts must be a genuinely separate, timestamped record from the client's blind-count entries — not a view onto the same rows — because ISA 501 is testing whether the client's count is *right*, which is meaningless if the auditor's number and the client's number live in the same field.

ISA 450's misstatement-accumulation rules expose the clearest gap between what dedicated audit software already builds and what any mainstream accounting app — or Sentryfi's current plan — offers. The standard requires the auditor to accumulate every identified misstatement above a "clearly trivial" threshold (a materially smaller bar than materiality itself — "if there is any uncertainty whether an item is clearly trivial, it is not clearly trivial"), classify each as factual, judgmental, or projected, communicate all of them to management, and — if management declines to correct some — produce a summary of the *uncorrected* misstatements and re-assess materiality against it before forming an opinion ([ciferi: ISA 450 guide](https://ciferi.com/blog/isa-450-evaluation-misstatements-guide)). CaseWare Working Papers already models this as a distinct object type: its Adjusting Journal Entries module marks each entry "Financial" (which alone posts to the client's real books) or a separate proposed/unrecorded type that only feeds a dedicated "Summary of Misstatements" worksheet without ever touching the live trial balance ([CaseWare: Adjusting Journal Entries Interface](https://documentation.caseware.com/2018/WorkingPapers/en/Content/Accounting_and_Assurance/Adjusting_Journal_Entries/r_Adjusting_Journal_Entries_Interface.htm)). By contrast, QuickBooks Online Accountant's "Is Adjusting Journal Entry?" checkbox, Xero's manual-journal-plus-audit-trail, and Sage Intacct's before/after-value audit log are all single-mechanism: the accountant posts a real entry directly to the live ledger, tagged or logged, with no staged proposed state and no accepted/rejected disposition at all ([QuickBooks: Make adjusting journal entries](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/accountant-reports/make-adjusting-journal-entries-quickbooks-online/L1IvjNses_US_en_US)). Sentryfi's planned design — the auditor drafts, the accountant/owner accepts (which posts) or rejects — is already closer to CaseWare's model than to any mainstream accounting app's, which is the right instinct; what it is currently missing is the third and fourth CaseWare-style outcomes: a "passed" status for items management chose not to book because they're immaterial (which ISA 450 still requires to be *retained* in the accumulated-misstatements record, not deleted), and the automatic roll-up of everything left uncorrected into a single summary an accountant can hand to the auditor for the representation letter.

## The Maldives sets its own audit threshold, and mybooksai is not a meaningful competitor here

The Maldives Companies Act 2023 (Law 07/2023, in force 1 January 2024) removed the blanket audit requirement every private limited company used to carry under the 1996 Act; today, audited financial statements are required only once annual turnover reaches **MVR 10,000,000**, per MIRA's Income Tax Regulations, with smaller companies only obliged to keep accounting records ([WebSearch summary via Nasheed & Co](https://www.nasheeds.co/blog/companies-act-a-new-era-for-audited-financial-statements)). CA Maldives (the Institute of Chartered Accountants of the Maldives) has formally endorsed both IFRS/IAS/IFRS-for-SMEs and the full suite of ISAs for use in the Maldives, running a rolling adoption process that most recently absorbed the 2025 round of ISA amendments, and requires a practicing certificate to provide statutory audit services ([CA Maldives Auditing page](https://camaldives.org/auditing/)). MIRA requires audited companies to submit their financial statements — Statement of Profit or Loss, Statement of Financial Position, Statement of Cash Flows — alongside the profit/income tax return, prepared on the accrual basis under IFRS or an acceptable equivalent (companies under the MVR 10 million threshold may instead elect cash-basis accounting), and mandates that "large" businesses appoint a Category A auditor registered with MIRA ([MIRA legislation summary](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/submission-of-fs-and-appointment-b49)). This is a materially different environment from India's, where every company regardless of size faces Rule 3(1)'s mandatory, non-disableable edit-log requirement — the Maldives has no equivalent statutory audit-trail mandate for accounting software, which means Sentryfi's hash-chain seal check and immutable posted entries are a genuine competitive differentiator locally, not merely a defensive compliance feature matching a rule that already exists. On mybooksai specifically, the research found only marketing claims — an "AI Accounting Agent" whose reports "make it easy to share insights with... auditors and regulatory authorities" — with no documented auditor role, invitation flow, audit log, or Maldives-specific localisation found anywhere in public sources; it is an India-first GST invoicing app (GSTR 1/2/3B/9 filing) with no evidence of purpose-built audit support at all ([Zetran: Introducing myBooks AI](https://zetran.com/introducing-mybooks-ai-the-future-of-accounting-software/)).

## Verdict: what's right, what to change, and what to add — in build order

Sentryfi's core architectural bets are validated by this research, not merely defensible: the hash-chain seal check puts it ahead of every ERP surveyed except Odoo, its sample-with-population-count feature independently converges on the same shape TallyPrime's Auditors' Edition arrived at for the same statutory-audit use case, the capability-isolated "audit" role that writes only to its own tables is the correct pattern (mirroring how Confirmation.com and Circit isolate the client to a consent-only action), and the plan to build confirmations and Q&A on top of the existing portal link and comment threads reuses infrastructure the way every dedicated audit-collaboration vendor researched here treats as baseline. The five changes below are not redesigns — each is a field addition or a status discipline layered onto a feature that already exists or is already planned, ordered so the cheapest, most foundational fixes come first:

| Priority | Change | Why | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add posting-date **and** effective-date, preparer/user, source (manual vs. subledger vs. system), and description to the GL/journal export | ISA 240 red-flag screening (late postings, unusual users, round amounts, weekend entries) and AICPA ADS ingestion (Validis, MindBridge, DataSnipper) both require these as separate fields; a single-date export is unusable by real JE-testing tools | Extends the audit pack export (still planned) |
| 2 | Persist the sample's random seed and selection method (population source, filter, threshold vs. random, size) alongside the already-kept population count | ISA 530 requires a sample to be traceable and re-evaluated; without a seed, "the same one can be shown again" is a description, not a proof | Small addition to the live sampling feature (1.42.0) |
| 3 | Give proposed adjustments a real four-state status — **proposed / accepted / passed / rejected** — with "passed" (immaterial, left unrecorded) retained rather than deleted, plus an auto-generated summary of everything uncorrected | ISA 450 requires accumulating even trivial-adjacent items and summarizing uncorrected misstatements for the representation letter; no mainstream accounting app does this, but CaseWare's Financial/non-Financial split shows the pattern | Design detail for the still-planned "Proposed adjustments" item |
| 4 | Build confirmations so the client's portal link only ever shows an "I authorize" action — never the request content or the returned answer | ISA 505 and PCAOB AS 2310 require the auditor to select, send, and receive; Confirmation.com and Circit both prove this is buildable on a client-facing portal without breaking the standard, but only if the client role is architecturally blocked from reading confirmation requests/responses, not just discouraged from opening them | Design constraint for the still-planned "Confirmations" item |
| 5 | Make auditor access genuinely time-boxed (grant for a period, auto-revoke) | No vendor surveyed — not Xero, QuickBooks, Zoho, Sage, NetSuite, SAP B1, Business Central, Sage Intacct, or TallyPrime — was found to auto-expire external access; this is pure differentiation, not catch-up, and directly satisfies the TODO's "ends by itself" | Ships with sign-off (still planned) |

Beyond these five corrections, the remaining build-order for what's still planned should follow the sequence the TODO already lays out — audit pack, questions on entries, confirmations, watching the blind count, proposed adjustments, sign-off — because each later item depends on infrastructure the earlier ones establish (the audit pack needs the GL-field fix first; confirmations need the portal-authorization redesign before they're safe to ship at all; sign-off needs the auto-expiry mechanism to be meaningful rather than cosmetic). The one item worth pulling forward out of that sequence is the proposed-adjustment status model, since it is cheap relative to its ISA 450 payoff and, unlike confirmations, carries no compliance risk if shipped slightly ahead of schedule.

The through-line across every standard and every serious tool in this research is the same distinction Sentryfi's design already gestures at but has not yet made airtight everywhere: the client's software may *host* the audit's evidence, but it must never be positioned to *shape* it. Sentryfi is unusually well placed to get this right precisely because its journal is already tamper-evident and its auditor role already writes nowhere but its own tables — the remaining work is less about adding new audit machinery than about making sure the four still-planned features (confirmations, the audit pack, proposed adjustments, and time-boxed access) each preserve that same one-way boundary the seal check and the sampling feature already respect.
